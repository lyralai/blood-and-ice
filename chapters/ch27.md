# Chapter 27: Property Again

They kill Kala after the net has done its work.

Not the killing.

The order of it.

She was never the cargo. She was the scream tied to a stake. Once my wrists are bound, once the loop at my throat has taught my body the cost of fighting, once Marrow is netted and hooked and dragged where I can see him, Drevan gives a small nod.

No speech.

No anger.

Only permission.

A slaver drives iron behind Kala's shoulder into the heart.

Her body shudders.

The high stones go silent.

Even the men seem to feel some law has been crossed, though they crossed it by order and will collect pay for the crossing.

Kala's eye stays on me.

No fear for herself. No accusation. Only the keeping look she gave me in fever, when she folded her arm over a burning boy and taught his skin that not every hand meant ownership.

I pull against the ropes once.

The neck loop tightens.

Kala sees that too.

Her eye softens.

Even now.

Do not spend yourself on the hand.

The light leaves her while she is still looking at me.

I make no sound.

The scream has gone somewhere lower than sound.

For a breath I cannot find her in the body.

The shape is there. The gray-silver muzzle. The torn shoulder. The hand still curved toward the small dead she had turned right. But the keeping has gone out of it, and my mind refuses that emptiness the way a hand refuses fire after it has already burned.

Kala is not in the body.

Kala is in the arm that folded over me. In the sound that made stillness gentle. In Marrow's throat when he gives back the word she gave us. In the way my own hand moves, even bound, trying to reach the crooked small body beside me.

I wait for her chest to move. For her hand to close. For her smell to rise through blood and mud.

Men prefer noise. Noise tells them where the wound is. It gives them proof of power. They wait for me to break open, to thrash, to give Drevan the victory he arranged with nets and bait and an old mother's blood.

I give them nothing.

That frightens some of them.

Good.

Let them wonder where the scream went.

Drevan turns my face toward the light with hard fingers. He studies the split in my lip drawing itself closed, the cuts under rope already knitting, the eyes that should be fevered by now and are not. The island's sickness shines on his own skin. Sweat beads at his temple. His wound smells sweet with rot.

He knows I am not only an animal.

He does not know what I am.

That ignorance is why he crossed the water.

He says the brand-word again. Low. Certain.

Mine.

The word cannot reach where my name lives.

My name remains behind the broken teeth, whole and hidden. My mother's gift. The last unowned room.

The slavers begin their leaving.

They move fast now. Not because they fear the tribe. Because they fear the island. The raid has cost them more than they wanted. Two men bleed badly. One coughs red into his hand and hides it until Drevan sees. Another cannot stop scratching at the black swelling under his jaw. The green is still taking payment.

They drag the wounded Vorth away from the path and cut the nets only when they are sure hooks and spear points can hold them. They do not take the young. Not mercy. Calculation. Too much trouble. Too little price. The caged road has its own arithmetic, and I am learning to hate numbers.

Marrow is hauled beside me.

He fights until a hook kisses the skin under his jaw. Then he goes still.

Counting.

Not broken.

His yellow eye finds mine once. There is blood on his teeth. One ear hangs torn. His high shoulder is wrapped wrong by the net, and pain trembles through him in small waves he refuses to show the men.

I give him the still-sound with no voice.

Be held.

He sees it.

The slavers pull us down from the high stones.

Kala is in the arm that folded over me. In the sound that made stillness gentle. In Marrow's throat when he gives back her word. In my own bound hand trying to reach him.

The fire scattered. The dead shelf disturbed. Kala gray and vast among the morning stones, one hand still near the small body she turned right before the end. Stub crouched over the young ones, shaking. Torn-Ear on one knee, blood running down his neck. The scarred female watching me with a grief too hard for tears.

Smell follows me.

Burnt hide. Wet ash. Blood opened in old fur. The bitter sap from crushed thornwood. Kala's milk-warm scent under all of it, already leaving the air and becoming memory before I am ready to let memory hold her.

Men prefer noise. It tells them where the wound is. They wait for me to break open, to give Drevan the victory he arranged with nets, bait, and an old mother's blood.

Not wailing. Vorth mourning is low work, almost below hearing, a chest-note passed body to body until the shelf itself seems to hum. Torn-Ear begins it. Then the old female. Then Nomi, whose small voice breaks and comes back.

Kala had carried half of them at one time or another. Fed them. Struck them. Turned their faces from foolishness with one heavy hand. She was not soft. She was not gentle in the way human songs might want mothers gentle. She was weather and law and warm dark, and now the tribe has to breathe around the space where she stood.

The note reaches me.

It enters under the neck loop, under rope, under rage.

For one breath I am back against her side in rain while her hand covers my head and the island pounds around us. Kept.

Then a slaver jerks the pole, and the note tears away.

A little one crawls from beneath Stub's arm.

Too young to understand trap. Old enough to understand absence. It takes three steps toward Kala, hands open, making the small asking sound young ones make when food is near or sleep has gone wrong.

Stub catches it with one shaking arm and pulls it back against his chest.

The little one fights him.

Not hard. Not with fury. With need.

Not iron. Not Drevan's word. Not the neck loop.

A child wanting the dead to answer.

My bound hands move before thought. The pole between them jerks. A slaver curses and drives the butt of his spear into my ribs. Air leaves me. The neck loop tightens until black flecks swim across the world.

I make myself still.

Kala's last lesson again.

Do not spend yourself on the hand.

The little one keeps reaching until Stub turns its face against his chest and hides the sight.

Good, I think.

Hide it.

Then I hate the thought, because Kala never hid the world from me. She only stood between me and the part of it that wanted ownership.

There is no one standing now.

Voss-Marr stands above the path.

He did not come down.

No net holds him. No hook. No rope. He watches from the edge of the shelf where shadow cuts his face in two.

Blood darkens his muzzle.

Not his own, or not only his own. One of Drevan's men lies behind him in the broken brush, face turned the wrong way. Voss-Marr has killed today. He has paid something. That matters because easy cowardice is too clean a story, and none of us have earned clean stories.

His chest heaves.

His hands are open.

He could still come.

The path between us is not empty, but it exists. Two slavers with hooks. One net-man limping. Drevan's attention on me, on his cargo, on the fever eating his own blood. Voss-Marr is strong enough to make that path ugly. Maybe not enough to win. Enough to choose.

For one breath, our eyes meet.

I see the choice happen.

His hand flexes once on empty air. Then it closes.

If he comes, he may die and the tribe loses another strong body. If he stays, I am taken, the human trouble leaves, and the lip becomes his by absence.

All those names will have truth in them.

None will be the whole truth.

The lip is his now.

Not the way he wanted. Not won. Not given by challenge. Left behind by human hands and cowardice and a choice he will spend the rest of his life trying to rename.

I see it settle into his shoulders as I pass.

Shame.

Relief.

Hate.

Opportunity.

All mixed. All dangerous.

He will remember that he let them take me.

I will remember too.

Marrow remembers with his whole body.

When he sees Voss-Marr above the path, he stops fighting the hook for one breath. Just one. His yellow eye narrows. His teeth show, not in challenge but in promise.

Voss-Marr sees that too.

Good.

Let the island keep more than one count.

At the lower bend, Drevan pauses.

Not for breath, though he needs it. To make me look back.

I do.

The high stones stand above the green, smoke-stained and wounded. For one season of my life, they were the whole shape of safety. I know now that safety can be entered by men with enough patience, enough rope, and enough understanding of what a boy loves.

That lesson has a price.

Kala paid most of it.

At the shore, the boats wait in brown water.

The camp is already half-broken behind them. Cages loaded. Crates dragged through mud. Sick men helped by men nearly as sick. The black banner comes down last. When it falls, the eye-and-thorn folds in on itself like a closing lid.

They throw Marrow into a cage with a Mure and a Skell, as if he is refuse.

He lands badly. The Skell snaps. Marrow snaps back harder. Even bound, even hurting, he makes enough violence in the small space that the Skell retreats and the Mure lifts its head to look at him with dull surprise.

That almost makes me smile.

Almost.

They lash me separately because I am worth different rope.

Wrists. Ankles. Throat. A pole through the bindings so two men can hold distance between their skin and mine. Drevan has learned. He will not let me be near one throat, one hand, one careless knife.

He also will not let the others price me.

When one owner argues over the lost men and points at the cage where Marrow crouches, Drevan answers softly. I miss most of the words. I catch enough.

My catch.

My risk.

My sale.

The owner spits into the mud but steps back.

Drevan is sick, wounded, and hated by half the camp.

He is still captain.

That, too, is a cage with teeth.

Yara sits bound near the bow.

Our eyes meet once.

She sees Kala's blood on me. She sees the hand on my neck. She sees Marrow in the cage. She knows some part of this came through the road she opened, and the knowledge wounds her.

I cannot tell whether she betrayed me.

Both truths can live in the same skin. I have learned that lesson too well.

She mouths a word.

Not my name. I did not give her that.

Her own.

The true one she gave the dark.

She is telling me she is still there. Under the camp name. Under the rope. Under whatever Drevan made of her.

I give her the only answer I have.

I do not lower my eyes. I do not give Drevan my name. When the rope jerks, I keep my mouth shut.

The boat pushes off.

Mud sucks at the hull, then lets go.

The island begins to recede.

The mangrove mouth. The bone-spit. The seaward stack. The ridges above the fever fog. The high stones hidden by green but still there, holding Kala's body and the dead faced toward morning.

Home becomes ridge.

Then haze.

Then a line my eyes cannot hold.

They have made me property again.

They have not made me theirs.
