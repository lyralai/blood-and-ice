# Chapter 30: The Hand Opens

The beast is not stupid. Its mind is shut to me, but its body has been taught a narrow lesson by pain and hunger, and narrow lessons can be used. It expects the world to answer one way because men have made its world small enough to teach only that answer.

Charge.

Hit.

Tear.

Again.

I move right.

Marrow moves left.

The beast's blind head follows the larger motion, which is mine because men have trained it to hunt the body they throw first. Its digging claws strike packed earth where my leg was. Sand sprays. The side-mouth opens and shuts on empty air.

Marrow is already at the chain.

He has seen what I saw: quick-release hook, rusted pin, a line meant to drag dead meat out when the crowd is done loving it. He leaps, misses, catches the chain with his curled hand, and bites the pin. The pin sticks. For one breath he hangs useless, then blood shines and the rust gives.

The beast turns.

Too soon.

I throw the broken tooth.

It hits the armored head and does nothing except give the beast a simpler hatred. It comes for me. I let it. Three steps. Four. Loose sand under my heel. Gate to my left. Chain behind the beast now, Marrow hanging from it with all his crooked weight.

The pin gives.

The chain drops.

Not on the beast.

In front of it.

Its digging claws strike iron where earth should be. One claw catches through a link. Momentum does the rest. The beast's front half stops. The back half keeps coming. It folds into its own weight and hits the ground hard enough to shake old blood loose from the packed floor.

The crowd roars because falling looks like victory to those too high above the ground to read it.

One breath.

I spend it.

I run up the beast's plated shoulder, using ridges for footholds, and drive both thumbs into the soft place behind the head shield where a breathing slit opens and closes. The beast screams. Its side-mouth snaps backward, impossible wide, teeth grazing my forearm.

Pain blooms.

The Red answers under it.

Heat. Teeth. The wish to drive both hands deeper until the scream stops and the crowd learns what it has bought.

No.

I spend the refusal too. My hands shake before I need them steady. My forearm stays open. The Red hears no and keeps listening.

I hook one arm over the head plate and shout to Marrow.

"Left!"

The word comes out rough, half human, half Vorth. Marrow understands the part that matters.

Marrow darts in, small under the beast's blind side, and kicks the caught claw free of the chain.

The beast surges up.

Exactly where I want it.

It lunges not at me now, but away from the pain in its breathing slit, toward the open gate it came from. I drop, roll under the side-mouth, and come up with the loose chain in both hands. Marrow takes the other end. Together we pull across the beast's rear legs as it drives for the gate.

Iron bites.

The beast trips again, half in, half out of the gate.

Above, men shout. Pit hands scramble with hooks, suddenly afraid of losing expensive blood into the passage. The crowd's roar changes flavor. Surprise. Delight. Anger. The sweet confusion of people who paid to watch a killing and have been given a problem instead.

Drevan grips the rail so hard his knuckles pale.

Yara is smiling.

Only a little.

Only before she can stop herself.

It is enough.

Marrow sees it and bares his teeth at her like a king receiving tribute.

I nearly laugh.

His chin lifts after, proud enough to be absurd with blood on his ear and old sand stuck to his cheek.

For one breath he is not fodder.

He is Marrow on the high stones after stealing fruit from a sleeping young male, daring the world to notice and punish him quickly enough.

The breath hurts because it is bright.

Then the pit hands drive hooks down and drag the beast clear of the gate before it can tear through. One hook catches my shoulder. I slash the rope with the broken tooth, not deep enough to free myself from the pit, deep enough to make the man above swear and drop the line.

The beast is not dead.

Neither are we.

The crowd begins to chant.

Not my name. They do not have it.

Not Marrow's. They do not know his worth.

A pit-word. Prize. Wild boy. Blood-mark. I catch only pieces. It does not matter. The sound has changed. We were fodder and curiosity when the gate opened. Now the pit is reading us again.

Drevan reads fastest.

His fever-bright eyes move from my healing forearm to Marrow at my side to the chain on the ground.

He understands what the crowd has not.

I did not win by rage.

I made a smaller body matter. I made the ground answer. I made the pit's own tools turn against it.

That frightens him.

Not enough.

But some.

The wild boy has a price. The monster has a price. A creature that can make the pit look badly built has a different kind of price. I see him weigh it even through fever: sell quickly and lose the larger price, hold longer and maybe lose the hand.

For the first time since the high stones, Drevan looks less like the man setting traps and more like one standing inside a trap he built too well.

The same law as the mud. The same law as the Hesh.

The same boy, not broken enough.

Marrow presses his shoulder to my leg.

We stand in old blood while men scream above us, and for the first time since the boats came, the sound does not close over my head.

It lifts.

Not freedom.

Never mistake applause for freedom.

But room.

A handspan of it.

Enough to breathe.

Enough to count the next gate.

The next gate does not open.

If the crowd had been given the story it expected, another beast would already be coming. Fodder survives first blood, then fodder is fed again until the lesson corrects itself. The machine does not like surprises. It grinds them down quickly before anyone can wonder whether the teeth are weaker than they look.

But the pale-coated man lifts one hand.

The pit-master's mouth closes.

The hooks stay high.

The crowd hates waiting and loves knowing someone important has made it wait. The roar breaks into smaller sounds: wagers shouted back and forth, laughter, argument, a chant starting near the east rail and failing when the west rail does not take it up. Boys run with tablets. Coin men bend together. Scribes scratch.

Our breath becomes money while it is still inside us.

Marrow leans against my leg harder.

Not weakness, he would want me to know.

So I know it.

His torn ear is bleeding again. The swollen eye has narrowed to a yellow slit. His curled hand hangs oddly after the chain work, two fingers shaking in a rhythm he cannot stop. Still he watches the walls as if the pit has offended him personally and he is deciding which part to bite first.

"You are hurt," I whisper in Vorth.

He makes a soft rude click.

Yes, it says.

And?

The laugh comes before I can kill it.

Small. Ragged. Almost a cough.

The sound startles me more than the crowd. It should not exist here. Not after Kala. Not under these rails. Not with my throat bruised by the loop and old blood drying on my tongue.

Marrow turns his head slowly and looks up at me.

His face is a ruin of fur, blood, pride, and disgust.

Then he clicks again.

Better.

Not kind. Never that. Marrow would rather chew off his own good hand than be gentle where insult can do the work.

Better, he says with his broken mouth and bright cruel eye, because he has decided grief should not get the whole of me while he is standing there available to be annoyed by.

I almost laugh again.

Above us, Yara sees.

She is still by the rail, one rope around her wrists and another running to the guard with the split lip. The pale man has noticed her now. He speaks to Drevan and tilts his head in her direction. Drevan answers too quickly.

Yara's face goes still in the old camp way.

I know that stillness. Not peace. A door barred from the inside.

The pale man gestures.

The guard pulls her forward. Not hard enough to bruise. Public places have public manners. He brings her to the rail where she can see us and where the crowd can see that she is part of the display.

Translator.

Bait.

Proof that the wild boy understands enough words to suffer them.

The pale man says something to her. She swallows. Drevan says her camp name, warning folded inside permission.

Yara looks down at me.

For one breath I see the mud line: her true name, fingers signing no while her mouth spoke gift, Nerin in the tide, Kala's blood on stone.

She speaks.

"They ask what you are called."

The words are rough because she chooses the ones I know.

The pit quiets near us, then farther out as people notice language crossing the rail. Even the beast half-dragged into the gate passage stops screaming for a breath and pants wetly in the dark.

My name rises.

So fast.

So ready.

The old room inside me opens, warm and whole. My mother's voice lives there though I cannot remember her face clearly enough. The hold could not take it. The island could not force it. Kala never asked because keeping is not taking. Marrow never asked because he knew some food must be offered or not eaten at all.

The pit asks through Yara's mouth.

The crowd waits to own the sound.

I close the room.

"No," I say.

Yara hears more than refusal.

Her eyes soften and harden at once.

She translates something shorter, colder. Not his. Not yours. Maybe no name. Maybe refuses. I cannot tell. The crowd boos because denial is poor theater unless blood follows it.

The pale man does not boo.

He smiles.

A hidden name has value too.

Drevan grips the rail. Fever has made a tremor in his jaw now. He says something low to the pale man, and the pale man answers without looking at him. The scribe marks another line.

I see the board then, not fully, but enough.

Drevan wanted a sale.

The pale man wants a season.

A spectacle shaped by rumor and blood: the wild marked boy who will not give a name, the broken Vorth beside him, the captain who brought them in and may not live long enough to enjoy the silver.

Drevan has carried me to a market that knows how to keep meat fresh by keeping it dangerous.

His trap has opened under him.

The thought gives me no joy.

It gives me a map.

Pit hands come down by the iron ladder at last. Six of them. Hooks ready. The silver-ringed trainer comes with them, unarmed except for a short baton and a knife at her hip. That makes her the most dangerous.

She stops outside hooking distance.

Smart.

Her eyes flick to the chain at our feet, the broken pin, the beast blood on my arm, Marrow's shaking hand. Then she points at the wall near the left gate, where two rings are set into stone.

There.

Her voice is calm.

The hook men expect us to resist.

Marrow expects it too. His weight shifts forward. He would rather make them pay now, even if payment is one finger, one bitten wrist, one insult written in blood.

I touch his shoulder.

Not stay.

Wait.

He trembles under my hand, furious at the difference.

But he waits.

We walk to the rings.

The crowd jeers because obedience looks like defeat from above. Let them think so. The pit has just taught me its first mercy: if the people watching are high enough, they mistake nearly everything.

The trainer comes closer only when the hook men have chains through the rings. She crouches in front of Marrow, still beyond his teeth.

He lunges anyway.

Of course he does.

The chain stops him a finger's width from her boot. His jaws snap shut on air.

The trainer does not flinch.

She says a word I have not learned.

Marrow spits blood at her foot.

This time she laughs.

Not like the crowd. Not like Drevan. A short surprised sound, gone almost at once. She stands and looks at the pale man above.

Useful, her face says.

The pale man nods.

Drevan grips the rail with both hands. The hook has gone into him at last, only no one can see where.

The pit hands leave us chained to the wall while men drag the wounded beast away. It is still alive. I hear it scraping in the passage, hear the wet plea inside its breath. No one in the stands hears that part. They are buying sweet cakes now. Arguing over the next match. Wearing our survival as a story while we stand in the stink of its cost.

The beast costs someone.

I see it from the wall because chains make a person study every direction he cannot move. A pit hand with a torn sleeve kneels by the passage, one hand pressed to his own thigh where the beast's claw opened him. He is not old. He is not rich. Blood runs between his fingers and darkens the sand.

The silver-ringed trainer points two fingers at him.

Two other hands drag him back, not unkindly, but quickly, because the passage must be cleared before the crowd grows restless. A scribe marks a line. The trainer speaks to the pit-master. The pit-master looks toward the pale man. The pale man does not look at the wounded hand at all.

The injured man understands his place in the machine better than I do. He bites a strip of leather and reaches for the hook he dropped, trying to prove he can still work while his blood makes proof of the opposite.

No one chants for him.

No one buys sweet cakes in his name.

This place eats downward first. Beast. Captive. Pit hand. Trainer if she guesses wrong. Captain if fever takes him before contract. Only the pale man sits high enough to confuse distance with safety.

Marrow watches the wounded hand too. His nostrils flare. Then he makes a tiny sound that might be satisfaction and might be contempt.

I understand both.

Yara is pulled back from the rail.

Before she turns, she touches two fingers to her chest.

Her true name lives there.

Then she points at me, not asking this time. Telling.

Keep.

I hold her gaze.

I can keep a name.

I can keep a count.

I can keep a wound open long enough to learn who made it.

Beside me, Marrow lowers himself to a crouch and begins worrying at the chain with his teeth.

"Not yet," I whisper.

He clicks without stopping.

Yes, yes.

Not yet can still have edges.

Above us, the crowd turns to the next gate.

Below, in the small room the pit has accidentally given us, I count links. Guards. Keys. The trainer with silver rings. The pale man. Drevan's fever. Yara's rope. Marrow's breath. My own blood cooling where the Red wanted fire and did not get all it wanted.

Kala is dead.

The island is gone.

I am chained in a city that has learned to make joy from cages.

The hand opened once.

Hands can open again.

I count while the crowd forgets us: links, guards, keys, Marrow's hand, Yara's rope, Red.

The next opening.
