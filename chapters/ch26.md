# Chapter 26: He Goes Anyway

I move before the nearest slaver can shout.

Low across the stone. Not at Drevan. That is what he wants. The captain stands on the lip like bait carved into a man, and every road to him is netted twice.

I go left.

The first slaver turns too slowly. I throw a stone into his knee with all the force the green taught my shoulder. Bone gives. He drops. I take his spear before his mouth has shaped pain.

For two breaths, the trap is only men.

And men can bleed.

I drive the spear through a thigh and rip it free. Hook the butt into another man's ankle and send him down hard enough to split his chin on stone. A third grabs for my hair. I bite the meat below his thumb until blood floods my mouth and he screams.

The taste opens the door.

Red heat lifts in me.

Not anger. Anger has a face. This has teeth.

It rises from under the breastbone, under hunger, under fear, old and eager. The hand on the neck. The hold. The brand-sound. Kala bleeding in a net. Every command ever laid on me burns clean into one desire.

Hurt them.

The world becomes simple, and that is the sweetness.

No roads. No names. No mark with old meanings hidden under skin. No Yara with warning in one hand and betrayal in the other. No Voss-Marr choosing shadow. No tribe watching me walk away. No child reaching for Kala's dead body because it has not learned that wanting cannot call back the gone.

Only weight.

Grip.

Throat.

Heat under skin.

The Red takes all the tangled pain and makes one clean line from my hand to the next body. It feels like mercy because thought hurts more. It feels clean because it asks no question I cannot answer with teeth.

It frightens me because it makes monstrosity restful.

A slaver swings a hook at my head. I slip under it and drive my shoulder into his belly. His breath leaves in a wet grunt. The hook falls. I catch it before it hits stone and for one breath the iron feels made for my hand. Curved point. Good weight. A tool with no lie in it.

The Red loves it.

Hook meat. Pull. Open.

My arm begins to obey before the rest of me has chosen.

Then a small body flashes past my left.

Marrow goes at a slaver like a thrown knife.

Small. Crooked. Vicious. He slips under a hook, opens the man's calf with a shard of stone, then climbs him while he falls. His curled hand rakes at the face. His teeth find an ear. The man shrieks and thrashes, and for a moment the whole trap forgets the runt.

Drevan counted him.

The first net drops.

Not on me. On Marrow.

Weighted rope snaps around his high shoulder and drags him sideways. He twists, almost out. Of course he almost gets out. He has spent his whole life escaping hands larger than his body. But another man was waiting where an escape would go. That is the cruelty of a well-laid trap. It does not catch where you are. It catches where you are clever enough to be next.

Marrow screams then.

Not fear.

Fury.

Even caught, he tries to make the net regret him.

He bites a knot. Kicks a hand. Twists toward the smallest gap with a speed that would be funny if there were room in the world for laughter. One slaver yelps and jerks back missing skin from two fingers.

Good, I think wildly.

Bite him again.

I turn toward him, and the second net takes me from above.

Weighted edges wrap my shoulders. Tarred rope bites my mouth when I tear at it. I cut one strand with the stolen spear before the shaft is wrenched away. Bite another. The taste of tar and blood fills my mouth, and the hold rises inside me, black and whole.

The Red answers.

It does not speak in words.

It says more.

My wrist tears through the mesh. Skin opens. I feel none of it. A slaver leans close with a rope loop and I catch his forearm in both hands. For one breath I want to break him. Not to escape. Not to protect Kala. Not even to win.

To feel the warmth leave.

The Red wants appetite before freedom. Later Marrow smells that wanting on me and flinches before he can hide it.

I bite down before I choose to. Blood fills my mouth, hot and living. The slaver howls. Something in me howls back without sound.

More.

The word is not a word.

It is worse.

A hook catches the net and rips me backward before I can follow the hunger. A spear point opens my calf. Another net comes from the side. Men shout. Boots hammer. Someone calls for the neck loop. My wrist keeps pulling for him. The Red is late to notice air.

I hear Kala roar.

The sound shakes the high stones.

She rises under her net.

For one impossible moment, age and iron and blood mean nothing. Her great body heaves up from the clean shelf. Hooks tear wider through her shoulder and haunch. A slaver loses his grip and goes sprawling. Another backs away with the pure animal terror men feel when the world reminds them size can still matter.

"No," Drevan says.

I know that word now.

Not shouted. Ordered.

Three hooks drive into Kala at once.

Not the killing places.

The hurting ones.

They drag her down in pieces. Her hand claws at the stone, finds one of the small dead bodies knocked crooked, and turns it toward morning before the net tightens again.

The sight breaks something open in me.

The Red floods through.

I surge so hard the neck loop misses. The men holding the net stumble. One comes too near and I slam my forehead into his nose. Cartilage breaks. He falls back, blood everywhere, and the Red sings at the smell.

Marrow goes still.

Through the mesh around him, his yellow eye finds mine.

Not done, his body says.

Not like this.

That stillness reaches me where shouting cannot.

If I give the Red all of me, I may kill three men. Five. Maybe more before the ropes finish their work.

Then I will be empty when the hand closes.

I force one breath into the next.

The Red claws at my ribs.

I hold.

A rope loop drops over my throat.

This time it catches.

The more I fight, the more my own strength steals air. That is old craft. Hold craft. Let the captive strangle himself and call it restraint. My knees hit stone. Hands close on my wrists. Ankles. Hair. The back of my neck.

A palm presses me down.

The hold returns.

Not memory.

Return.

I scream into the rock.

The sound tears loose before I can bank it. Men flinch. Good. Let them have that much. Let them hear what they have put their hands on.

Then I bite the scream off.

Drevan steps into view at last.

He walks carefully. The drag in his leg is worse. Fever shines on his skin, and the scar from eye to jaw has gone swollen and wet at the edges. My island is killing him. Every breath costs him more than it did yesterday.

He is smiling anyway.

He has paid in men, blood, sickness, and time, and now his hand is on the catch.

Yara stands behind him, still bound. Her face is white under the grime. I cannot tell whether she is looking at me or at the blood on Kala's net.

Drevan crouches beside me.

He studies my torn wrist already pulling itself toward closure. My eyes clear of drug. The blood on my mouth that is not all mine. The mark against my chest.

His expression changes.

Not surprise.

Confirmation.

He did not come hoping for a monster from the green. He came expecting one, and I have just made him right.

The men nearest him see it too.

One makes a sign against evil. Another whispers a prayer with his lips barely moving. The young owner I saw scrape spilled water from mud stares at my wrist as if the healing cut has opened under his own skin. Even with blood on his mouth from Drevan's public lesson, even with shame still fresh enough to guide his eyes downward, he looks hungry now.

Afraid.

Hungry.

Men can be both.

Fear wants distance. Greed wants a closer look.

Drevan knows which desire will win if he gives it room. He shifts his body between me and them, subtle as a door closing. The catch is his. The story is his. The price will be his if fever does not take him first.

Yara sees that movement.

I see her see it.

For the first time, her fear changes shape. Until now she feared what Drevan would do with me. Now she understands what other men may do if Drevan falls before selling his prize. A wounded captain can still hold one road. A dead captain opens many.

Cruel order may be all that keeps worse hunger in line.

I hate the thought.

It remains true enough to hurt.

One of the owners says a word I know from the cage line.

Share.

Drevan looks at him.

No anger. No raised voice. Only the flat attention that made a free man kneel in mud.

The owner lowers his eyes.

There. Another chain. Not on my wrists. On theirs. The owner who said share has already started looking past Drevan's shoulder, measuring what might be taken if fever finishes what my teeth began.

If I kill the captain here, the camp will not become clean.

It will become many hands reaching at once.

If I break, he owns a body.

If I rage, he owns a story.

Men above pits and men behind clean tables will pay more for a story than a body. The wild boy. The blood-mark. The island creature that eats poison and smiles with red teeth. Drevan sees the price forming, and I see him count it.

The Red wants to answer by making the story true enough to drown him in.

I keep my mouth shut.

He says the brand-word.

Mine.

The old sound. The hand-sound. The hold-sound.

It falls off my face.

My name stays behind my teeth.

Kala's eye finds me across the stone. Still open. Still keeping.

Marrow is under his net, breathing hard, counting.

I hold all of it.

The Red. The name. The shame. The captain's word lying useless on the stone between us.

Drevan thinks the quiet means breaking.

Let him spend money on that mistake.
