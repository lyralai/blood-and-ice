# Chapter 29: The Pit Opens

The crossing ends in light and shouting.

They drag me from the hold half-blind. Day goes into my eyes like a spear. I smell the place before I see it: smoke, dung, iron, sweat, old blood. Not jungle. Not sea. A human stink, packed and permanent.

Land waits under my feet, but it is not ground yet.

Ground can be read.

Men crowd the docks. More men than I knew the world could hold in one place. Marked owners in leather. Collared carriers bent under cages. Boys running with water skins. Women selling skewers of meat beside gutters thick with flies. Guards with hook spears. Children perched on rails, pointing at the boats as if the dying green inside them is festival prize.

Humanity is not a shape.

The rules show in small violences.

A guard cuffing a water boy without looking down. A woman counting coins before handing over meat gone green at one edge. A collared carrier stepping around a free man's shadow because even shadows have rank here. A child reaching toward a caged Skell and laughing when its muzzle chain snaps short.

No one stops the child.

That teaches me more than the walls.

The slavers have brought pieces of my island to market. A Mure drying under wet cloth. Two Skell chained muzzle to tail. A bird with knife-feet hooded in black. A cage full of small scaled creatures that throw themselves against the bars until their noses bleed. Men walk past and judge them by price, danger, novelty, meat.

One points at me and laughs.

Drevan strikes him with the butt of his spear so quickly the laugh becomes teeth on stone.

Not mercy.

Investment.

The wound in Drevan's face has gone gray at the edges. Fever shines around his eyes. Each step costs him now, though he hides it from everyone but me. My island is still eating him. He left the green, but the green did not leave his blood.

That pleases me.

Not enough.

They march us through the market toward a roar that grows larger with every step.

At first I think it is sea.

Then I understand.

Voices.

Many voices, lifted together, hungry in the same direction.

The pits lie beyond a ring of walls.

Pits cut into earth, shored with stone, circled by timber stands and iron rails. Men crowd the tiers, roaring down at whatever bleeds below. Cages line the far side: Mure, Skell, furred brutes, scaled shapes sleeping with eyes open.

Behind the stands, scribes scratch marks onto tablets. Coin men weigh silver. Butchers wait near the drains. A healer checks a fighter's teeth the way a buyer checks a horse. Rich fools in red cloth sit above the main gate, faces painted against the stink.

Hunger has been taught to stand in rows, and every row tells me who can hurt us first.

At the bottom are pit hands with scarred arms and quick feet. They know when to lean away before the crowd knows anything moved. They carry hooks, sand buckets, chain keys, sour water, forked poles for throats and limbs.

Above them are the trainers. They watch teeth, limp, breath, the way a captive grips a blade or refuses one. One woman with silver rings looks at Marrow and frowns, not from pity, but because the pit has been handed a bad description. Her eyes move from his high shoulder to the cage latch he tested without seeming to move.

I mark her.

Above the trainers are coin men, scribes, buyers' servants with tablets and dyed cords. They know which house owns a fighter's debt and which only owns a share in the winnings. Soft hands. Sharp eyes. Men who never drag nets but know how many nets a boy's blood can buy.

Above them sit the patrons.

Painted faces. Clean cloth. Perfumed wrists. Little knives worn for beauty. Their laughter carries differently because no one may interrupt it. Best shade, farthest from blood. Power can make pain small enough for entertainment.

The pale-coated patron belongs there but stands below. He enjoys letting others see him descend.

He is not only buyer.

He is theater.

When he moves, scribes look up. When he speaks, trainers stop pretending not to listen. Drevan straightens because the man has the smell of a gate, not a wall. A gate can open onto silver, pardon, ships, clean surgeons, safer contracts. A gate can also close.

The pale man sees that Drevan knows it.

That pleases him.

The hold was ownership hidden below deck.

The pit is ownership made public.

They bring me to the largest pit.

The crowd changes when it sees me. Curiosity first. Then disappointment. I am smaller than rumor, maybe. Too boy-shaped. Too thin. Not enough claws. Not enough monster for the price Drevan's men have whispered through the market.

Drevan hears it.

His jaw tightens.

Good.

Let him need me impressive.

The same older patron meets him by the rail. Not a guard. Not a pit hand. Too clean for both. His fingers never touch Drevan's, but the captain straightens anyway.

Buyer, I think.

Or owner of buyers.

Or the mouth of one.

The man looks at me, at the mark, at Drevan's rotting face. He says a few words, calm as a knife laid flat.

Drevan's sickness vanishes from his posture.

Only from his posture.

He answers with numbers.

I know numbers now when I hear them. Losses. Price. Proof. Risk. Drevan's voice stays steady, but the old man's eyes move once to the fever shine at his throat.

Drevan is selling me before he has finished owning me.

The pale man has a scribe with him, a narrow woman with ink-stained fingers and no expression at all. She looks at me longer than the patron does. Not with hunger. With measurement.

Height.

Weight.

Wounds.

Mark.

Healing.

Her reed scratches across wax. I cannot read the signs, but I know the rhythm of accounting. A body made into lines. A life cut into columns small enough for another person to own without losing sleep.

Drevan points to his own face, then to the mark on my chest. He speaks of the island, I think. Of poison. Of fever. Of men lost. He opens one hand toward Marrow as if the small Vorth is proof of more value, not less.

The pale man glances at Marrow and nearly dismisses him.

The silver-ringed trainer, standing three paces back, clicks her tongue once.

Soft.

The pale man hears.

He looks again.

Marrow chooses that moment to bare his teeth at a pit hand and make a sound so foul in Vorth meaning that even without knowing the words the hand steps back.

The trainer smiles.

Not kindly.

Accurately.

The pale man says a number.

Drevan stiffens.

Too low.

Even I hear that.

He answers with another number and the word blood.

The crowd around us has begun to notice the bargain. Wagers travel faster than speech. A boy with red cords runs along the rail taking calls. Two women in yellow sleeves argue over odds while a priest-faced man with a jeweled cup points at me and makes a slicing motion across his own palm.

They are pricing not only whether I live.

They are pricing how I bleed.

The knowledge should freeze me.

Instead it clarifies.

In the camp, ownership hid behind need. Water. Fever. Order. Here, ownership wears bells.

It wants me to hear it coming.

He leans close before the gate.

"Mine," he says.

The brand-word has followed me across water, through dark, into sunlight, and still it cannot find purchase.

I look at him.

He mistakes that for understanding.

Then they cut the ropes at my ankles, loosen the neck loop just enough to keep me breathing, and shove me over the edge.

The fall is short.

I roll when I hit, shoulder and hip taking the blow. The floor is packed earth soaked black with old blood. It sticks to my palms. Sand has been thrown over the wettest places, but blood always teaches through cover.

The crowd roars.

The sound comes down the walls in waves and makes the old dark in me flinch. Men above. Hole below. No path out. Every face wanting something from the body in the pit.

I put one hand on the ground.

Ground can be read.

Packed earth. Slope toward the center where blood drains. Gate left. Gate right. Iron ladder not for fighters. Three chains near the far wall, each with a quick-release hook. Loose sand near the western edge, bad footing. A broken tooth from some previous beast by my knee.

The world returns in pieces I can use.

Something moves at the far wall.

I turn, ready for teeth.

I see a high shoulder.

A curled hand.

Marrow.

They have thrown him in as fodder. The runt, the broken Vorth, a small ugly prize to make the valuable animal perform. The crowd sees a crippled island brute. Drevan sees pressure. The pit-master sees entertainment.

I see the right hand they carried across the water without understanding its worth.

Marrow comes toward me at his crooked run and stops an arm's length away.

Blood mats the fur at his neck. One eye is swollen nearly shut. The torn ear has dried black at the edge. He should look smaller here under all these human throats.

He does not.

He gives the stilling sound.

The one I gave him.

The one Kala gave me.

Low, rough, nearly ruined by his throat, but shaped right.

Be held.

Nothing comes.

Then something does.

The warmth that rises in me is not the Red.

The Red is teeth. The Red is appetite. The Red wants throats and heat leaving bodies. This is deeper. Older. It begins under the breastbone where grief has been howling since Kala died and moves outward through blood, not hot exactly, not power exactly.

Recognition.

A thread pulled tight between two bodies that should have been broken and are not.

For one breath I feel Marrow through the space between us. Pain. Fury. Refusal. A living answer.

Then it fades when I reach for it.

Of course it does.

No answer gives itself whole.

But it was there.

Marrow saw it too. I know because his eye widens, and for one breath the pit, the crowd, the chains, the dead island behind us, all fall away.

Above, the crowd begins to jeer.

They wanted us to tear at each other.

We stand too still.

A pit-master calls from the rim. Some words are closed to me. I know enough.

Fight.

Blood.

Begin.

Drevan stands behind the rail, fever-bright and angry. Yara is near him, still bound, guarded by a man with a split lip. Her eyes find mine through the bars.

She sees Marrow at my side.

Something like hope crosses her face before fear covers it.

Then the pale-coated man turns toward her.

She lowers her eyes at once.

He says her camp name. Drevan answers before she can. The guard with the split lip tightens his grip on her rope, not cruelly, exactly. Practiced. A reminder.

So she is cargo too.

Or witness.

Or both.

The road she opened has closed around her feet as well.

Then a gate opens.

Chains rattle. Men shout. A smell rolls into the pit: musk, rot, old straw, rage fed badly and kept too long in iron.

The first beast comes low through the gate.

Not from my island. Too much shoulder. No eyes I can find at first, only armored plates over the head and a mouth that opens sideways. Its claws are made for digging, but the pit has starved it into using them for killing. Scars cross its hide where other crowds have taught it pain.

Good.

Now the floor is ground again.

Ground can be read.

Marrow moves to my left without being told.

I take the right.

The crowd wanted a monster and a cripple to tear each other apart. Instead two wrong-made creatures stand shoulder to shoulder in the old blood, reading the charge.

Above, men shout because they do not understand what they are seeing.

Let them shout.

I count the pit wall. The gates. The chains. The loose sand. The quick-release hook. Drevan's bad leg. Yara's bound hands. The beast's blind head and digging claws. Marrow's breath. The brand-words stored like stones inside me. Kala's eye, faced and kept. My name.

My name.

Still hidden.

Not forever.

The beast charges.

I smile with blood in my mouth.

The hand opened.

Not enough to free me.

Enough to put Marrow at my side.

For now, that is a beginning.

The beast is fast in the first three strides. In the fourth, training makes it stupid.
