# Chapter 16: The Long Hunt

No one hunts the Hesh with courage.

Courage is meat that has not yet understood the mouth.

I hunt with patience.

For three days I sharpen nothing, throw nothing, strike nothing. I watch. I lie in warm mud downwind while the Hesh sleeps with only its eyes above the rot pool. I learn where it basks, where it turns, where it drags its belly between water and bank. It is strongest in the soft edge and almost useless on stone.

Watching gives the monster edges.

Not mercy. Not kinship. Edges.

The Hesh hates white birds. It snaps at their reflections even when no meat waits beneath them. It will not cross fallen ironwood unless rain has darkened the bark. It sleeps with its head toward the channel and its tail in the reeds, always leaving one path for retreat, though nothing on Saqarro is large enough to make it retreat.

It marks its ground without lifting a leg like a tree-cat or dragging claws like a Skell. The whole low country marks for it. Crushed reed. Stripped mud. Trees polished smooth where its hide has rubbed away moss and beetle nests. Pools emptied of frogs. Paths where even flies are fewer. Wherever it rests, the green gives way and then grows back wrong, bent toward the shape of its passing.

Once I find a pile of dung high as my knee and full of little white bones.

Bird bones. Fish spines. Two Vorth finger bones, too large and dark to be human.

Marrow sees them when I do. He makes no sound. He only touches his own fingers, then mine, as if counting what a mouth can take from the hand.

Once, near dusk, it drags a dead Mure from the shallows.

The pale body hangs from its mouth, webbed hands limp, eyes open to the leaves. The Hesh does not eat at once. It carries the body to the edge of the black pool and lays it there as if making an offering to its own hunger. Then it waits.

More Mure rise in the channel.

Five. Seven. Maybe more under the skin of water.

They watch the dead one.

The Hesh watches them.

No sound passes between them.

After a long while the Mure sink.

Only then does the Hesh eat.

I lie in the mud with the insects tasting my eyelids and understand something I wish I did not.

Even monsters have politics.

The Hesh does not only kill. It reminds the drowned ones where the border lies.

The Mure do not come for the body. They come to be seen not coming. That is a kind of speech. The Hesh understands it. So do they. So do the Skell watching from the scree with their bellies pressed to stone.

Every strength is tied to a ground.

Change the ground, and strength can drown.

Marrow comes with me on the second day. I try to send him back. He bares his little teeth and follows anyway. He is light enough for the rot skin and quick enough to vanish when the Hesh lifts its eyes. More important, he sees what I see before I finish showing it.

A strip of firmer root mat between the pool and a deeper thicket. Beside it lies a black throat of mud, hidden under leaves and moss. A Vorth would vanish there. Even the Hesh avoids it by the width of a hand.

So we move the truth.

Where the ground is firm, we loosen the roots beneath and leave the skin whole. Where the mud throat waits, we lace vine and root across it and cover the weave until it looks safe enough for one breath.

One breath is all we need.

We are wrong the first time.

If the Vorth made songs the way men do, this is the part they would cut away. Songs like clean teeth and clean victories. The island keeps the rot in.

Our first snare lies too high. I think of Vorth ankles and Hesh forelegs. I forget the belly.

At dusk the Hesh comes for a half-rotten river-pig we drag near the root mat with hooks of bent thorn. It smells the bait, circles once, and slides forward. The vine loop tightens around the ridge behind its foreleg.

For one glorious breath the line holds.

Marrow's eyes blaze.

Then the belly rolls over the vine and the vine disappears into mud with a soft tearing sound. The Hesh does not even thrash. It keeps going, dragging half our labor under its weight as if the green itself has reached up and failed to hold it.

The river-pig vanishes into the mouth.

The Hesh chews twice.

The vines float up behind it in ruined strings.

Marrow kicks a root so hard he hurts his foot and has to bite his wrist to stay silent. I want to do the same. Not kick. Bite. Something in me cannot bear the calm of the great mouth. It should know we tried. It should turn. It should fear the clever little shadows working at the edge of its sleep.

It does not.

That night we rebuild lower.

The second failure costs blood.

A young Skell finds our bait first. Hunger has pulled it too far from stone. It darts from the reeds, snatches at the pig flesh, and sets one claw through a loop buried under leaves.

The line snaps tight.

The Skell screams.

Every bird in the low green lifts.

Marrow looks at me in horror. I move before thinking, crawling fast through the black water to cut the vine loose. The Skell sees only a body coming from shadow. It bites my hand hard enough to meet bone.

Pain flares white.

The Hesh lifts its eyes from the pool.

All sound in me stops.

I cut the vine. The Skell tears free and scrambles into the reeds, dragging one bad leg. Its blood marks the leaves in bright drops. The Hesh turns toward that color.

I press my bitten hand into the mud.

Not to hide the wound. To hide the scent.

The Red stirs under my ribs, warm and eager as a coal given air. Blood wants answer. Teeth want answer. The Skell bit me after I freed it. There is a clean cruel shape to that, and the Red loves clean cruel shapes.

Marrow reaches me and shoves his curled hand over my mouth.

The pressure is clumsy and damp and full of fear.

It brings me back.

The Hesh slides through the reeds toward the Skell's trail. We lie in the mud, my hand burning, Marrow half on top of me to hold me down though he is smaller than my terror.

The Skell escapes because it knows stone.

We escape because the Hesh chooses the easier trail and loses it at the rocks.

When the green goes still again, Marrow takes my bitten hand and turns it over. Blood wells black in the moonlight. He makes a small broken sound.

By morning the bite has closed.

The shame has not.

I did not mean to spend another body's pain as lesson. I did not mean to pull a Skell into our trap. Meaning does not matter to the bitten leg. The island does not ask whether a plan was noble before it names the cost.

We change the trap again.

No free bait. No high loop. No line that can catch a hungry small mouth before the large one comes. The ground must be the snare. The Hesh must choose the snare itself.

Better.

The work takes days. We move when the Hesh sleeps. We freeze when its eyes lift. A poisoned spine enters my forearm and sets the blood on fire. The arm swells, tight and shiny.

By night the swelling is gone.

Marrow watches the healing with a face too still for a child.

I know.

I want answers too.

He touches the place where the swelling was, then touches his own crooked hand.

No healing there.

The question is plain.

Why you?

I could turn away. That is what I do with questions that press too close to the mark and the name and the places in me that return whole when they should not.

Instead I put my hand over his curled one.

I have no answer.

He studies my face.

Then, with great seriousness, he licks the back of my healed forearm.

I jerk away.

He spits into the mud, offended by the taste of me, and gives the Vorth sound for spoiled fruit.

The laugh that comes out of me nearly gets us killed.

The Hesh lifts its eyes.

We freeze.

My chest shakes silently. Marrow's shoulders shake too. For a terrible breath the largest mouth on the island stares toward two fools hidden in reeds, and the only fact saving us is that laughter can be held in the body if terror holds it down hard enough.

When the Hesh lowers its head again, Marrow gives me a look of deep accusation.

As if I am the one who licked poison-healed skin.

I love him then.

The thought lands clean enough to steal my breath.

Not hunger. Not use. Not debt.

Love.

I have no safe place to put it, so I put it where I keep my name and go back to tying the trap.

The trap is only half the hunt. The Hesh must come fast enough to trust the ground it knows. For that, it needs bait.

Me.

Marrow understands before I finish the gestures. His curled hand closes on my wrist. For a moment he looks very young.

"High," I tell him, pointing to the leaning tree and the vine line. "Pull. Hold."

He hates it.

He goes.

At dawn the Hesh leaves its pool.

I step into the open.

Its eyes find me at once.

The great head swings. The mouth opens. For the first time, the Hesh hurries.

The world narrows to distances.

Sixteen strides to the false ground.

Ten to the tree where Marrow waits with the vine.

Four to the little rise that might keep my legs above the first rush if the Hesh strikes from the side.

None of those distances care that I am afraid.

I show it blood.

A shallow thumb cut. The scent opens in the damp air. The Hesh's mouth widens.

I run.

Not fast at first. Fast enough to promise panic, slow enough to keep the great head behind me. A hunted body wants to spend all its strength at once. I have been hunted too long to obey that want. I count mud patches. I step where root holds. I hear the Hesh behind me, not as footsteps, but as the collapse of everything soft.

The breath of it reaches my back.

My blood answers.

Joy lives under the fear. I hate it. The world stripped down to move or be meat. The Red hums, asking to turn and meet the mouth.

No.

Run.

Then Voss-Marr appears on the bank.

He has followed me.

Of course he has.

The cruel part of me wants to call it pride only. Pride must witness failure to feed itself. But that is not all of him. Voss-Marr has been bred to stand where danger comes. The tribe looked to his back before it looked to mine. If a wrong-blood child walks into the mouth of the island and returns with the answer, what is left for the son of the king except to follow and prove he was never afraid of the question?

He steps onto the old firm run, the ground I loosened beneath its green skin, and drops to the thigh in black mud.

The Hesh is coming.

I run to Voss-Marr instead of away.

He thrashes once. The mud takes him deeper. I slap his wrist and give Kala's stilling sound, harsh and broken.

Be still.

He looks at me with fury, terror, disbelief.

"Still," I rasp.

He obeys.

That saves him.

It also costs me the lead I built.

The Hesh is close enough now that its shadow falls over both of us. I smell the dead Mure between its teeth. I see my own blood bright on the mud where I cut my thumb. One eye fixes on Voss-Marr. The other fixes on me.

It knows two meals when one offered itself.

Voss-Marr's hand closes around my wrist. For one wild instant I think he means to pull me into the mud so he can climb over me. I see the thought cross his face too, not chosen, not yet, but alive. A drowning body will build stairs from love, hate, bone, whatever lies nearest.

Then his grip changes.

He pushes.

Not enough to free himself. Enough to give me the width of a breath.

I hate him less in that breath than I did before.

Then there is no room for hate.

I put his hand on the true root line and roll away as the Hesh lunges. Its whole weight comes down where I stood a heartbeat before.

The ground opens.

The Hesh drops.

Mud swallows its front half. The long body thrashes, teeth snapping on air. Marrow screams from the tree and throws his whole crooked weight onto the vine line, pulling the woven skin tight over the throat so the Hesh cannot find root to climb.

I get to the line and pull with him.

The Hesh fights like strength always fights when the ground betrays it. Harder. Wilder. Deeper into its own death.

Voss-Marr crawls onto true ground and does not run.

He watches the two smallest creatures in the green hold the apex under.

Mud covers him to the ribs. It has stripped the heir-shape from him and left a young male with terror in his teeth. He sees me see it.

That wound will last longer than the mud.

For one moment the three of us are joined by the line.

Marrow above, all crooked fury and clenched teeth.

Voss-Marr on the bank, mud to his waist, pride stripped from him in black ropes.

Me between them, pulling until my palms split.

The Hesh thrashes below us.

If the line breaks, we all die.

Voss-Marr sees that.

He gets his hands on the vine.

Not for me.

Not for Marrow.

For the tribe, maybe. For himself. For the pride that says he is not a body to be saved and set aside.

For the future that was supposed to be his, and is slipping by inches through my split palms.

He pulls.

The vine tightens until bark fibers cut under his nails.

Together, we hold.

When the thrashing slows, the Red leans toward it.

Not belly-hunger. Not glory. The same red wanting that opened when the swine bled and went quiet when the Hesh first passed me. It rises now as the great killer goes still.

Take the neck.

Make the fear yours.

I set my teeth around my hidden name until the wanting passes.

I will kill to live.

I will not live only to kill.

We cannot drag the whole body home, so I take the head.

It costs hours and most of my strength. The dead men's knife saws through hide and gristle. Marrow helps with both hands. Voss-Marr does not help, but he stays near enough that the jungle reads him as with us.

For him, that may be a kind of help.

At the last, when the head finally parts and drops heavy into the mud, he puts one hand under the lower jaw before I can. He lifts for one breath, no more, just long enough for me to get the vine under it.

Then he lets go as though he has touched fire.

I leave thanks buried. Every sound I could make would turn the moment sour.

We reach the high stones after dark.

The tribe is gathered, waiting for death.

I lay the Hesh's head beside the fire.

For a moment no one moves.

Then the fear breaks.

Torn-Ear bows first. Others follow. Kala pulls me against her and gives the full stilling sound, all the way to its end.

Be held.

Nothing comes.

But Gull-Marr watches from the lip with narrowed eyes.

Voss-Marr stands below him, wet to the waist with rot, and whatever passed between us in the mud is already hardening into something sharper.

I saved him.

That will not make him love me.

He wipes mud from his mouth and does not look at my hand. That is answer enough.
