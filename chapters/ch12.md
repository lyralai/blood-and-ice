# Chapter 12: Marks and Meaning

I unroll the dead men's skins by firelight.

For weeks I have carried them wrapped in fat-soaked hide, safe from rain, safe from rot, safe from my own fear of what they might not tell me. Now the Vorth sleep around the stones and the fire burns steady. Marrow is curled against Kala's side. The seer watches from beyond the light, as if she knew I would come to this before I did.

The skins crackle under my fingers.

Marks cover them in ordered rows.

I know they mean.

I cannot make them speak.

That loss is different from hunger. Hunger claws and demands. This sits behind my eyes and aches. There was a time when marks might have opened for me. I feel the shape of that skill the way the tongue feels the space of a missing tooth.

Once, maybe, I could read.

Now I bend over a dead man's message like an animal over bones.

But all marks are not the same.

Some are little and crowded, too many to hold. Others stand apart: lines of coast, bends of river, a black bar where the wreck lies. I know those because my feet know them. The dead man drew the island small. The channels I have crossed. The mud reach where his ship broke. The high stones where the Vorth keep fire.

Then I find the eye.

Ringed. Open. Pierced by the thorn.

The same mark hangs cold against my chest.

I set the disc beside the drawn mark. Metal and ink. Same shape. Same wound through the looking. The sight of them together makes memory stir, but not wake.

I press my thumb to the drawn eye.

Nothing comes.

Only certainty without language.

Mine.

The map runs inland beyond the wreck, deeper than I have gone with the Vorth. Up a black channel. Past two bends. To a shape drawn larger than the rest: stone steps, a crown of walls, and the eye marked above it again and again.

I have seen that place.

Once, before Kala found me, I followed a fat-tailed animal too far into the interior and came to a clearing the jungle had tried and failed to swallow. Stone rose there in tiers. Not natural stone. Made stone. Steps climbed into the canopy, slick with moss. Channels had been carved down them, long grooves running from the top to a basin below.

Old darkness stained the stone.

The eye-and-thorn was carved everywhere. Worn by rain. Split by roots. Still looking.

I did not climb to the top.

Fear refused before thought could dress itself as courage. Every hair rose. My teeth locked. I left that place quickly and told myself it was only another ruin.

Now I see the dead men were going there.

They had the map. They had the mark. They brought chains, iron, and hunger from across the water. They came toward the eye and died on the road to it, killed by the same green that cannot seem to kill me.

The seer moves into the firelight.

She does not touch the skins at first. She crouches over them, old head lowered, milk-pale eyes following the rows. She cannot read them. I see that. But she knows they are made to be read, and that is its own knowledge.

She brings ash.

Not by accident. She reaches into the dead edge of the fire and takes a pinch of gray between two fingers. Then she draws on the stone beside the skins.

One mark is a circle with a break at the top.

She points to the fire.

Kept.

One mark is a line crossed by three short cuts.

She points to the dead shelf.

Remembered.

One mark is a bent shape like a root diving under stone.

She points down toward the rot country.

Hidden.

Marrow has woken by now. He crawls closer, eyes wide, and immediately tries to draw his own mark with a whole fist of ash. It becomes a smear. He looks offended by ash as a substance and by his own hand as its servant.

The seer flicks his ear.

He flicks ash at her foot.

The tribe would be horrified if they saw. Maybe. Or maybe old seers are allowed one foolish child the way kings are allowed scars. Her mouth folds inward as if she is hiding laughter where age cannot steal it.

She takes Marrow's curled hand in hers and guides one finger through the ash.

This time the mark holds.

A small crooked line. A short hook. A dot beside it.

Marrow stills.

The seer points to him.

Not leavings.

Not my human sound.

Some Vorth mark for him, old enough that no mouth has used it aloud where I can hear. Marrow stares at the ash as though he has found a pool showing a face he had never been allowed to own.

Then the seer wipes the mark away.

Marrow makes a betrayed sound.

She presses his hand flat to his chest and gives the meaning-sound.

Kept in here.

Maybe.

Or maybe I only want that meaning because I need it.

Marks are dangerous that way. They let longing pretend to understand.

Then she touches the disc.

The long meaning-sound rises from her chest, the one the Vorth use for the dead, for old gifts, for anything carried forward from before.

Kept.

That is the closest word I have.

She touches the drawn eye. Then the metal one. Then my throat, where the pulse beats.

I go rigid.

The throat is a dangerous place. A hand there means ownership, silence, death.

Her finger is light.

She listens to my blood.

When she feels it, her face changes.

The old eyes sharpen. She looks from my pulse to the mark, from the mark to the map, from the map back to me. She sees a pattern I cannot reach: the green that kills men and leaves me standing, the eye carved in old stone, the disc that stirs my lost memory, the dead expedition, the blood under her finger.

I want to seize her and shake meaning from her mouth.

Instead I hold still.

She bows over my blood.

That is worse than an answer.

An answer can be refused. Reverence gets under the skin.

I am not holy. I am not chosen. I am a boy with scars, a knife from a dead ship, and a name hidden behind broken teeth. Yet the oldest Vorth bows to the mark on my chest and the blood in my throat as if both belong to a story older than her bones.

When she leaves, I sit awake long after the fire lowers.

The map lies open in front of me. The ruined steps wait in ink and memory. The eye looks from skin and metal both.

A made place in the jungle.

Dead men walking toward it.

Vorth reverence.

My blood.

No piece explains the whole, but they are pieces of the same whole. I feel that much.

I roll the skins carefully and bind them again.

A mark made to mean does not stop meaning because the reader is broken.

One day I will read it.

Or I will find someone who can.

The seer takes me to the ruin three mornings later.

Kala tries to stop us.

She plants herself across the path down from the high stones, one arm heavy before my chest. Her keeping sound is not soft now. It is command wrapped in grief. Stay. The old fear in her knows what old places take. The dead shelf behind her is proof of every life she has failed to keep.

I touch her wrist.

Not to move it. I could not.

To tell her I know the cost of going.

She huffs hot air over my hair and bares her teeth at the seer. The old one does not answer with anger. She only lifts the eye-and-thorn disc in her two long fingers and gives the meaning-sound that belongs to dead stories.

Kala looks at the mark.

Then at my face.

For a moment I think she will pull me back into her arms and hold me there until the world ends.

Instead she comes with us.

So does Marrow, because Marrow comes where I go unless something larger than weather ties him to stone. The twisted Skell follows from the scree at a distance, limping between roots. I pretend not to see it. Marrow pretends badly.

He keeps glancing back with his whole face, then snapping forward as if innocence can be performed by walking too stiffly. Once he trips over a root because of it. The Skell freezes. Marrow freezes. I look away before either of them can decide I have seen too much.

We go by a path the Vorth do not use.

The high stones fall behind. The green rises over us, wet and layered and full of small hungers. The seer moves slowly, but the jungle seems reluctant to touch her. Kala breaks branches when they lean too close. Marrow ranges ahead and returns with signs in his hands: old stone, dead water, a smell like iron left in rain.

By midday we reach the made place.

The ruin stands in a bowl of black trees.

It is larger than memory. Memory had made it a broken stair and a bad feeling. The truth is worse. Stone rises in five tiers, each one carved with channels that run from a flat top down into a basin at the foot. Roots split the steps. Vines hang from the upper walls like old hair. The jungle has covered the place, but not eaten it. That is the first wrongness.

Everything else on Saqarro rots.

This waits.

The eye-and-thorn is cut into every level. Some are small as fingerprints. Some are wider than my spread arms. All of them look outward, pierced through the center, the thorn entering from above or below or the side, never the same way twice.

Marrow will not step onto the first stair.

The twisted Skell makes a thin sound and backs under a root.

Kala's hand closes around my shoulder.

The seer climbs.

I follow.

At the top lies a stone basin shaped almost like a mouth and almost like a hand. The channels run from it down the face of the ruin. Old darkness stains them. Rain has washed them for years beyond counting and still the color remains under the moss.

Blood.

The word comes from some deep place in me.

The seer hears the shape of my breath. She gives three sounds, one after another.

Mouth.

Rot.

Taking.

The words are not exact in me. I know enough to feel the disagreement inside them. She is not explaining. She is naming three doors and refusing to open any one.

The meanings come to me as broken images. A mouth in the stone. A family mark turning sick in the blood. A hand taking red life from something too large to die quickly.

The seer lays her palm flat over the carved eye.

Then she lays the same palm over my heart.

The warmth behind my breastbone answers.

Not heat. Not the red hunger from the kill.

Only a pulse, low and unwelcome, as if something in me had heard its name spoken through stone.

Kala feels me tense. Her arm comes around me at once.

The warmth goes quiet.

Below, in the trees, something splashes.

We all turn.

At the edge of the black water below the ruin, a pale head rises from the brown channel. Smooth skin. Wide eyes. Fingers webbed around a root. It watches us without blinking.

Mure.

I know the name only later. In that moment I know only that the sight of it makes my blood step backward. It is not Vorth. Not Skell. Not beast. Too human in the wrong places. Too drowned in the rest.

The Mure opens its mouth.

No sound comes.

Then it slips under the water without a ripple.

The seer makes the meaning-sound over the empty channel.

Not grief.

Warning.

We do not leave fast enough.

The channel below the ruin begins to move in places where water should be still. A pale hand appears around one root, then another farther along the bank. Not one Mure. Three. Maybe four. They do not rise all the way. Only eyes. Fingers. A slick curve of shoulder. Patient as stones under water.

Marrow backs into my leg.

Kala lifts him with one hand and puts him behind her without looking. Her lips peel away from her teeth. The sound in her chest is not fear. It is hatred kept old and fed carefully.

The seer taps her staff against the top stone.

Once.

The water stills.

Twice.

The pale eyes blink.

Three times.

One Mure opens its mouth wide. Too wide. The inside is dark blue, lined with small teeth angled inward like fish bones. Still no sound comes. That silence is worse than a scream. It feels aimed at the blood behind my ears.

The warmth under my breastbone answers again.

This time the answer is sharper.

For one breath I see the ruin clean of moss. Fire on the top tier. Lines of red running down channels. Pale heads in the basin below, mouths open, drinking what they are given or what they have stolen. Vorth shapes on the lower stones, not worshiping. Bound.

Then Kala's hand clamps around the back of my neck and the vision breaks.

I am on my knees.

The seer is staring at me, not at the water.

Marrow makes my name-sound for him. Not my hidden name. His need for me. Rasped. Frightened.

I breathe.

The Mure vanish beneath the black.

On the stone where my hand has fallen, blood beads from my palm. I have cut myself on a chip of carved eye. The blood does not run down the channel. It gathers, round and bright, then creeps upward along the shallow groove toward the top basin.

Only a finger's width.

Enough.

Kala snarls and smears it away with her own hand. The seer makes a sharp sound I have never heard from her before.

Fear.

Not of the Mure.

Of me touching the stone with open blood.

We leave before the rain comes.

On the path back, Marrow walks close enough that his shoulder bumps my leg every few breaths. Kala does not release me until we can see the high stones. The twisted Skell follows with its belly low and its eyes too bright.

When the high stones come into view, the Skell stops.

I make no call. I have no sound for it. I only take the pale shell it gave me from the pouch at my waist and hold it up where the light can catch.

The Skell lowers its head.

Marrow makes a soft sound beside me, not mockery this time. Recognition.

Then the Skell slips back into the green.

That night I dream of the ruin.

In the dream the channels run red.

Not down from the basin.

Up toward it.
