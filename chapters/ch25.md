# Chapter 25: The Bait

I am halfway down the coast road when the high stones scream.

Not one voice.

Many.

Vorth voices breaking against metal. Young ones shrieking. A male's challenge cut short. Stone struck by iron. Nets snapping open with the wet slap of weighted rope.

For one breath I stand between roads.

Below: the camp, its fires, Yara's words, the mark's answer, the hand I thought I was choosing before it chose me.

Above: home, suddenly full of human sound.

Then I run uphill.

Marrow is behind me. Of course he is. I told him to stay and he obeyed only long enough to hate me for asking. His crooked gait cuts through the dark at my heel, faster than fear, faster than sense.

We leave the path before the first bend. The path will be watched. I take the rot gully, the old boar cut, the root ladder that tore my palms raw in my first season and now takes my weight like a remembered hand. Marrow follows every turn. Once he slips in black moss and catches himself with the curled hand, making no sound though I know it hurts.

The high stones scream again.

Then a low Vorth roar shudders through the trees.

Kala.

My speed breaks into something worse.

We reach the last shelf and crawl under leaning thornwood.

The home I left is gone.

The fire is scattered. Burning brands lie in three places, hissing where water has been thrown on them. The sleeping hides are kicked apart. The dead shelf is trampled. Gifts have been crushed under boots that do not know what a dead child means. Small bodies, once faced carefully toward morning, lie crooked now, their stones knocked aside.

Not the blood.

The disrespect.

Slavers stand on the high stones.

Eight above. More below the road. Two at the dead shelf. Three near the lip. Others hidden by smoke and broken brush. Nets hang open between them, stone-weighted and tarred dark. Hooked irons wait in their hands. One man holds a coil of rope threaded through a sliding neck loop. Another carries a jar of pitch with a burning rag stuffed in its mouth.

Fire if I run into green.

Nets if I break for stone.

Hooks for anything too strong to net cleanly.

Drevan did not come to kill the tribe.

He came to make the tribe into a snare.

Vorth bodies litter the shelf. Not many dead. He was careful there too. A young male pinned through the thigh by a hook. Torn-Ear bleeding from scalp and mouth, alive but senseless. Stub trapped under a net with two little ones clutched beneath him. The scarred female crouched over a child with her teeth bared while a slaver keeps a spear point just far enough from her throat to make the threat last.

Voss-Marr is nowhere on the open stone.

I mark the absence and hate that I mark it.

Then I see his sign.

Not him. The trace of him. A broken young slaver near the west run, throat opened by teeth too large for any knife. Blood dragged toward the old Voss-Marr path and then stopped. A Vorth handprint in ash beside it, broad and clean, pressed once into the stone.

He fought.

That truth does not absolve him.

It makes the rest harder.

He fought, and then he chose where to stop.

Kala is down.

She lies on the clean shelf among the dead, netted in stone-weighted mesh. One hook is sunk in her shoulder. Another in her haunch. Blood darkens the gray of her hide and runs between the morning stones. Her great hand claws weakly at the rock, not to free herself.

To reach the nearest small body and turn it right again.

My breath leaves.

They have not killed her.

If they wanted Kala dead, she would be dead. Iron in the throat. Spear through the eye. Fire in the fur. They have left her living because pain is useful while someone who loves the wounded body remains free.

The slavers are not looking at Kala.

They are looking into the green.

At me.

Drevan did not wait below for my answer. He used the waiting itself. Yara at the mud line. The empty offering. The false choice. While I stood between hunger and home, men climbed another road. Not the one I used. A harder one, maybe shown by a map from the dead expedition, maybe found by patient scouting, maybe bought with sickness and bodies. He followed my absence back to what I could not leave.

He also used the tribe against itself.

The old paths are not all mine. Hunters know them. Rivals know them. Voss-Marr's young strong ones know cuts through the upper green I have never trusted because heavy bodies made them. Men could have found one by chance. Men could have found one with patience.

Or a frightened Vorth could have retreated along it and taught them where home had a weak rib.

After battle breaks open, the mind runs to blame because blame is easier to hold than loss.

The slavers have not taken the shelf by courage. They have taken it by work. At first I see only bodies. Kala. Marrow. Fire. Net. Then smaller things force through terror: stakes where feet turn, pitch on root curtain, nets under ash, hooks not in hearts but joints. Shoulders. Hips. Thighs. Places where strength stays alive and becomes a handle.

The owner near the dead shelf wears a strip of blue cloth around his arm. So does the man by the lip. So does a third lower on the road. Signals, then. Not a mob. Crews. One for bait, one for fire, one for dragging bodies down to the boats if the catch changes.

Drevan has made a hunt from men who do not know the island.

He learned enough from watching me. From Yara's words. From dead expedition skins, perhaps. From every failed net and poisoned meat and night of waiting while fever ate his camp. He paid for his knowledge and has come to spend it all at once.

I hate the care in it.

Care should belong to hands that heal, not hands that bind.

I think of Voss-Marr then and hate myself for how quickly the thought comes.

He knows upper paths I never learned. His young strong ones move through the high green with the sure-footed pride of bodies born to those stones. He wants me gone. He wants the lip. He wants the tribe to remember the order that existed before a human climbed into it with tricks and wrong blood.

All true.

But wanting a door open is not the same as opening it.

Maybe he fought until the choice narrowed and chose the tribe left behind over the king walking down to men. Maybe I am making him simple because simple enemies hurt less than broken brothers.

The shelf gives me no answer.

Only blood.

Then, through smoke, I see a shape move above the west run.

Voss-Marr.

Not in the open. Not hiding either. He is half behind a leaning stone, one young strong one bleeding against his side, another crouched low with a hooked iron caught through the meat of his forearm. Voss-Marr has gathered three of his own there, maybe four, a knot of strength pulled out of the snare before it closed.

He sees the shelf.

He sees Kala.

He sees Stub under the net with the little ones.

He sees me in the green.

For a breath all the world narrows to the line between his eyes and mine.

He can charge down.

He can take his knot of strong bodies into the men by the dead shelf. He may break the hook line. He may free Stub. He may reach Kala before the second net tightens. He may die in three breaths and leave no one strong enough to pull the young from fire if the pitch jars break.

Or he can hold the west run.

Keep a path open. Keep his own alive. Wait for the humans to spend their trap on me, because I am the prize, because I brought this hunger down from the coast, because a king who has walked away may still be used as shield by those who stayed.

I watch him choose.

His jaw works once.

He pulls the wounded young male back into shadow.

I hate him then.

I also understand him.

That understanding is a poison no blood can burn clean.

The young male beside him tries to go anyway.

I see the movement. A half-rise. A hand closing around a stone. Blood running from the hook wound in his forearm while his eyes fix on Stub and the little ones under the net. He is young enough to believe courage is a straight path. Young enough to think dying toward a loved body must be better than living away from it.

Voss-Marr catches him by the scruff and drags him back.

Not gently.

The young one twists, snarling, and for one breath Voss-Marr bares his teeth in his face with all the old authority he wanted from the lip. Stay, the snarl says. Hold. Live ugly if living is the only road left.

The young one obeys.

I hate Voss-Marr more for saving him.

It would be easier if his choice were only fear. Easier if he ran, if he crouched low and shook while others bled. Instead he keeps three wounded bodies from throwing themselves into hooks that would make their bravery useful to men. He preserves strength while Kala lies in a net. He lets me become the answer to a trap he did not make but does not refuse.

There are betrayals made of cowardice.

Voss-Marr is still counting bodies while I am trying not to count Kala's blood.

The bait is not meat now.

Not water.

Not Yara's voice.

Not the mark's answer.

The bait is home.

Marrow shifts beside me. His breath comes fast and wet. He has seen the net around Kala. The disturbed dead. The little ones under Stub. He makes no sound. That frightens me more than a cry would.

Below, a slaver turns too quickly toward our hiding place.

I catch Marrow before he lunges. My hand closes over his mouth, and for a breath he fights me with everything in him. Teeth against my palm. Nails in my wrist. I hold until he understands that one small sound will close every net.

Then I let him go.

He is shaking.

So am I.

Drevan steps onto the lip.

He should not be there. That is my place, and the wrongness of his boots on that stone strikes harder than if he had put them on my throat. He carries no raised weapon. A marked leather coat. A knife. A short spear in one hand, point down. The scar I gave him has gone dark along the cheek, fever shining at the edges. He looks ill.

He looks satisfied.

Yara stands behind him between two owners.

Her hands are bound.

That cuts through my anger in a new direction. She did not come freely. Or she did and paid for it. Or she told him too much and learned too late how he would spend it. I cannot read enough from here, and that uncertainty is one more barb in the trap.

Her eyes search the green.

When they pass over my shadow, something in her face breaks.

Not surprise.

Apology.

One of the owners grips her arm hard enough to whiten the fingers. She does not look at him. She looks at Kala.

Not surprise.

That matters. Some part of her knew a net was coming. Some part did not know it would be laid through an old mother's shoulder and a child's turned stone. Drevan has used her road farther than she meant it to run.

Her bound hand opens once at her side, useless and empty.

Marrow sees it too. His body goes tight with hatred, but not only for her. For all of it. For every road that brought men here.

Drevan speaks in the human tongue. I know enough pieces now.

Marked one.

Come.

No more hurt.

He touches the eye-and-thorn on his chest and then points to Kala.

The meaning needs no translation.

Come, or I spend what you love.

Kala hears enough.

Her head lifts from the stone. Blood runs from her mouth. Through net and pain, through age and iron, her eye finds the shadow where I hide.

She sees me.

Of course she does.

The sound she makes is not come.

It is go.

Not loud. Not a command for the tribe. A breath shaped for me.

Leave me.

Take Marrow.

Live.

That is the whole last gift she has strength to offer.

The clever part of me wakes cold and bright.

I could obey.

The route is there. Not the path. The thorn crack behind us, then the upper ridge, then fever fog and broken stone where men with nets cannot move quickly. Take Marrow. Free the little ones if a gap opens, or leave them if it does not. Let the green kill the camp slowly. Let Drevan have a wounded old Vorth and a trampled shelf and nothing else.

The thought is clean. Useful. True.

Kala's eye stays on me.

Still telling me to go.

The clever part of me breaks under it.

Some hooks you see and take anyway.

I touch Marrow's shoulder.

He looks at me once.

No, his whole face says.

I step out of the green.

Every slaver on the stone turns toward me.

Drevan smiles like a man hearing a lock open from the inside.
