## Best-Fit Research Targets

### Donald Maass — Donald Maass Literary Agency

Why he fits: Longstanding fantasy/SF powerhouse; Reedsy lists fantasy, horror, literary fiction, science fiction, thriller, and commercial fiction. This book's epic fantasy structure plus craft ambition fits the broad DMLA lane.

Risk: Very competitive. Query only after the package is extremely clean.

### Laurie McLean — Fuse Literary

Why she fits: Reedsy lists fantasy, science fiction, romance, crime, LGBTQ, and YA; known for genre fiction. Worth checking if she is personally open and whether the darker literary-survival tone matches current taste.

Risk: The manuscript is darker and less commercial-romance than some Fuse lanes.

### Pete Knapp — Park, Fine & Brower Literary Management

Why he fits: Reedsy lists fantasy and science fiction. Strong agency; worth researching for voice-forward fantasy with clear series potential.

Risk: Must verify he is open and whether adult epic fantasy fits current list.

### Jessica Sinsheimer — Context Literary Agency

Why she fits: Reedsy lists fantasy, science fiction, thriller, commercial fiction, and YA. Potential fit for accessible speculative work with a strong hook.

Risk: Need current wishlist check; the book's brutality may or may not align.

### Stephanie Rostan — Levine Greenberg Rostan Literary Agency

Why she fits: Reedsy lists fantasy, literary fiction, historical fiction, thriller, and commercial fiction. Good research target if positioning the manuscript as literary epic fantasy rather than pure grimdark.

Risk: Query angle must emphasize voice and emotional stakes, not just monsters/chains/pit.

### Juliet Mushens — Mushens Entertainment

Why she fits: Reedsy lists fantasy, commercial fiction, historical fiction, literary fiction, and thriller. UK-facing but potentially good for big commercial fantasy with sharp voice.

Risk: Highly selective; check current submission status and genre appetite.

### Alex Land — Mad Woman Literary Agency

Why he fits: Manuscript Wish List agent list shows fantasy, folklore, gothic, horror, magical realism, science fiction, speculative, thriller, and related categories. The manuscript's monster-family, horror pressure, and speculative mythology could fit.

Risk: Need direct wishlist and submission-guideline verification before querying.

### Danielle Bukowski — Sterling Lord Literistic

Why she fits: Public listings describe interest in literary/upmarket fiction and select SFF, distinctive voices, strong writing, powerful sense of place, and a hook with pace. This manuscript's best argument is voice + place + survival hook.

Risk: The book must be pitched as literary/upmarket speculative fantasy, not conventional epic fantasy.

### Ciara Smith — PS Literary

Why she fits: MSWL material shows adult fantasy interest, especially fantasy with heart, risk, and layered stakes. Worth checking if the book's non-romance adult epic fantasy fits her current list.

Risk: If her current adult fantasy appetite is primarily romantasy, this may be a weaker fit.

### The Knight Agency — Agency-Level Research Target

Why it fits: The agency submissions page says its agents are looking across a wide spectrum of genres and are not accepting screenplays. Research individual agents there for adult fantasy/speculative appetite.

Risk: Do not query agency-level without selecting the right agent and current guidelines.

## Query Batch Recommendation

Start with **5-8 agents**, not 30.

Batch 1 should mix:

- 2 high-fit/high-competition fantasy agents,
- 2 voice-forward speculative/upmarket agents,
- 1-2 newer or actively building agents,
- 1 backup agency target after guideline verification.

Track:

- query date,
- exact sample sent,
- prologue vs Rook-first opening,
- personalization line,
- response,
- request/rejection notes.

If no requests after 8-12 carefully targeted queries, revise the query/opening before expanding the batch.
