# Query Letter

Dear [Agent],

BLOOD AND ICE: WILD BOY is a 73,700-word adult epic fantasy novel with visceral literary prose, survival intensity, and a trilogy engine. It follows a formerly enslaved boy whose only untouched possession is the true name he keeps hidden behind his teeth.

Rook wakes alone on Saqarro, a tropical death-jungle that should kill any human within days. Heat, rot, venom, hunger, and impossible predators take their turns. Rook survives anyway. His wounds close too quickly. Poison fails to keep him down. Even the island's creatures seem to recognize something wrong in his blood before he does.

Then the Vorth find him.

They are not human, not safe, and not gentle. But among their teeth, rank, grief, and brutal laws, Rook finds the first family he has ever known: Kala, the old mother who keeps him; Marrow, the crooked young outcast who becomes his brother; and a tribe that teaches him belonging can be earned without chains. Rook rises from stray to king of the wild things.

When human slavers cross the water, Rook sees two hungers at once: the danger of ownership and the aching possibility that his own kind might still know what he is. Captain Drevan Sool uses that wound. He studies the island, turns Rook's love into bait, kills Kala, captures Marrow, and drags Rook across the sea to a pit city where survival is spectacle and names have prices.

At the bottom of the pit, Rook has no crown, no tribe, and no freedom. He has Marrow beside him, a hidden name still unspent, and the old red force in his blood beginning to speak. If he gives himself to it, he may survive. If he refuses it, he may stay human. Either choice will cost him.

BLOOD AND ICE combines the bodily survival drive of *Red Rising*, the brutal political consequence of epic fantasy, and the intimate monster-family emotional core of a feral coming-of-age story. It stands alone as Book One while opening a larger trilogy about blood, ownership, and the cost of becoming a great man without becoming a monster.
