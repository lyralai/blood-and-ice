# One-Page Synopsis

Rook, a formerly enslaved boy, wakes on Saqarro after a wreck. He has no knife, no map, and no one to call his name. The island is a tropical death-jungle of heat, rot, venom, hidden water, and predators that kill humans as a matter of weather. Rook survives by inventory, hunger, and old lessons beaten into him by slavery. But his body heals too fast. Fever passes through him and leaves him cleaner than before. Blood in the pools scares the fish away. Something is wrong in him, and the island knows it.

Before Rook's story begins, a sailor named Coll witnesses a dead ship on a warm sea. Its crew have been emptied of blood and warmth by a pale, impossible thing from the hold. The cold threat vanishes into haze, leaving the first hint that the "ice" hunting this world is not weather but an old enemy of living blood.

On Saqarro, Rook learns the first laws: water before hope, hunger before pride, easy meat is bait. He makes fire, claims a den, kills, scavenges a dead expedition wreck, and discovers the eye-and-thorn mark that connects his own relic to older island ruins. Then he sees the Vorth, huge nonhuman people with teeth, rank, grief, language, dead-shelves, and law. At first they are only danger. Then one of them, Kala, chooses to keep him.

Among the Vorth, Rook learns that kinship is not softness. It is food, sleeping space, rank, shame, and public consequence. Kala becomes his mother in every way that matters. Marrow, a crooked young Vorth outcast, becomes his brother. Rook earns usefulness by going where larger bodies cannot: thin branches, rot mud, trap lines, narrow paths. He names Marrow, and the name begins to carry social weight. The tribe's seer recognizes Rook's eye-and-thorn mark as part of an old blood story, but Rook does not understand its full meaning.

Rook's standing rises after he helps the tribe survive the Hesh, an apex predator that has made fear into geography. His intelligence changes how the Vorth hunt and judge danger. That rise creates enemies, especially Voss-Marr, the king's heir, whose claim is threatened by a human boy who should have been prey. Rook wins challenges by reading ground, bodies, shame, and need. Eventually he becomes the Vorth's chosen leader, not because he is strongest, but because the weak and discarded gather to him first.

Peace lasts only long enough to hurt.

Human slavers land on Saqarro and build a camp where an old expedition died. Rook studies them from the mangroves. He recognizes ownership immediately: collars, ledgers, cages, hooks, boiled water, portable prisons. Captain Drevan Sool is not a simple brute. He reads systems, counts costs, studies Saqarro as a market, and sees Rook as a rare prize. Yara, a bound translator with her own hidden name, begins teaching Rook human words at the mud line. Through her, Rook feels the pull of his own kind and the old hunger to be seen by people shaped like him.

Drevan turns that hunger into a trap. He feeds the dark with gifts, words, and hints about the eye-and-thorn. Rook knows it is bait, but true things can still hook the wounded place. While Rook is pulled between Yara's road and Kala's warnings, Drevan raids the high stones. He uses nets, pitch, hooks, and the tribe's own paths to take Kala, Marrow, and the young as leverage. Voss-Marr fights but chooses to preserve a path and several wounded Vorth rather than spend them all in a doomed rescue. Rook hates him and understands him.

Kala, netted and dying, tells Rook to go. He cannot. He steps out of the green.

Rook attacks and nearly breaks the trap, but the Red wakes in him: an old blood-force that offers strength through appetite, rage, and surrender. He refuses to give it everything. Drevan captures him, kills Kala after the net has done its work, cages Marrow, and drags them from Saqarro. Rook becomes property again, but he keeps his true name hidden.

In the ship's hold and the pit city beyond, Rook learns a larger human machine: ownership as spectacle. Drevan wants to sell him. A pale patron wants to keep him. Yara is bound into the transaction, guilty and useful. Marrow survives beside him, injured but unbroken.

In the pit, handlers expect Rook and Marrow to die as fodder. Instead, Rook reads the beast, the chains, the gate, the ground, and Marrow's crooked strength. Together they turn the pit's own tools against it and survive the first match. The crowd names him without owning his true name. The pale patron recognizes his value. Drevan realizes the sale has become larger than his control.

Rook ends chained in the pit city, not free, but no longer only a victim. Kala is dead. Saqarro is gone. Marrow is beside him. Yara has told him to keep what can still be kept. The Red wants more than Rook has given it.

Rook counts links, guards, keys, Marrow's hand, Yara's rope, and the Red.

The hand opened once. Hands can open again.
