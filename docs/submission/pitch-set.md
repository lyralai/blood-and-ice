# Pitch Set

## One-Sentence Pitch

A formerly enslaved boy survives a death-jungle, becomes king among its monster-peoples, then is captured by human slavers and forced to decide whether the old blood-force inside him is salvation or another master.

## Three-Sentence Pitch

Rook owns almost nothing except the true name he keeps hidden behind his teeth. When he wakes alone on Saqarro, a tropical death-jungle that kills humans, he survives long enough to be taken in by the Vorth, brutal nonhuman people who teach him that family can have teeth and still be real. But when human slavers turn his love into bait, Rook is dragged back into ownership and must learn whether the Red in his blood can help him break the pit city without making him a monster first.

## Short Query Hook

Rook escaped one cage into a jungle that should have killed him. Instead it made him useful, then beloved, then king. When human slavers take him back, they think chains will make him property again. They do not understand what the island taught him to count.

## Back-Cover Draft

Rook has been property longer than he has been anything else.

When a wreck leaves him alone on Saqarro, survival should be impossible. The air rots wounds. The water hides teeth. The jungle eats human certainty first and human bodies soon after.

Rook survives anyway.

His blood closes cuts too quickly. Fever passes through him and leaves him cleaner than before. The island's creatures read something in him he cannot name.

Then the Vorth find him: huge, brutal, grieving people with their own laws, dead, children, rank, and teeth. Among them, Rook becomes more than escaped cargo. He becomes kept. Then useful. Then beloved. Then dangerous.

For the first time, he has a home.

When human slavers cross the water, they bring cages clever enough to travel, ledgers for the dead, and a captain patient enough to turn love into a snare. Rook sees the trap. He takes it anyway.

At the bottom of the pit, with his brother chained beside him and his true name still hidden, the old red force in Rook's blood begins to speak.

It can help him survive.

It can also teach him a new kind of ownership.

## Comp Positioning

Working comps to validate before querying:

- *Red Rising* for propulsion, first-person intensity, and a boy remade by a brutal system.
- *The Rage of Dragons* for visceral epic fantasy violence and revenge-pressure momentum.
- *The Will of the Many* for institutional power, hierarchy, and a protagonist reading systems from below.
- *The Spear Cuts Through Water* or *The Blacktongue Thief* only if positioning toward literary/weird fantasy agents who respond to voice-forward secondary worlds.

Avoid overclaiming direct similarity to *A Song of Ice and Fire* or *The Name of the Wind* in a query. Keep those as private craft influences, not market comps.
