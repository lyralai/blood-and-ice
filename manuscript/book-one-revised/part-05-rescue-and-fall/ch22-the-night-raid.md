# Chapter 22: The Night Raid

They came for the Kala.

---

He did not read it in time.

The count had said they would come for him — for the thing with the disc, the thing that kept the monsters back. The count was wrong. The count was the count of a thing that leans at its own wanting and reads the world through the wanting. The count did not account for the big one reading the high stones with the eyes of a thing that has hunted value in every port and every sea, and the value the big one read was not the two-legged thing with the disc.

It was the Kala.

A single great Vorth — silvered, vast, the hide worth a lord's ransom in the fighting pits or the collector's cages of the old cities. The thing the sky-reader drew on her map. The thing the steady-handed woman priced in her head when she saw the warriors on the killing ground. The big one read the high stones the way a hunter reads a herd — counting, learning where it bunches thick, where it thins — and what he saw was not a tribe. What he saw was *stock.*

And the Kala was the slowest. The Kala was the vastest. The Kala folded down at the edge of the high stones in the dark, the keeping-arm over whatever was nearest, the great slow tide of her breathing the long even breathing of a thing that had been the mother of the tribe for longer than the boy had been alive and had forgotten how to be afraid.

They came in the dark before the low. Four of them. The big one was not there — the big one drew the plan and stayed at the spit, because the big one was the holding-center and the holding-center did not walk into the thing that would break the center if it went wrong. The thick-armed one led. The young one was the runner. Two others the boy had not read close — hard-faced, quick-handed, the kind that does the taking.

They had nets. Rope nets, weighted at the edges, the weave tight enough to hold a thrashing thing. They had the short iron. They had a plan — the sky-reader's plan, drawn on the map, the way in and the way out through the green where the canopy thins at the landward edge of the high stones.

They came through his green in the dark and he did not hear them.

He heard everything in his green. Every footfall, every breath, every shift of weight in the canopy. But the thick-armed one had learned something in the lows on the spit. He moved the way a thing moves that has been taught by the green what the green kills — careful, placed, the weight on the flat of the foot, the breath held and let through the nose. Not silent the way the boy was silent. Silent enough. Silent enough for a four-thing raid in the dark before the low when the tribe was sleeping and the king was on the seaward stack reading the water for the thing the king feared was coming for him.

The king had been wrong. The king had been reading the wrong thing.

---

Marrow heard them.

Not the thick-armed one — Marrow did not hear the thick-armed one in time either. Marrow heard the Kala. The low sound she made when the net went over her — not the killing-sound, not the challenge. The *surprise* sound, the sound of a vast slow thing that had not been threatened in a thousand lows and did not have the sound for it ready in the great chest. A sound like the earth moving. The Kala came up off the stone under the net and the weighted edges dragged and the thick-armed one was there with the short iron and the rope, and the rope went around the great wrists, and the Kala made the sound again — louder now, the surprise going to something else, the thing under the surprise, the thing that said *I am taken* — and the sound went across the high stones like a wave and hit the boy on the seaward stack and he was off the rock before the sound had finished.

Marrow was ahead of him. The runt had heard it first and was already running — the rocking crooked gait, the wrong body at full speed, the high shoulder pumping, the curled hand closed to a fist. Running for the Kala the way he ran for the boy when the boy had been too long in the dark, and he did not know what he was running toward and he ran.

He got there first. He got to the edge of the high stone where the Kala was being taken and he put his small wrong body between the Kala and the thick-armed one and he made the only sound he had — not the still-sound, not the name the boy had given him, the *other* sound, the sound the boy had never heard him make, the sound that comes out of a thing the order cast out when the thing it loves is being taken. It was small. It was torn. A body putting itself in front of a weight that would crush it because the body behind the weight was the body that kept it alive.

The thick-armed one did not hesitate. He backhanded Marrow out of the way.

The small wrong body left the stone. Not far — a body-length, two — and hit the rock and rolled and the curled hand scraped across the stone and the sound Marrow made was the sound of a body hitting ground wrong, the air going out of it, and he did not get up.

The boy was there.

---

The thing that came out of the canopy onto the high stone was not the king.

He knew this the way he knew the tide — from the outside, from the body that watches itself do a thing the head has not agreed to. The king set the jaw and read the ground and counted the lows. The thing on the high stone did not count. It was what the still place held back, the thing under the crown, the thing that had wanted to be the hand since the hand was first laid on the back of the neck.

The Red came up.

Not the omen-pulse the seer read, not the slow warm wrongness under the blood. This was the *other* thing — the thing the boy had never let all the way up, the thing the second mouth feeds on, the thing he held back on the king's seat when the flat eye was on him and the wanting was bright and hot. The thing he did not feed.

He fed it.

The sound that came out of him was not a human sound. It was not the rasp, not the cough, not the falls. It was the sound the Hesh made when it came through the green on the moved line, the sound the old male made when the rot-tooth went bad and the fever took the brain — the deep-chest killing-sound of a thing that has come all the way out from under the still place and is not holding any part of itself back.

The young one was the first to see it. He was in the canopy above the raid, the runner's place, and he looked down and what came over his face was the look of a body that has been afraid of the green since it beached and has just found out the green was right. His mouth opened. No sound came out. The body locked. The branch he was on swayed.

The thick-armed one turned from Marrow's body on the stone and read the boy and the axe came up — fast, trained, the muscle-knowledge of a thing that has fought in tight spaces — and the boy went through him.

Not around. Not under. *Through.*

The speed was wrong. The speed was the thing the Red brings up from the place under the blood, the thing that is not muscle-speed and not wind-speed but the speed of a body that is being run by something older than the body. He took the thick-armed one's axe-arm at the wrist and the wrist bent the wrong way and the axe dropped, and the other two were turning, the rope and the net in their hands, and he was at the first one before the net was off the Kala and the short iron was in his hand and then out of the hard-faced one's hand and the man went down.

The Kala was making the sound. The deep-chest sound, the *I am taken* sound, and the rope was on her wrists and the net was on her back and she was pulling — the vast slow strength of her pulling against the rope — and the rope was holding because the rope was made for things that are strong and the maker of the rope knew what strong was.

He went for the rope. The second hard-faced one put himself between the boy and the Kala and he had a blade — shorter than the short iron, curved, the kind that opens things — and he was fast and he was trained and he was not ready for the thing the boy was this low. The blade opened the boy's arm — the hot bright pain of a thing that cuts, the blood coming — and the wound did not slow him because the Red was up and the Red did not read the body's pain the way the body read it. The cut was deep and the blood ran and the edges were already pulling shut when the boy took him by the throat and put him on the ground.

The young one dropped from the canopy. He dropped behind the boy, between the boy and the edge of the stone, and he had a blade — small, shaking, the hand that holds it not a fighter's hand — and he was making a sound, a word, the same word over and over, the body reads the shape of it even past the Red: *please, please, please.* His face was white and wet and the blade shook and he was fourteen lows old maybe, maybe younger, and his eyes were the eyes of a thing that has just found out that the world has things in it that cannot be read and cannot be fought and do not stop.

The Red said: *take him.* The Red said: *the throat, the wrist, the thigh, the thing that is between you and the Kala, break it.* The Red was the thing under the blood that has no word and no law and no crown, and it runs the body like the hand ran the body in the hold — owning, certain, the grip that says *you are a thing and the thing is mine.*

The boy looked at the young one. The blade. The shaking hand. The eyes.

And the still place — what lived behind the Red, what held the line on the king's seat, what did not feed the second mouth on Gull-Marr when the neck was there — the still place came up behind the Red and read the young one and read the blade and read the age of the thing in front of him and said one word, low, the way the Kala says *be still*:

*No.*

The boy turned from the young one. He went to the Kala.

---

The rope was thick. The weave was tight. His hands were bloody and the short iron was in his grip and he sawed at the rope and the rope gave, strand by strand, and the Kala pulled and the net shifted and the great dark eye found him over the shoulder and read him — read the Red still up, read the blood on the arm where the cut was already closed, read what her boy had become — and the still-sound came out of the great chest. *Be still. Be still. Nothing comes.* Not to him. To the Red. The mother talking the beast out of the boy the way she talked the fever out of him on the bank.

But there were more of them. Not four — more. The big one had been at the spit, but the big one was not at the spit. The big one was at the edge of the green with the rest of the pack — the sky-reader, the steady-handed woman, the cook, the others — and they had the Kala's trail marked and the rope and the chain and the plan, and the plan was never four raiders. The plan was four raiders and then the rest.

They came out of the green with the chains.

The boy fought. The Red ran the body and the body did things he could not count later — the short iron and the teeth and the weight thrown low and sideways, the law of pulling the ground from under the strength. He broke the first chain. He broke the second. The thick-armed one was on him with the bad wrist and the weight and the boy put him down again, hard, and he did not get up this time, and the Kala was pulling the net and the net was half-gone and the rope was going and—

The third chain went around his arm. The fourth went around the Kala's neck.

And the nets came from behind — not the raid pack, the *rest*, the ones the boy had not seen because the Red had him reading the four in front — and the nets came over the Kala from the landward side, weighted, layered, and the great body went down under the weight of them, slow, the way the tide goes down, and the still-sound died half-made in the great chest.

The boy went down on top of her.

Not the fighting-down — the *holding.* The body curling around the Kala's neck where the chain was, the arms going around the great head, the front pressed to the hide where the gentling-sound lived. Shielding. The thing the Kala did for him on the bank when the fever took him under — the body over the body, the keeping-arm over the thing that is kept. He did it with the chains on his arm and the blood on his front and the Red still up under the skin, and he would not let go.

The big one stood at the edge of the stone and read the thing on the ground. The boy curled around the chained beast, the arms around the great head, the body making the shape of the keeping-arm the Kala taught him. And the look on the big one's face was not the look of a thing that has taken a score.

It was the face of a man who has seen something he did not expect to see and cannot stop seeing.

He made a sound. Low. The others stopped. The nets settled.

---

And the small one came.

Marrow. Off the stone where the thick-armed one hit him. Not dead — the boy read him through the Red's fading edge, the chest going, the curled hand twitching against the rock. He was up. Wrong — the high shoulder riding wrong, the gait listing, blood on the face where the rock took the skin. But up. And coming. Not away — *toward.* The small wrong body hauling itself across the stone toward the thing that chained him and the thing he loved, and what came out of it was the sound from before — not the still-sound, not the challenge, the *other* sound, the one the boy had never heard him make before this night and now twice in it. The sound of a thing that will not be left behind.

A hard-faced one moved to stop him. The big one made a sound — short, sharp. *Let it come.* The big one had read the boy on the ground curled around the beast, and the small one crawling toward the boy, and the thing the boy did when the small one was hit — the Red, the killing-speed, the going-through. The big one calculated: the small one was leverage. The thing that makes the boy comply. The thing that makes the killing-machine go still when the chain goes on.

Marrow reached the boy. He put the curled hand on the arm with the chain — the arm that was around the Kala's head — and he folded down against the boy's side, and the small wrong body was warm against the great warm body, and they were three things chained on the high stone in the dark. The boy and the runt and the beast, curled together, the one around the other around the other.

The bold heart came last. The small fierce thing that rode Marrow's shoulder — it dropped from the canopy where it had fled when the nets came, and it landed on Marrow's back and gripped the wrong-made spine with its claws and made the sound it makes when it is afraid — high, sharp, with nowhere else to go. It gripped. It did not leave.

The big one read the three of them on the stone. The boy, the runt, the beast, the bold heart. His face did what it did when he saw the disc on the high stones — the widening, the breath-hold. He was seeing something he did not expect, and the seeing was changing something behind his eyes the boy did not have a word for.

---

The Red went down slow under the still place. The still place came up. The name was there under the tongue — *Rook* — and the brand was there on the neck, and the disc was there at the chest, and the Kala was there under the chains, breathing, alive, and Marrow was there against the boy's side, breathing, alive, and the bold heart gripped the wrong-made spine with its claws and would not let go.

And the boy did not remember the thick-armed one's face when he took the wrist.

This was what the seer's finger found. Not a passenger. Not a thing *in* the boy — what the island *made* of him. A thousand lows of the green. A thousand lows of the fever that does not kill and the swarms that do not land and the rot that eats everything but the one thing it cannot eat. The body went through it and the body survived it and the surviving burned the survival-thing down to the root — burned away the part that knows when to stop.

The cold came — the shivering, the wounds pulling, the weakness. These he could bear.

The gap he could not bear.

The Red had been up and the body moved and the boy was *in* the body the way he was in the body when the green took him through the canopy — the body first, the head riding behind. But the Red does not take the body through the canopy. The Red takes the body through the place the island built — the deep survival-place where the killing is not a choice but a law, the law of the thing that does not stop, the law that kept him alive when every other thing on the fever-island died. And when the Red is up that law runs the body and the body does what the law says and the head is not there. The head is behind. The head is riding. And when the Red goes down the head comes back and the head does not have the whole picture.

He remembered the thick-armed one's axe-arm. He remembered the wrist bending the wrong way. He did not remember the face when it bent. He remembered the hard-faced one with the blade and the blade opening his arm. He did not remember the sound the man made when he went to the ground. He remembered the young one on the ground with the shaking blade and the still place saying *no.* He did not remember how close the still place came to saying nothing.

The gap was his. The Red did not eat the memory — the Red *was* the memory. The Red was the deepest part of the boy running without the rest of him attached, and the part that runs without the rest does not record. It acts. It is the purest survival-thing the island left — the thing that does not think, does not count, does not hold the still place. It just goes. And the going is fast and thorough — the thing that kept him king of the wild things, and the thing that would have taken the young one's throat if the still place had been one fraction slower.

Every time the Red came up, the still place was slower. Not because something was eating it. Because the island wired the survival-thing deeper than the still place, and every time the survival-thing ran, the wiring held a little better and the still place held a little worse. The island made the thing that does not stop. The still place was what the boy built *after* — the name, the count, the crown, the Kala's teaching. The after-thing. Newer. Shallower. And the island-thing was the root.

The boy curled around the Kala's neck. The cold came — the body-cost. The shivering, the numb hands, the cut on the arm pulling open. These he could bear. But the gap sat in him where the memory should be, and the gap was the shape of what the island made, and he did not know if the after-thing — the name, the count, the still place — was deep enough to hold the root when the root runs.

The warmth he could give her now — the blood-warmth that steadied her breathing on the king's seat — was there. But it lived in the same deep place the Red lived in. The survival-place. The island-place. Reaching for the warmth meant reaching for the root, and the root was awake now. The root went down but the root liked the running and was ready to run again. He could not reach for the warmth without reaching for the thing that does not stop.

He did not reach. And the gentling-sound came one more time, muffled by the net, aimed at the boy who held on.

*Be still. Nothing comes.*

Something had come. And the boy was counting the breaths between what had come and what comes next, and the number was not going down.

It was gone.
