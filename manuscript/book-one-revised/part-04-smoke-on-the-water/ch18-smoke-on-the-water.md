# Chapter 18: Smoke on the Water

There were things on the water that sat it wrong.

---

The boy was up on the seaward stack in the green half-light of the low, where his green ran out to bare wet rock and the channel opened past the mangrove into the wide flat water and the haze. He came up here before a hunt to read the tide — the set of the swell, the dark roads under it where the fat river-pig ran the shallows. The water lay past the pinch the way it always lay, hot and pale and old under the standing haze.

And there was a thing on it that had no business being there.

One shape. Long, dark, sitting the swell the way a thing sat it that had lost the fight with the water. But it held a line — not the dumb hold of a drift-log pushed flat, the lean hold of a thing that still had a will in it, pointed, even now. Even dying.

Marrow was the one who still first. The boy felt him go — the high shoulder drawing level with his own, the curled hand resting against his chest where it rested when it had nothing yet to do. Marrow had come for the river-pig. He had stopped looking at the shallows. He was looking out past the boy at the shape, and every part of him had gone quiet at once — even the breath. The curled hand had started to work, slow, open and shut against the chest, the only moving thing on him. He made no sound. He did not need to. The boy had read him a thousand lows, and the working hand had only ever meant one thing.

The boy read the water himself before he let the stillness in.

He had read it more lows than he had counted. The tide-beast rolled with the swell, gave to it. A drift-log did not roll, but there was no will in it; it went where the brown flood shoved it. This did neither. It sat up on the water with its will in it and it cut. The swell lifted it and it came out the other side still pointed. A thing that held its line against the sea had a head. A head chose.

The word came up out of him. Up from far down, from under all the green he had packed over everything before the green, and it came up whole.

*Boat.*

The whole skin of him drew tight at once — the small hairs lifting along the arm, the gut clenching to a fist. The body knew the word before the head could turn it. The body wore the word.

The boat was wrong. It wallowed where it should have cut. The oars that worked were out of time, the hull sat too low in the brown water. There were not enough oars for the size of it. He read it the way he read a wounded thing — the listing, the slowness, the desperation of a body that felt the ground going under it. The boat was running from something.

---

Marrow dropped the high shoulder. All the way down. It was the thing he did when the green had gone bad and he wanted to be small in it, and he had never once done it for a thing the boy could fight.

The boy was off the stack before the head knew the body had chosen to move. Marrow was off it the same breath, low at his flank, the rocking crooked run eating the broken ground as fast as the boy's clean stride — quick where he was wrong-made, always quicker than the strong thought a thing built like him could be.

Low. Belly to the wet rock. The boy went down off the high stone into the broken country he knew in the dark of him — the rot-gullies and the root-cages and the mud that took a foot to the knee if the foot did not know the seat of it. He knew the seats. Marrow knew where he put his. They went fast and silent through the place that was only his, *his green,* and he did not stop at the crack worn to the shape of his back, and he did not go up to the high stones where the tribe slept easy behind the new crown. He went to the lookout over the white spire of bone and sand, the place he had not walked since he dragged the relics off it, because the thing in him that hungered was already turned that way and he could not not look.

His heart went against the slab like a hunted thing in a fist. And it was not the boat his heart ran from. It was what came up behind the word for it.

The brand. The dark of the hold, the wet wood sweating, the close stink of too many bodies packed where they could not stand. The big hand closing on the back of his neck from behind — slow, sure, owning the whole of him in the one grip — and the low sound made behind the teeth that went with the hand every time, the sound that meant *come,* that meant *be still,* that meant *you are a thing and the thing is mine.* The hand and the sound were one. He felt it now as a weight on the neck, and the skin there crawled and tried to get away from a hand far out on the water and lows past counting gone into the deep.

That was what the shape meant. That was what hands meant. That was what *many-of-them, together, pointed* meant.

He lay flat on the rock in the steam and watched the boat come in. Marrow lay down at his side without being told. He did not look at the water now. He looked at the boy, then at the high stones behind them, then at the boy — the curled hand still working, the eyes going back along their trail. He wanted off this rock. Every other low of his life he had read the green for the boy and given him the truth before he asked. This low the truth was in his body, and the boy was not turning to read it, because the hunger had already turned him the other way.

---

The boat came like a thing into ground it could not read — slow, the oars feeling the channel, the dark uprights leaning out over the bow to read the brown water for the bank that would take the keel. They did not know the bank. He knew the bank. He knew the mud that breathed and the still warm shallows where the Mure rose at low water — pale, wet-skinned, web-fingered — and took a thing down into the brackish before it had the sound out. The boat came in over the Mure's own water and did not know it. The dead expedition had not known it either.

The shore made you pay. These had not learned the first toll.

They ran the boat in on desperation and luck. The keel ground up the sand of the bone-spit, and the dark uprights came over the sides. Not many. Fewer than the boat was built for. Some moved wrong — stiff, favoring, one with an arm bound in torn cloth. They were hurt. Not the green-hurt — the hurt of things that had been hit. There was a smell off the boat the boy knew from the drowned-deep place: the iron-smell of someone else's blood in the seams of the deck. They had been in a fight and come away broken.

They hauled the hull up the mud and set things down and took things up — the endless handling that only the upright pack did. None were the very small ones. These were a pack come without its young, a pack that came to take and not to live. But the taking had gone wrong, and what was left of them stood on the death-shore and did not know it.

And the boy read them the way he read a herd before the hunt — counting, learning where it bunched thick, where it thinned and went soft and a thing could come in along the blind side. The far hunger opened under his breastbone so wide it hurt worse than the belly ever hurt.

Because they were his. Not the tribe-mine. The other mine. The shape of the thing he had before the hold turned over far down in him, under the green, under all the lows. They went on two legs as he went. They had the working hands, the dark scarred clever hands that turned a rock to see what folded under it. They made the sounds at each other — words thrown mouth to mouth and caught and thrown back, the whole easy road of it that he had lost behind the gap where three teeth were torn. They were the shape of the hole in him that the tribe filled with everything but its own exact shape. And the hole knew itself when it saw itself walk up out of a boat. It strained toward the shape of its own outline.

Marrow made a sound at last. Low in the throat, almost nothing — the small distress-note he made over a wound that would not close. He was not looking at the pack. He was looking at the boy looking at the pack. He had seen the reach in him before the boy had owned it, read it off his front as he read everything, and he did not like it. The curled hand had stopped working. It had closed to a fist.

The boy had a crown this low. He had the Kala's arm over him in the easy sleep and the bold heart warm on his shoulder and a home with no between in it, the first he had ever had. He had Marrow at his flank, the nearest thing to his own kind the green ever gave him and not his kind at all. The far hunger had been full. He had felt it go full on the king's seat, all the way down. And it pulled anyway, toward the spit and the two-legged pack on it, toward a warmth shaped exactly like the one torn out of him before the green, and it would not be told it was already fed.

That was the new wrongness. Not the body-wrong, the blood-wrong, the old wrongness he had set down and walked past a thousand lows. This was a head-wrong. A want-wrong. A thing could be full and reach anyway — could open a new seam under the full one, the way the body opened a seam to taste fear, and let in the old human-shaped hunger that no Vorth was ever cut to fill. Not even Marrow. Marrow most of all not.

---

One of them stood where the handling was thickest and the others turned to it before a thing went down. Not the biggest. But the big ones looked to him. He was thick through the chest and slow in the moving, and when he put a hand on a back it stayed there a breath longer than work needed. The chin pointed and the work bent that way.

He moved through the camp the way Gull-Marr had moved through the tribe — slow, certain, touching things as he passed. A shoulder here. A hand on a back there. Not the hand that owned. The hand that held together.

The boy's gut turned.

He stopped at the one with the bound arm and crouched and unwrapped the cloth, and his hands were gentle on the wound. Then the tired settled into him — the tired of a thing that had held something together too long. He was the holding-center. The boy read it the way he read the old male with the torn ear who had come to the foot of the lip: *this one bears the weight so the others do not feel the ground.* And the reading turned something in the chest that the crown had not settled, because the holding-center was the shape of the hand that owned and the shape of the hand that held and the boy could not tell, from this distance, in this light, which one it was. Maybe the big one did not know either. Maybe that was the thing the tired meant.

The boy watched the big hands on the bound arm and the body remembered the cracked-nail hand. The hand that hit and the hand that fed. The same hand, the same act, landing on the same boy. He had read that law in the cage and laid it down as the cage's true lesson. And here it was on two legs on the bone-spit — the gentleness and the holding and the slow certain way the big one moved through the pack, and the boy could not separate it from the brand-hand because they were made of the same shape and the body would not let the head forget it.

A thick-armed one stood behind the holding-center the way Voss-Marr had stood behind the king. He carried a thing on his hip that had only one shape to it — not for carrying and not for making. He was not the strength. He was the fear of the strength, the held blow. And under the held-blow stillness, something the boy did not expect: the thick-armed one was afraid. Not of the green. Of the holding-center's face when the dying went too far. He watched the big one the way Marrow watched the boy — reading the front for the thing behind the front, braced for the moment it cracked.

And a young one — the runner, the climber, all quick joints and no weight in the settling. He was the age the boy had been when the green took him. The holding-center put a hand on the back of the young one's neck and held it there a breath. Not the brand-hand. Something else. The boy knew the weight of that hand. He had worn it. The Kala put that weight on the back of his neck in the dark, and the still-sound came with it: *be still, you are held.* The big one's hand on the young one's neck was the same weight and the same holding and it meant the same thing, and the far hunger pitched toward it so hard the boy's breath broke.

He had seen this. Not the shape — the thing under the shape. The holding-together. The shoulder-laid and the hand on the back of the neck that meant *you are mine and I am yours and the thing coming for us has to come through that first.* He had seen it in the Kala's arm. He had seen it in the old male standing foot-for-foot at his side. It was the thing the tribe gave him. And here it was on two legs, made of the same shape that made the hold and the brand — and it was not the brand. It was the other thing. The thing the hand could be when the hand was not closing.

A woman read the sky. She stood at the tide-line with her face up, reading the wind. She had a heading that did not match the big one's, and they argued — short, sharp — but the argument had the shape of two minds that trusted each other enough to be wrong at each other.

A steady-handed woman whose hands went to wounds the way the boy's hands went to a kill. A big gentle one who cooked and made sounds nobody laughed at, and the not-laughing had affection in it.

They were a tribe.

The word sat in the boy's chest like a stone dropped in still water. He had not thought it. It had thought itself. And the far hunger, which had been full, which had been fed on the king's seat until the far mouth closed for the first time in his life — the far hunger opened a new seam under the full one, the way a root splits stone, and the new seam was shaped exactly like the thing dying on the sand below, and the boy could not close it.

---

The wrongness stirred.

Not the reaching of the far hunger — the other thing, the old thing, the thing under the ribs. It had been quiet since the crown. It had gone to ground the way it went to ground when the apex was near, folded small and still against the bone. But it was not cowering. It was reading.

The wrongness was reading the pack the way it had read the swine before the kill — studying, weighing, the quality of attention it brought to a thing it was learning the law of. And what it read was not prey and not threat. What it read was *the shape of the hand.* The wrongness knew the hand. It had been made by the hand, shaped in the cage the hand built, fed the iron taste the hand laid on the boy's tongue. The hand was on the water and the wrongness turned toward it the way it turned toward fire and rage and the taste of blood — not wanting. Recognizing.

The boy felt the recognition and it was worse than the wanting. The far hunger reached for the pack because it was hungry. The wrongness reached for the pack because it was *known.* The thing under his ribs had been shaped by the hand, and the hand had made a shape it fit, and the shape was calling it home.

He pressed the wrongness down. It went the way it had gone after the king's fall — unwilling, sullen, with its teeth. But the going was harder now. On the high stones the only pull was the appetite for the fallen king. Here the pull was the shape of the hand, and the shape of the hand was older than the cage, and the wrongness knew it even if the boy did not.

The shelf in him took the weight of the two wants — the far hunger reaching and the wrongness recognizing — and the shelf bore down the way it always did, heavier with each thing he added, and never lighter. Eight wrongs on it now. The shape of the hand made eight.

---

He should go. Every fast part of him said go — up to the high stones, to the crown and the Kala, put the whole broken country between him and the hands. A pack this many would sweep the green like the rains swept the gullies. He wore the crown now and the crown was still one body, and he had seen what the many did to the one. He wore it on the neck. The many were how the one was unmade. Marrow knew it too — he was pressed to the rock as small as he could make himself.

But he did not go.

He lay on the wet rock and watched them set up on the bone-spit where every other human had died, and the body read the thing the head was slow to hold:

They were going to die.

He knew it the way he knew a tide before the sky showed the moon. He did not want to know it. The green would eat them. Not the teeth first — the green did not need its teeth for the upright pack. It had the fever-air that dropped every other body and left him clear-headed. It had the bad water and the still rot-pools that looked like drink. It had the swarms — already he could see them lifting dark off the rot as the boat settled. It had the Mure under the shallows they had beached across. It had the killing heat that would turn every scrape those hands took to rot and fly-blows in a few lows while his drew shut clean.

The green ate humans. That was the one law of this place he had learned without learning it, because he was the one human it had never been able to eat — and he had never known that was a strange thing to be until he had no others to measure against.

He watched the dark uprights build their fire on the spit. Standing smoke, straight up in the still steam — fire a thing made on purpose and tended and not let go out. Smoke on the water. And he lay above it, the thing the green could not kill, and counted, low by low, the deaths the green had already laid out for them.

He knew which water did not poison and which air did not drop the body. He carried the one thing the dead expedition had not had, and he could lay it on the air for them the way they laid their words. The body did not go. The not-going had a taste — the flat taste of the hold, the taste of every hand that knew and did not come. And now he was the thing on the rock that knew the green would eat them and did not open its mouth, and the staying-shut pressed against the fullness the Kala had laid in him until the two were the same weight — the held name and the withheld knowing, both locked behind the teeth, both his, and the green ate, and he let it eat.

The cage had taught him what the many did to the one. The crown had taught him what the one did when the one had something to lose.

This was the cost the seer had not named — not the blood-wrong but the crown-wrong. The thing the having-something-to-lose did to the boy who had been nothing and had known every shape of the losing. Now the losing was not all there was, and the boy who let the green eat his own kind because opening his mouth was the first thing the crown would cost him.

---

The green took them fast.

The first low: a coughing one. The wet tearing in the chest, the drowning sound. The steady-handed woman's hands on him, the orders barked short, the professional set of her jaw. Before the dark the holding-center went in to read what her hands found, and when he came out his face had changed. The coughing one died in the night. They covered him with stones.

The boy lay above and watched the stones go down and the body counted: *one.* The wrongness watched too. It did not gloat. It did not cower. It catalogued, the way it catalogued the wrong things — fever-air, bad water, fast healing, swarms, fire-lean, den-settling, iron taste, and now the dying of his own kind, the eighth wrong turned inside out. The green did to them what it could not do to him, and the wrongness filed it under the same heading: *the thing that makes me different.* The shelf bore down.

The second low: a sting in the shallows. The steady-handed woman cut the puncture, packed it with paste from wrong leaves — she did not know this green. The leg went the color of the rot. She shook her head.

A third went into the green to cut limbs and came out with a bite that swelled and turned hot. She cut the arm off at the elbow with the thick-armed one's blade. He lived one low past the cutting and died in the dark.

The thick-armed one stood over the dying and his face went flat and he turned away and walked to the tree line and stood with his back to the camp. The young one followed — not touching, not speaking, just there — and after a while the thick-armed one put his hand on the young one's head, brief, rough, and they came back to the fire.

The boy watched the hand on the young one's head. The gesture was all wrong and all right at once — the held-blow hand learning to be the holding hand, the fear-of-strength learning to be the strength. The body knew that learning. The body had done it on the king's seat when the wrongness wanted the fallen king's neck and the boy took the standing instead. The cost of the not-feeding. The cost of choosing what the hand becomes. The thick-armed one was choosing, and the choosing was harder than the dying around him, and the boy lay above and read the whole shape of it and the far hunger turned toward the thick-armed one the way it turned toward the bold heart — because the thing under the held blow was the thing under the crown: the cost of having something to lose.

---

By the third low the holding-center stood at the tide-line and read the green wall and the thinning pack at his back. He was counting lows. The boy could read the counting in the set of the shoulders, the way the big body held itself when the holding-center was doing the arithmetic of what he had left. The sky-reader stood beside him and pointed at the green — inland, away from the death-shore. He shook his head. She pointed again, sharper, her voice going up. The voices had the worn edge of a thing argued before, many times, and not settled either time.

And the holding-center put both hands flat on the rail of the boat and looked at them — his own hands — and the boy read the gesture because the body had made it. The looking at the hands to find what the hands were. The boy had looked at his own hands after the king's fall — the cracked-nail hand, the hand that had pulled the ground from under the king and then did not take the neck. The hands were the question and the answer and the boy knew the weight of standing over your own hands and not knowing what they were going to do next.

The big one nodded. Once. The nod of a thing that had drawn a new line because the old one was killing everyone behind it. They broke camp. They went inland, toward the green, toward the high stones.

The boy followed them through the canopy. The far hunger pulled at every step they took into the deeper shade. They did not know what lived in the high green. They did not know about the Vorth.

Marrow followed him. Behind them the bone-spit held its dead and the green came down over the camp. Ahead of them the hurt pack walked deeper into the island with the holding-center at its front and the sky-reader behind him and the young one in the branches above.

And the boy counted the lows they had left against the thing they were walking toward, and the number was going down.

---

He lay above the moving pack in the last of the half-light and held the two things the water had given him, the way he had held the two things the crown gave him on the king's seat — the fullness and the terror, the having and the losing, pressed against each other in the chest where the far mouth lived.

The first was the law. The green ate humans. These had come to walk into the death he had survived, and he knew it before they took their first bad drink. There was a thing in him — old, under the crown — that wanted to lie here and count them down into the mud, until the green had eaten the shape of the hole and he could stop reaching at a grave.

The second was the pull. He stretched toward the dying pack anyway, because they were the shape of it. The crown had not closed it. The Kala had not closed it. Marrow had not closed it. Nothing on this island closed it but the thing the green was going to eat.

He kept the law behind his teeth, and the pull beside it, next to the name no one could catch and the brand he would give back someday across the water. The fed fire burned low and red on the white spire where every human before him had died. The swarms settled on every body but the two lying still above them — the boy and the runt who would not leave him.

The crown was the highest ground he had ever stood on, and what stood on the water was the taking that ground invited. He had known a taking would come. It had a fire now.

And beside him Marrow had given up nudging and put his curled hand over his eyes — the small wrong body saying the thing words could not: that the boy had already started counting the lows between, and that he knew it, and that he would count them with him anyway.
