# Chapter 21: The Sigil

The shape he carried out of the dead men's mud had found its hunter, and the hunter wore its brother on his chest.

---

She did not keep it.

The boy learned that the way he learned a turn in the weather — by the thing doing what it did not do before. The owned girl had drawn the Eye-and-Thorn on the wet air at his line and laid her careful sound on it, and she had not kept it behind her own teeth. She had carried it back up the spit in her bent mouth, across from the road of the owned to the road of the owning, and laid it down at the owning-fire, and now the fire knew.

He read the knowing from above. Three lows lying in the high drowned roots over the bone-spit, reading the upright pack the way he read a herd that had caught a scent. Before, the camp had bunched at its work — the hauling, the cutting, the endless setting-down and taking-up, the cages dragged and lashed, the green hacked back off the old high-water flat. Now a thing ran through it that had not run before. It went mouth to mouth, low. A turning of heads, a leaning-in, a sound passed and a sound passed back. They had a thing to chew that was not meat. He did not have the sounds of it. But he had its shape in how they moved, and the shape was this: there is a thing in the green that wears the keeper's mark, and the keeper wants to know.

The far mouth pulled tight under his breastbone. The man-shaped space the fires had carved in him strained toward the warm. He lay still in the roots between the two of them and read the camp leaning back, up the spit, toward him. The thing they chewed was his.

---

He should have killed her, or left her.

The law said it. He had known the law and done the third thing — the thing that was not the law — and now the third thing had a face turning up the spit toward his green.

But the body would not lay it that way. The gut laid it plain first: he had let her see the green move, and she had not run, and she had given him back the shape torn out of him, and he would do it again. He filed the knowing low — that his own wanting had opened a door in his wall, and the brand had walked a step closer through it — and pressed at the edge of it, wanting to see how the door came open. He could not. There was no shape in him for a thing that was its own undoing. He kept his eye on the fire.

---

The captain read the mark himself. The boy watched him do it.

She was brought to him. Not roped — they did not rope her; she was the one they kept close, owned and worth keeping — but two of the owning walked her to the fire with their hands near her, the way a hand stays near a thing that might bolt, and she went folded, shoulders drawn up, the shape a body makes around a wound it does not want to open. The captain stood. The camp made a path for him the way the green made a path for the old predators, and he came to her slow, with the still on him — the coming-on of a thing that did not have to hurry to what it meant to take. The boy had stood under that still on a deck. The back of his neck crawled under it now from three lows off, the old crawl, the skin trying to get away from a hand that was not on it.

The captain said a thing to her. The edge of it came up the spit on the down-wind, flat and low, sounds set against sounds. She took it and turned it in her mouth and laid it back changed, and her hand came up off her drawn side, slow, and drew on the air between them.

The eye, and the thorn crossing it. The same shape she had drawn at his line in the rain. The boy saw her draw it for the captain the way she had drawn it for the dark, and something in him turned, because the shape that had lived between the two of them in the dark at his line was a thing now between her and the keeper. Given over. Gone up the road of the owning.

And the captain went still in a new way.

The boy knew that stillness. It was the stillness the seer had gone into with its old ash-dark finger laid to the blood in his throat — a thing turning toward something it did not look for and now half-saw. The seer had touched the mark, and then touched the blood under the mark, and bowed its head, and the sound it made was a wondering: *this is worth something, this is from before, and I do not know whether it is a blessing or a thing to fear.* The captain heard that same wondering now. The boy watched it come up in him. The man heard the shape of his own iron drawn on the air by his owned girl, drawn off a thing she had found in the dark of the green that ate his men — and the slow certain weighing-look rose and fixed. Not on her. Past her. Out, up the spit, into the wall of dark where the green began.

Into the place where the boy was.

The captain's eyes went over the high drowned roots where the boy lay poured into the mud, and slid off the way every eye slid off — but they went slow now, where before they had passed quick. He was reading the green the way the boy read the green. Looking for the wrongness in the still, the thing that moved when nothing should move it. He did not find him. The wind held true and the boy did not move a frond, not this low, not for this man. But he had the captain's shape whole: the mark on his iron had a brother out in the dark, and the brother was the boy's, and the keeper had just learned that a thing he did not own was wearing the thing that said *owned*. A man made of the slow sure coming-on did not let that lie. He filed it the way the boy filed. Low, sure, kept.

They were reading each other across the spit, the keeper and the kept that got away, and only the boy knew they were both doing it.

---

She came back at the bare of the low.

He had not thought she would. The owners had kept her in after the relic-low, the big one's hand lifting over her bowed neck and not falling, and he had thought the camp would close her up now that the fire had caught. But she slipped the thornwood at the same hour as ever, when the owning-fire was heavy with grease and the owned line was let down off the bundles, and she came down the slick black mud, slow, picking her way, toward the dark wall of his. And she came wrong. Not the easy talking-to-the-dark shape. The strung shape, the hunted-going-still shape, the whole rope of her pulled tight — and she kept looking back over her shoulder at the camp, twice, three times, the way a thing looked back that knew it was watched.

She was watched. He read it before she was halfway down. A shape had come out past the thornwood behind her, low, in the gray, not coming down the spit but standing at the wall of the camp where the cut stumps were, still, with the captain's still on it. A smaller one, sent — the way a captain sent a hand to do the watching his own eyes would not do. They had let her come. To see where she went, and what she went to, and whether the thing in the green would move for her the way the shape drawn on the air said it might.

She was the bait now. She did not know it, or she knew it and came anyway, folded and strung and looking back. The owned girl had walked down the killing spit a second time, but this low she was the hook and not the hand on it — the soft live thing set out on the line to draw the dark up where the watcher could cut it out of the green.

He lay in the roots and the wrongness lay flat in him and read the watcher at the stumps, and the law came up hard behind his teeth: *do not move the green.* Do not lean. Do not give the watcher the wrongness in the still. The far mouth wanted her — wanted the falling sound that did not close, wanted the shape she had given back — and he held the want down by force, pads dug into the mud, throat shut, because the want was the door, and the door had a watcher at the stumps this low with the captain's eye in it.

---

She stopped at his line.

She did not draw on the air. She did not name the weed or the snail or the going-out of the water. She crouched in the streaming gray at the edge of where dry mud went to wet, two of his body-lengths off, near enough that the down-wind brought him the rain off her skin and the thin sour under-smell of a body that had been hungry. And she lifted her face.

She did not aim the sound at the moved frond. She aimed it at him. Low, level, straight up the line of the dark to the exact place where he lay poured into the roots — not where she thought a haunt lived, not where the green had stirred, but *here*, at the man-thing in the mud, eye to where his eye must be. And he understood, the body knowing it a long breath before the head: she had found him. She had stood at this line before and laid her sounds at a guess, at a haunt, at the green itself. Not now. She had put it together in the bent quiet of the owned road — the mark on the chest, the mark drawn on the air, the green that moved and gave back — and she knew. She was not talking to the dark. She was talking to a person. She knew there was a kept thing in here that had gotten loose and wore the keeper's iron, and she had come down the killing spit under a watcher's eye to speak to it on purpose. To him. She had chosen it.

Then she made the sound. Low. Quick. It fell fast and it did not close — it was hers, the open-ended one that landed in his chest and did not grab — but shaped wrong, shaped like the camp-sounds, the sharp careful ones she laid down like stones across bad mud, and it came too fast, a thing pushed out under a held breath. And while she made it, her carrying-hand, the one with the rope-mark worn above it, stayed low against the mud where the watcher at the stumps could not see. The fingers moved. Small. They drew, low against the wet, where only the dark could read them: the eye, and the thorn crossing it. Then they broke. They scattered, the fingers, a thing flying apart. Then they pointed — quick, low, back over her bent shoulder, at the camp, at the watcher, at the fires — and closed. A hand closing.

She was telling him a thing.

He did not have her sounds. He had stolen the weed and the snail and the two leaning water-sounds and the careful camp-sounds, and they were not enough to take the fast low push of this. But the hand he read. The hand was the body's road, the one under the easy road, the one he had read all his life when the mouth-road was shut to him — and the hand said the shape, and the shape flew apart, and the camp, the closing. She was crouched in the rain telling him the mark had set the camp moving. That the keeper wanted the thing that wore it. That the closing hand was coming. She drew it, and carried that knowing down the killing spit under a watcher's eye to lay it at the wall of the green like a thing she could not keep behind her own teeth.

She knew what she warned. That was the thing that turned the whole low over. Not a beast, not a haunt — him. The mirror had lifted its drawn face to the dark and chosen the dark anyway, and warned the kept thing of the keeper, and the warm came up slow and lay down around the warning and held it. She was not the home. She was the owned girl who had walked the killing spit twice and chosen the dark both times. But no one had crossed that ground under watch to warn him of a closing hand. Not since the Kala. And this one had come knowing he would hear it.

---

The watcher called her in.

A sound came from the stumps, short and edged, the closing-sound, the brand-shape laid on the air — *come* — and her head came up and her body folded and she went. She did not look at the dark again. She rose out of the crouch and turned up the spit and walked back into the camp bent under the owning, owned, the warning given and carried and spent, and the watcher fell in behind her the way a hand falls in behind a thing it drives, and the thornwood took them both.

The boy lay in the streaming roots with the brand-shape ringing in his ear. *Come.* The low ugly sound made behind the teeth that meant *mine, be still, you are a thing.* Behind his teeth he held the three things he had held before and a fourth one new. The name that was his and answered nothing. The brand that was theirs and meant *you are a thing.* The path she built for him one low at a time. And now the fourth: the eye flying apart, the camp, the closing hand. The warning. He laid it down beside the rest in the warm hollow where the lost name lay, and the warm came up around all of it together, and he understood — body first, head long after — what the low had made plain.

The shape he had carried out of the dead men's mud meant *owned* to the men.

The seer had read it on his blood as kin-omen, a thing of the kept, a thing from before, and bowed its head and could not say whether it was worth or threat. The keeper read it on his chest the same — a thing of his, a stray gone loose in the green, wearing his mark and not owned by him — and he could not say either, and a thing he could not weigh was a thing he wanted in his hand. The same shape. Two readings. Both of them called it worth the having, and neither could name why, and both reached. The seer had reached with an old ash-dark finger laid light to the blood and named him *kept*. The keeper reached with the still face and the hooked iron, and what he would name the boy was *cargo*. A thing worth the taking. A thing worth carrying off across the water in the dark of a hold, the way the boy had been carried once, the way the lashed things went dull in their cages this low, the way the chained ones stopped fighting the lash and only breathed.

He pressed at it hard this low — why the mark was his and the captain's both, why the seer's finger and the keeper's iron had found the same thing in him and called it the same and could not name it. He turned it every way he had and there was no way in him that fit it. It was the deepest thing on the shelf now, under the wrongs and the fever he did not feel and the gap where his name had gone, and he could not lift it, and not lifting it was its own kind of pain. But he had the body of it, and the body of it was this: he had spent his whole life a thing the world kept reaching for — the hand closing on the back of the neck, the brand, the hold. He had gone into the green and the green could not eat him and he had made himself a king of the wild things, and he had thought the highest stone was the safe stone. It was not. The mark on his chest was a hand reaching up out of the before, out of the dead men's mud, out of the captain's iron, and it had found him again at the top of his green, and it was closing.

---

He lay above the camp until the gray died and the fires came up red on the cut mud.

He watched the captain stand among them with the Eye-and-Thorn on his breast, still, fed, the keeper of the warm in the killing green. He watched the owned girl at the owning-fire, kept close, fed from the owning-pot, the shape she had drawn gone up into the keeper's reading now and out of her hands. He watched the watcher posted by the thornwood where the spit came in, new this low — a body set to read the dark for the thing that wore the mark. They were looking out for him now. The camp bore up the spit toward the green, where before it had bent only at its work. He had drawn its eye without meaning to, and the drawing was done and would not come undone.

He should take the tribe and go deep. Up into the high broken stone and the fever-fog where men died and he did not, where no boat reached and no brand burned, and never lay his shape where the keeper's weighing-look could cut it out of the green. The near part of him said it. The legs said it. The law of the green said it: a thing the keeper wanted was a thing already half in the hold.

He did not go. He had not gone and he would not, and the body knew why before the head could shape it. Because the man-shaped hollow was down there at the fires, straining, full of the easy road he had lost. Because the mark was his and the captain's both and he had to know whose it had been and why it lived in him, and the not-knowing sat in him like a thorn worked in deep. And because there was one down there at the owning-fire who had come down the killing spit under a watcher's eye, who had lifted her face to the exact dark where he lay and spoken to him knowing he was a thing that could hear — the owned girl, the mirror, the one who knew the shape and had chosen to warn the thing that wore it. The warm had lain down around her warning and would not get up off it.

He went back into his green in the full dark, up toward the high stones, toward the Kala and the bold heart and the home with no between in it. The whole way up the rot he carried the new knowing behind his teeth, laid against the name and the brand and the road: the mark he could not read had made him a thing worth the taking, and the keeper had read it, and the camp had caught it, and the hand was coming up the spit out of the before. And the one who had set it moving and the one who had warned him of it were the same owned girl, and she had warned him with her eyes open, knowing what he was.

The taking had read his mark off the air at last. It knew there was something in the green worth the iron. It did not yet know what — and neither did he — and the not-knowing on both sides was the only thing between him and the hold.

Behind him, in the dark above the high stones, Marrow waited at the den-mouth. The runt had not come down to the bone-spit. He had stayed above, the high shoulder turned away from the water and everything on it, and the boy felt him there the way he felt the ridge — a fixed thing in the dark that would not move. Marrow did not know about the sigil or the captain or the warning. He knew only that the boy lay at the lip of the thing that ate them, and that his warning had run out of body to make, and that the boy went back to it every low. The runt's whole crooked body said what no sound could: *come away from the water.* The boy did not. Marrow stayed anyway.

The boy lay down at last in the warm of the Kala's vast slow arm with the eye looking out from his chest into the dark, and the wrongness lay quiet under the ribs — not fighting, not straining, only listening, the way it listened when it was learning the shape of a thing it intended to use. It had heard the captain's stillness and the girl's warning and the closing-sound from the stumps, and it filed them all the way the boy filed them: low, sure, kept. The boy and the wrongness agreed on the captain the way they agreed on the apex — both reading, both weighing, both knowing a thing that did not have to hurry to what it meant to take. That agreement was the worst thing about the low, and the boy felt it under the ribs and could not name why.

He had been seen, two ways now. The seer's way, with the bowed head and the meaning-sound. And the keeper's way — the weighing way, the way a hand reads a neck before it closes. The same mark had drawn them both. Neither could say what it was. Both had reached.

He had been seen, and he was wearing the mark, and the mark said *mine.*
