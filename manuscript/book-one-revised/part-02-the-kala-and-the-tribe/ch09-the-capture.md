# Chapter 9: The Capture

The tribe came on the sixth low after the Kala took him.

He had learned the shape of her days. She left at the turning of the tide and came back when the light changed on the high stones. She did not go to the high stones. She went down, into the places the tribe's feet did not press. She brought back meat and the clear-glass things and once a root that bled red when she broke it and tasted of the warm that was not fever. She laid each thing down in front of him the way the king laid his hand on the dragging one — not to own, to offer. The body took what it was given and did not set it on the shelf because the gap was where it belonged now, and the gap was filling.

He did not hear them come.

The first sign was the Kala's head lifting. Not the slow lift of the gentling sound. The sharp lift. The one the body made when the green changed its mind. Her milk-pale eyes went to the mouth of the hollow and stayed there. The split ear twitched once, flat against the jaw. Then the great body came up off the floor in one motion, the arms taking the stone, the head low, reading the green the way she read the mud for prints that did not belong.

He followed her eyes.

They stood at the channel-edge. Six of them. The king at the front, the pale-rip one at his shoulder, the dragging one three paces back with the young one beside him. The rest he did not know — shapes he had watched from the ridge, heads tipped one way when the call went out, hands that scooped and lifted and touched. They carried no spears. They carried the call.

The king made the sound first. Low. Round. The one that had run down from the high stones every low since the first sighting. *You are still ours. Come back up. We have you.*

The Kala answered with nothing. The body between him and the mouth of the hollow was the answer. The arms spread. The head low. The sound she made was not the gentling. It was the other one. The one the body made when the hand came too close.

The king stepped forward. One step. The pale-rip one moved with him, the eyes measuring the distance between the king's back and the hollow the way a man measures the weight he can carry. The dragging one stayed where he was. The young one looked at the Kala and then at the king and the look was the look of a thing that had never seen a choice made in two directions at once.

The king spoke again. The sound was the same but the shape of it had changed. It carried the weight of the high stones and the count and the fire that was tended and not left to die in the mud. It said: *the many are waiting. The count is wrong without you. The call is for you.*

The Kala did not move.

The wrongness in him stirred. Not the lean toward blood. Not the reach toward warmth. A new thing with no name yet. It pressed against the inside of his ribs the way the tide pressed against the stones, and the pressure was the shape of the question he could not ask: which call belonged to which body.

The king took another step. The pale-rip one matched it. The young one did not. The dragging one did not. The space between the king and the hollow narrowed to the length of an arm and then less.

The Kala made the sound again. Lower. The one the body made when the closing was certain.

The king stopped. The hand that had opened the dragging one's leg lifted, palm up, fingers still. The same offer the Kala made with the clear-glass things. The hand that touched and did not close.

The Kala's head turned. The split ear twitched. The milk-pale eyes went from the king's hand to the young one and back to the king. The body between the hollow and the channel did not move, but the weight of it changed. The wrongness under his ribs went still the way it had gone still before the apex print — recognition, not fear, the old blood knowing something the head had no words for.

The king spoke a third time. The sound was smaller. The call without the weight of the many behind it. Just the sound a body makes when it has been set apart and has stopped trying to be counted.

The Kala answered with the gentling sound. Rough. Broken in places. The one she made against the top of his head when the fever took him. She made it at the king.

The king lowered his hand. The pale-rip one did not. The young one stepped forward once and the dragging one put a hand on the young one's shoulder and the young one stopped.

The capture happened without a hand laid on him.

The king turned. The pale-rip one followed. The dragging one followed. The young one looked back once at the hollow and the look was the look of a thing that had seen two calls answered by the same body and did not know which answer was the true one. Then the young one turned and followed.

The Kala stood at the mouth of the hollow until the last of them had gone back up the channel. The body did not relax. The arms did not come down. The head did not lift from the low reading posture. The wrongness under his ribs stayed still, and the stillness was the thing he could not set on the shelf.

She came back to him when the light changed on the high stones. She laid the meat down. She made the *eat* sound. But the body that had occupied the green the way water fills a channel now moved the way he moved — low, tight, the profile narrow. The green that had opened for her closed a hand's-width around the hollow, and the closing was the whole of what the tribe had taken.

The gap behind his teeth held two names and the sound of the call that had not been answered.

The far hunger began to count the lights between again, the way a man counts the spaces where a fire had been.

The green had decided after all.
