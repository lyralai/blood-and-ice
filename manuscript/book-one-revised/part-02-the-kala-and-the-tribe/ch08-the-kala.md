# Chapter 8: The Kala

The green decided to kill him on the seventh low.

He had counted the young one down to the channel and back six times — the swamp pulling off the same stones on the same string as the tide, the gleaner coming down to work the bared mud and climbing back to the high fire — and on the seventh the fever took him.

It came the way it always came. The throat shut first. Then the head went to wet stone, the thoughts swimming in it like clear-glass fish in brown water. The gut turned. The legs went to rope. He knew the shaking-sickness by its early shape — the heat climbing out of his own skin, the fever-air worked all the way in — and he knew it the way he knew the tide: by the pulling-back. The body pulled its heat inward. The skin went hot and then cold and then hot again, and the green leaned in close and opened its mouth.

He had burned before. Each time the wrongness in him had won — the heat going out fast, the head coming back clean, faster than the dead men in the wreck, who did not mend at all. Each time he had pressed at the *why* the way he pressed at a print he could not read, and the buzzing where the answer should be, and no word to lay against it, and the shelf holding the asking with everything else and giving nothing back.

He crawled down through his own green to the channel-edge. Too sick to keep his shape. Too sick to be nothing. A man-shape laid plain on the bared stones in the first light, burning, and he did not care, because the green had decided, and a thing the green had decided did not waste its last strength hiding. He drank from the slow brown current and brought it back up. He drank again and kept it. The mud was cool under him and the cool was the last good thing.

The shelf held seven wrongs now. The fever-air was the first, the old wrong, the one he had stopped asking about. But the shelf had never held a thing the green sent twice. The body did not answer him. The body was busy.

---

He did not hear it come.

The first thing was the smell. Not the green-smell, not the rot. Something alive and rank and warm — matted hide and old meat and under it a thing like heated stone, a warmth that had its own weight. The body read it before the head could turn. The small hairs lifted along the arms. The gut clenched. Not the fever-clench. The old clench — the one the body made when a largeness was near.

It stood at the channel-edge. Big — bigger than him by more than double, heavy through the chest and shoulder, the arms long enough to take the ground. Dark hide gone gray-silver along the muzzle and the great ridged back, the gray of a thing that had carried many lows. It crouched, the arms taking the mud, the head low, reading him the way he read the green — slow, taking the whole shape before going down into it.

Not the tribe.

He had watched the tribe from the ridge. He knew their shape — the pack moving with one will, the held line, the heads tipping together. This was one. Alone. The way he was alone. The hide carried scars the tribe's did not — long tears healed crook down the shoulder and across the ribs, and one ear split, hanging in a dark flap against the jaw. The milk-pale eyes sat deep in the gray face. They were not the eyes of a thing that had a pack behind it. They were the eyes of a thing that owned its aloneness the way he owned his — by the tooth and by the keeping.

The old low sound started in his chest. *Hands. Hold. The weight on the neck.* But the fever had burned it thin. He could only lie on the mud and look back, two things on the same stones, neither one moving.

Then it made a sound. Low. Up and down. No edges. The gentling sound — the one he had heard a big one lay on a small one across the green, soft, when the small one was scooped up against the warm. This thing made it at him, at the burning man-shape in the mud, and the sound was rougher than the tribe's, broken in places, as if the throat had forgotten the exact shape of it — or had not used it in a long time.

Then the great head swiveled, reading the green behind it. Listening for the tribe. For the call that ran from the high stones to the lone gleaner. And what it heard — or did not hear — settled it. The head came back. The split ear twitched. And it crossed the bared stones toward him on all fours, slow, low, the head turned sideways to show the neck, the hands open and flat.

He tried to crawl. The last clear thought the body had was the one it carried since the hold, flat and certain: *better the green than the hand. Better eaten than owned.*

And then the warm was on him.

---

Not the closing grip. Not the hand.

It came beside him first. The great body laid itself along his, the matted hide against his bare skin, and the warmth came off in slow waves — not the wet heat of the fever, not the dead warm of rot. A living warm. A warm that breathed. The ribs expanded and contracted against him in a vast slow tide, and the heartbeat went through his shoulder, deep and measured, a drum beaten at the bottom of a well.

He tried to crawl away. The arms came around him.

Panic. Every scar the cage had left fired at once — the grip closing, the weight on the neck, the sound behind the teeth that meant *you are a thing and the thing is mine.* He thrashed. He had nothing to thrash with — the fever had taken the arms and legs — but the body threw what it had against the warm and the warm held. Not tight. Not the closing. The arms folded over him the way a hand folds over a coal — loose enough to breathe, close enough to keep the dark out — and the great head bent down over the top of his skull and made the sound. The gentling. The rough, broken gentling. Over and over against the top of his head, and the warm breath moved through his hair.

He wore the other hand. He had carried its weight his whole life, the slow sure grip on the back of the neck, the hand that closed and owned and did not open. He knew the closing to the ounce. This was not it. This was the hand the king had laid on the dragging one's bad leg — the hand that touched and opened, and the opening was the whole of it. The body knew this one was not it, and the body went slack against the warm, and the breath came out of him in a shaking rush that carried everything he had left.

The far hunger — the hunger no meat had been shaped to feed, the one that had leaned and leaned toward the fire-light and the lifting-up and found nothing to lean on — went still.

It had never gone still. Not once in all the dark on this island. And here, with the great slow heart going through him and the arm folded over him, it lay down in the dark under his ribs and was quiet, and the quiet was so strange and so deep that the body shook harder than the fever ever shook it, and water came up out of his eyes that was not sweat, and he had no word for the thing happening to him. Only the shape of the hole, going full.

---

She carried him. Not slung under, face down, arms bound — the way the cage-hands carried. She scooped him. One arm under the knees, one behind the back, held against the warm of her chest. But the arms shook — not with the weight of him. With the *having* of him, with the shape of a hold the arms had forgotten and were remembering. He felt the tremor in the great muscles, and the body understood it, because the body had the same tremor — the shaking of a thing that had been empty a long time and was full again and did not trust the fullness to last.

She did not go up. Up was the high stones, the tribe, the kept fire, the king, the many. She went the other way. Down and in, deeper into the green where the canopy closed over and the light went thick, past his known ground, into a part of the green too thick and too dark and too wrong to survive in.

She went through it sure-footed, reading the ground, the milk-pale eyes moving. She knew this green. She had been in it a long time, alone, and she had her own paths — paths he had never found because the thing that made them was bigger and went different places. The green that had tried to kill him every day opened for her and let them through.

The swarm ringed him close and did not land. She smelled it — the border where the swarm would not cross — and her nostrils flared, and the great head came down and sniffed along his chest where the disc sat bound at the breastbone, the eye-and-thorn. The milk-pale eyes went wide and then narrow, and she made a short, sharp sound. Recognition. She knew the mark the way the body knew the name — by a warmth that came up slow from the deep — and the body felt her know it and filed the knowing with the bare stone and the king's back and every thing that meant more than it looked, and the shelf took the weight without giving anything back.

---

She had a place. Low. Not the high stones. A hollow under the living green where a great tree had fallen and torn a wound in the canopy and the wound had healed over with vine and leaf. Dry, dark, the floor beaten smooth by a body turning in the same spot for many lows. It smelled of her — the musk, the hide, the heated-stone warmth held in the hollow the way a bowl holds water.

Not a den. A den was a thing you made. This was a thing she wore — shaped to her body the way the rot-hollow was shaped to his. She had been here a long time. Alone. The scars told the story the wall did not — the long tears, the split ear, the crooked-healed gash. Things done to her and walked away from.

She laid him in the hollow and folded in beside him. The heartbeat came through his ear where it lay against the broad chest, and the heartbeat slowed the way the tide pulled off the stones, until it was the deep slow drum at the bottom of everything, and his own heart found it and matched it, and the fever went out of him in the matching.

The body did something it had not done since the hold, since the black water, since the name went out: it rested. Not the shallow rest of the den, where one ear was always up. A deep rest. The rest of a thing set down in a place that holds it, by hands that opened instead of closed.

---

The fever broke on the second low.

He felt it go — the heat climbing out in a single long wave, the head coming back from the wet stone to itself, fast, faster than a thing should mend. She felt it go too. The great arm tightened — not the closing, the checking — and the milk-pale eyes came down to his face and read him.

She had carried up a burning man-shape from the channel-stones, the death that laid everything down, the death that had taken her own small one from the high clean stone. She had carried the man-shape to her hollow the way she had carried the small one and waited for it to go out.

It did not go out.

He climbed back up out of the brown water of himself whole and fast, leaving no mark, and the milk-pale eyes went wide. The surprise of a thing that has tended death a hundred times and sees it refuse to take. And under the surprise — the reading. Deeper. She leaned the great head down and sniffed the disc again, and the recognition sound came again, layered now, thicker. She smelled the mark and she smelled the wrongness — the thing in his blood that the green could not eat — and the two smells were not the same, and she knew they were not the same. She had been in the green alone. She had watched the green eat everything it touched. And she was touching a thing it could not eat.

She made the gentling sound — rough, broken, the throat remembering a shape it had not used in a long time — and the arm tightened over him.

*Mine. This is a kept thing. I keep it.*

---

He did not set it on the shelf.

In the hollow, in the warm, with the far hunger lying still for the first time since the green had swallowed him — he did not file it with the bare stone and the disc and the seven wrongs. It would not fit the shelf. It was too big or too wrong or too *something*, and the something was the thing he could not name, the hole where the knowing should sit, and the knowing not there. Only the shape of the hole. Going full.

He did the old thing. He went in behind the teeth, into the gap where three were torn out, the dark warm hollow where the name his mother had set on him used to live — to find a sound to set against this. To send into the warm the one thing that meant *him.*

The gap was empty. The way it was always empty.

But this time he did not stop. He lay against the great slow heart, and the gentling came down against the top of his head, and he took the sound in through the ear and down into the chest, and he laid it in the gap where the name used to be. Not the name. Not his. But a sound that came down out of a warm that chose to hold him, and the gap took it.

He had no name to send. But he had a sound to keep now, laid in the place the name was torn from, and a warm to keep it for. And the far mouth, full at last, began to count the lights it had, instead of the lights between.

---

She taught him things. Not with words. By doing, and letting him watch, and waiting for the body to find the shape on its own.

She moved through the green differently than he did. He moved the way the cage had taught him: low, tight, the body pulled in, the profile narrow. Minimize the shape. Take up less. Be the thing the green could not find. She moved the way the green itself moved — heavy, sure, the body given to the space. She did not minimize. She *occupied.* The body filled the green the way water fills a channel — touching every wall, pressing every root — and the green that had tried to kill him opened for her and let her through.

She had been of the tribe once — the scars told it, or the ear told it, the split done by a hand big enough to tear a hide like hers, healed alone, not tended. She was not of the tribe now, and the green still opened for her. That told him a thing about the green he had not known: the green did not open for the tribe. The green opened for the *people* in it, and a people was a thing you carried in the blood and the way you moved, and you could take it out of the tribe and it stayed.

He tried her way of moving. The body fought it — every instinct the cage had beaten into the meat said *small, small, small, the hand sees the big thing.* She watched him crouch and crawl and minimize into the mud, and the milk-pale eyes held something he had not seen in them before. Patience. Not the cage-patience — the dead patience of a hand that would close when it chose. The living patience of a thing that had sat by a still small body on a clean stone and waited for the stillness to change. The waiting that was the whole of the love.

He stood up. Not all the way. Not her way. But the body opened a hand's-width from the crouch, and the green that had pressed him flat for all his life gave a hand's-width back, and the body wanted more of it, and the wrongness in him leaned with it — not toward blood, not toward warmth, but toward *the way of the body that does not hide.* And the not-knowing of which reach was his filled the hollow under his ribs with the old confusion, and he set it on the shelf, and the shelf bore down the way it always did.

---

She showed him the water.

The channel. The bared stones. She put her hand in the water, flat, palm up, fingers still, and she waited. The clear-glass things came to her hand. They settled on the palm — drawn to the warmth, the stillness — and she closed the fingers slow and brought them out and ate them alive.

He gleaned by taking. By turning stones and reaching quick. She gleaned by *being.* By putting the warm still hand in the water and letting the living things come to it. The difference was the difference between the cage and the hollow — between the closing and the opening, between the taking and the *receiving.*

He put his hand in the water. The clear-glass things did not come. The hand shook — the wrongness in the blood, the old tremor, the body's memory of the cage-hand that closed. She watched with the living patience, and made the gentling sound, and the hand went still. Not all the way. But still enough. And one clear-glass thing drifted close and settled on the palm, and the wrongness did not push it away.

He closed his fingers slow and brought it out and ate it alive, and the taste was the taste of every clear-glass thing he had ever taken — and it was also not that. It was the taste of a thing given instead of taken. The far hunger stirred once — not toward blood, not toward warmth — toward the *way* of it. The way of the open hand.

And he did not set it on the shelf because the shelf was not where it went. It went in the gap. The gap that was filling with the rough gentling and the slow heartbeat and now the flat salt taste of a thing received instead of taken. The gap was filling with *her*, and the filling was the thing he could not name and did not set down and did not lose.

---

She did not go to the high stones.

He tested it over lows. Every path went *away* from the high stones, down, deeper, into the green the tribe did not walk. When the wind brought the call down from the high fire — *you are still ours, come back up, we have you* — she stopped. The milk-pale eyes went toward the sound the way a head turns toward a name spoken in an empty room. And then she turned away from it, and the turning was not the turning of a thing that had not heard. It was the turning of a thing that had heard and could not answer and had stopped trying.

She was a thing the tribe had set down and walked away from, the way they walked away from the dead — except she was not dead. She was here. The tribe's call did not include her. The tribe's count did not count her. He understood the shape of it — *set apart*, *the one the many walk away from* — because he had worn it in the hold. The body the other bodies shifted away from, the one the cage-hands singled out for the teeth. He had been too small and too wrong and too *something*, and the something had made the others draw off the way the swarm drew off from his skin, leaving a clean width of nothing. She carried the same width. The tribe carried the same width. And between the two widths was the whole story, and the story was in the scars and the split ear and the hollow worn smooth by a body alone.

---

He learned her name.

She made it in the dark. Not the gentling, not the *eat* sound. A sound she made when the hollow was warm and the feeding was done and the two of them lay curved together in the seam worn to her shape. A sound low, almost below the hearing, with a shape in the throat — a shape the throat made when it was saying a thing that meant *one specific body.*

He knew it was a name because the gap behind his teeth knew it. The gap said: *that is a sound that means one body. A sound a body makes when it is not alone.*

*Kala.*

The tongue pressed forward behind the teeth, behind the gap, and the sound opened outward — low and round, no edges. She was not saying *his* name. She was saying *hers.* The sound the tribe had stopped making. The sound a body makes when it has been named, and the naming is the one thing the tribe could not take from her when they took everything else.

He did not say it. He did not have the mouth for it. But the gap took it and set it alongside the name that was already there — the torn-out name, the mother-name — and the two names lay together in the gap like two coals from the same fire, and the warmth did not close, and the warmth did not own, and the warmth did not go out.

---

On the fifth low he woke and she was gone. The warm went out of the wall. The heartbeat was gone. The body came up off the floor fast, every scar firing — *gone, the hand opened and the kept thing is not kept and alone, alone, the old alone —*

She came back. Through the green at the mouth of the hollow, the wide shoulders ducking under the vine-roof. She carried a fat-meat beast across her shoulders and laid it down and made the *eat* sound and began to tear it apart.

She had gone to kill for him and come back. The body read the coming-back the way it read everything — by the shape of the weight in it. The cage-hands did not return. The cage-hands left and came back when they chose and the space between was the nothing, the alone. She returned. The returning had a shape. The hand that pushed the meat toward him was the hand that had held him — the same hand, the same act — and the keeping and the feeding were one thing, and the body knew this because the body wore the other hand, the hand that hit and the hand that fed, and they had been the same hand too. Two hands. Mirror of each other. The body knew the difference the way it knew the difference between the closing and the opening.

By the warmth.

---

The green was outside. The dark was outside. The many were outside, up on the high stones with the kept fire, and the king sat highest, and the dragging one was not cast out, and the old one tended the dead with smoke. All of that was outside, and he was inside the hollow, inside the warm, inside the arm.

And the wrongness — the thing in the blood, the thing the green could not eat — the wrongness was quiet. It did not lean toward the Kala the way it leaned toward the fire. It lay in the dark under his ribs, beside the far hunger, and the two of them were quiet together. The wrongness did not want to eat her. The wrongness did not want to be her. It lay still, and the quiet was the thing he could not set on the shelf and could not name and could not lose.

The quiet of a thing that has been fighting a long time and has found, for the first time, nothing to fight.

He pressed his forehead to the warm hide, and the heartbeat went through his skull, and the gentling came down against the top of his head, and the gap held the two names — the one torn out and the one given — and the lights between did not need counting anymore, because there was only one light, and it was here, and he was inside it.

The green did not get to kill him.

He was kept.
