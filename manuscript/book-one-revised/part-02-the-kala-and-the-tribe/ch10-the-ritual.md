# Chapter 10: The Ritual

The Kala did not sleep.

She lay with her back to the mouth of the hollow, the great body a wall between him and the channel. The split ear twitched at every sound the green made. The meat she had brought lay where she had set it. The clear-glass things lay beside it. The root that bled red when broken lay untouched, its scent sharp and warm and wrong in the way the wrongness liked.

He watched the rise and fall of her ribs. The body that had filled the hollow the way water fills a channel now took less space. The arms were drawn in. The head stayed low. The wrongness under his ribs stayed still, the way it had stayed still before the king's hand.

When the light changed on the high stones, she rose without the gentling sound. She made the *eat* sound once, low, and when he did not move she made it again and then stopped. She went to the mouth of the hollow and stood there until the light was full on the stones above the channel. Then she came back and made the *come* sound.

He followed.

The tribe waited where the channel opened into the wider space between the high stones. The king stood at the center. The pale-rip one stood at his right shoulder. The dragging one stood at his left, the young one beside him. The rest formed a half-circle, heads tipped one way, hands still. The fire they tended burned higher than the one in the hollow, fat-fed and steady, the smoke rising straight.

The king made the call sound. Not the one that had come down the channel. A different shape. Rounder. The one that said the many were here and the count was whole and the thing that had been missing was no longer missing.

The Kala answered with nothing. She walked to the edge of the half-circle and stopped. The body between him and the king was the answer. The arms spread. The head low.

The king stepped forward. One step. The pale-rip one matched it. The dragging one did not move. The young one looked at the Kala and then at the king and the look was the look of a thing that had seen two calls answered by the same body and had begun to choose which answer it would keep.

The king spoke the sound that meant *the many are waiting*. The sound that meant *the count is wrong without you*. The sound that meant *the call is for you*.

The Kala made the other sound. The one the body made when the closing was certain.

The king stopped. The hand that had opened the dragging one's leg lifted, palm up. The same offer. The hand that touched and did not close.

The wrongness under his ribs pressed against the inside of his chest. Not the lean toward blood. Not the reach toward warmth. A new thing. The shape of the question he could not ask. Which call belonged to which body. Which answer the Kala would keep.

The king spoke again. The sound was smaller. The call without the weight of the many behind it. Just the sound a body makes when it has been set apart and has stopped trying to be counted.

The Kala answered with the gentling sound. Rough. Broken in places. She made it at the king.

The king lowered his hand. The pale-rip one did not. The young one stepped forward once and the dragging one put a hand on the young one's shoulder and the young one stopped.

They took him to the high stones.

The ritual place was a circle of stone between the two highest ridges, the fire at the center, the bones of old fires black in the ash. The tribe stood in the circle. The king stood at the north. The pale-rip one stood at the south. The dragging one stood at the east. The young one stood at the west. The Kala stood beside him, the body between him and the fire.

The king made the sound that meant *the many are here*. The sound that meant *the count is whole*. The sound that meant *the thing that was lost is found*.

The tribe answered with the call sound. All of them at once. The sound ran around the circle and came back to the king and the king made it again and the tribe answered again and the sound became one sound, the many voices making one voice.

The Kala did not answer.

The king stepped into the circle. He carried a thing made of bone and cord and the dark wood that drank blood. He laid it at the king's feet. The pale-rip one stepped into the circle. He carried a thing made of stone and the clear-glass things and the root that bled red. He laid it at the king's feet. The dragging one stepped into the circle. He carried a thing made of hide and the meat that had been cooked over the fat-fed fire. He laid it at the king's feet. The young one stepped into the circle. He carried nothing. He stood with his hands empty and the look on his face was the look of a thing that had seen two calls and had chosen.

The king made the sound that meant *the many offer*. The sound that meant *the count is made whole by the offering*. The sound that meant *the thing that was lost is offered back to the many*.

The Kala made the sound that meant *the closing is certain*.

The wrongness under his ribs went still. Not recognition. Not fear. The old blood knowing something the head had no words for. The thing that had gone still before the apex print. The thing that had gone still before the king's hand.

The king stepped forward. The hand that had opened the dragging one's leg lifted. The palm up. The fingers still. The offer.

The Kala made the sound again. Lower. The one the body made when the closing was certain and the body had decided which call it would keep.

The king stopped. The hand lowered. The pale-rip one moved. The dragging one moved. The young one did not move.

The wrongness under his ribs came up off the floor of him. Not the lean toward blood. Not the reach toward warmth. The new thing. The shape of the question. Which call belonged to which body. Which answer the Kala would keep.

The Kala turned. The split ear twitched. The milk-pale eyes went from the king's hand to the young one and back to the king. The body between the hollow and the channel did not move, but the weight of it changed. The wrongness under his ribs went still the way it had gone still before the apex print — recognition, not fear, the old blood knowing something the head had no words for.

The king spoke a third time. The sound was smaller. The call without the weight of the many behind it. Just the sound a body makes when it has been set apart and has stopped trying to be counted.

The Kala answered with the gentling sound. Rough. Broken in places. The one she made against the top of his head when the fever took him. She made it at the king.

The king lowered his hand. The pale-rip one did not. The young one stepped forward once and the dragging one put a hand on the young one's shoulder and the young one stopped.

The king turned. The pale-rip one followed. The dragging one followed. The young one looked back once at the hollow and the look was the look of a thing that had seen two calls answered by the same body and did not know which answer was the true one. Then the young one turned and followed.

The Kala stood at the mouth of the hollow until the last of them had gone back up the channel. The body did not relax. The arms did not come down. The head did not lift from the low reading posture. The wrongness under his ribs stayed still, and the stillness was the thing he could not set on the shelf.

She came back to him when the light changed on the high stones. She laid the meat down. She made the *eat* sound. But the body that had occupied the green the way water fills a channel now moved the way he moved — low, tight, the profile narrow. The green that had opened for her closed a hand's-width around the hollow, and the closing was the whole of what the tribe had taken.

The gap behind his teeth held two names and the sound of the call that had not been answered.

The far hunger began to count the lights between again, the way a man counts the spaces where a fire had been.

The green had decided after all.
