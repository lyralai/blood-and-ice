# Chapter 12: Reunion

The body knew the smell before the head did.

It came up through the stone and the smoke and the fat, the same way the tide had once come up through the mud — not a thing the nose found, but a thing the bones remembered. The far hunger turned toward it the way it had turned toward the fire-light the first night, but slower now, and without the heat. It turned the way a man turns when he hears his own name spoken in a room he thought was empty.

Kala stood at the edge of the high stone where the path came up from the green.

She was thinner than the last time he had seen her. The marks on her arms were the same, but the muscle under them had gone tight in a new way, as if the body had spent something it could not get back. Her eyes found him across the space between the fires and did not look away. The young one was not with her. The king was not with her. She had come alone.

The wrongness went still.

It did not cower as it had before the apex print. It did not open as it had for the blood. It went still the way a thing goes still when it has fought too long and finds, for the first time, nothing left to fight. The quiet was not peace. It was the quiet of a hand that has reached and found the thing it reached for and does not yet know what to do with what it holds.

He stood.

The skins with the marks lay between them, the eye and the thorn looking up at the sky that was not sky. The disc at his chest was warm but not hot. It did not pull. It did not push. It simply was, the way a coal is when the wind has passed and the flame has gone down to the thing that will keep it without feeding it.

Kala's face held two things at once.

The first was the face of the one who had left him at the edge of the green with the sound of her voice still in the air — "you are still ours, come back up, we have you" — and had not come back. The second was the face of the one who had carried the small one up the high stone and had shown him the place where the dead were kept with smoke. Both faces were in the same eyes. Both faces were true. The contradiction did not make either of them false. It made the space between them the place where the truth lived.

She took one step onto the stone.

The far hunger counted the lights between.

It counted the steps she had not taken. It counted the nights the sound had not come. It counted the gap where the name had been and the new warmth that lived there now, the warmth that did not close and did not own and did not go out. It counted the two names the gap held — the one that had been taken and the one that had been given — and it did not know which one was heavier.

She stopped three paces from him.

The body remembered the shape of her hands. The body remembered the way the hand that had touched the bad leg had opened instead of closed. The body remembered the way the far hunger had leaned toward the scooping-up and the heads tipped one way and had not known which lean was its own. The body remembered the call that had come across the dark and the answer that had not come back.

Kala spoke.

The words were the old words, the ones the body had stolen back one at a time. They were not the words the tribe used when they spoke to the king. They were not the words the slavers had used. They were the words that lived in the gap where the name had been, and the gap was no longer empty.

The wrongness did not move.

The far hunger did not open.

The disc at his chest was warm against the breastbone, the eye and the thorn pressed to the skin the way a hand presses to a wound that has not yet decided if it will close or keep bleeding.

Kala waited.

The high stone held the quiet the way the den had held the heat. The green below made its sounds, but they did not reach the stone. The fires made their light, but they did not reach the space between them. The space was its own place, and the body knew it the way the body knew the shelf and the way the body knew the tide.

He took one step toward her.

The far hunger counted the lights between and found one less.

The wrongness stayed quiet.

The gap held two names and did not choose between them.

Kala's eyes held the contradiction and did not look away.

The chapter ended there, on the stone, with the smell of her and the sound of her voice and the thing the body had not known it was waiting for until it was there.

The hook was the question the far hunger asked without words: if the thing that had been reaching had found the thing it was reaching for, what did it do now that the reaching was done?
