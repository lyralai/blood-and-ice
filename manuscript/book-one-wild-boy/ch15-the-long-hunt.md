# Chapter 15: The Long Hunt

You did not hunt the thing that ate you with the last of yourself. You hunted it with the second-to-last, and kept the last back for the dying you did not mean to do.

That was the first law Rook laid down, and it was not a brave law. The brave ones went strength to strength against the Hesh and were rolled and held and not given back. He was the smallest thing in the green. He did not go up against the Hesh. He went up against the *ground* it stood on, and he kept the last of himself banked low under the ribs, for the dying he would spend it on if the reading was wrong.

So he went down. Down off the clean shelf in the thick fever-light before the sun, into the steaming dark under the drowned giants, into the rot-thicket where the long body dragged its weight. There he began the hunt that had no blow in it. The reading-hunt. The only one the weak thing had.

---

For three lows he did not throw a thing or sharpen a thing. He only learned the Hesh. He would not go up against the apex of his green knowing nothing and bleed for it blind, the way he had bled for everything else.

He lay still in the warm soup at the channel-edge, downwind, a stone that did not move. The make-yourself-nothing the hands had beaten into him in the dark, turned now to the watching. The Hesh did not find him. The high eyes passed over the place where the nothing was, because a thing that had never had to hurry from anything had never learned to look for the still small thing in the rot. That not-looking was his whole standing against it.

He read where it lay up in the heat of the low, sunk to the eyes in the rot-pool. He read the firmer mat it dragged its weight along, because even a thing that feared nothing chose ground that held. And he read the one thing the body would not let go: the Hesh was built for the water and the soft edge. It did not climb. It had stopped where the firm ground began and lifted its head after the old male and not followed, because the long weight that made it the strongest thing in the green was the one thing the high stone had no use for. The strength was its death on the rock. The old male's strength had been his death in the rot. Strong was only ever right ground and wrong ground, and the wrong ground was where he would stand it.

Every strong thing in the green was roped to the one ground its strength could stand on, and the rope was what he would pull.

On the third low he found the place. There was a run the Hesh dragged itself along between the rot-pool and the deep throat of the thicket, the one line of held ground through a soup that would take a heavy thing down. And a body's-length off it, the rot kept its hidden throat — the dark vein of soup under root and leaf that had taken the old male to the chest, that read weight and thrashing and pulled and did not give back.

The Hesh kept off that throat. It read the ground as Rook read it; it was the apex because nothing had ever taught it not to be. It would not set the long weight down where the ground was a lie.

So he would move the lie a foot-width over. The lie where the truth was, the truth where the lie was — and the apex would trust the ground it had always trusted, and the rot would take it.

---

He did not have a word for the thing he was building. He had all the weather of it, and the weather was the noose.

The loop was for the neck of a thing that ran, and the Hesh did not run. But a noose was only the small shape of a larger law: a weight over a high seat, a long pull, and the thing too heavy to lift came up. He had made the green the arm when he brought the old male out of the rot's throat. He would make it the arm again — only this low it would not pull the strong out of the killing ground. It would pull the killing ground out from under the strong.

And this low he was not alone on the firm run. Marrow came down off the high shelf at his back, rocking on the crooked gait, the high shoulder riding, and squatted in the soup beside him, the curled hand tucked against his chest, the quick eyes already working the bank before Rook had shown him a thing. He had been made wrong where Rook was made wrong, and the wrong-made learned early to read what the strong never had to. Rook gave him the work in gesture — the run, the throat, the moved line — and he gave it back faster than Rook had given it, his good hand sketching the weight coming and the floor going and the small thing light off the floor, *yes, this, and then this.* A thing turned over in Rook that had no place on the shelf with the wrong things. It was good. It was only good. The smallest two things in the green, laying a death for the largest, and one of them grinning his broken grin sidelong in the rot-light. Rook was grinning it back before he knew he had a face for it.

They worked it three lows more, in the heat when the Hesh lay sunk and the eyes were down. They crawled the firm run downwind, beside the thing that named the bank its own, and Rook kept his name behind the gap where the three teeth were gone, biting it when the body wanted to bolt. Marrow did not bite a name. He hummed, low and tuneless under the breath, *I am here, the small one is still here,* and Rook learned to hunt to the sound of it.

They cut the woody runners thick as his arm and wove them across the deep throat, root to root, and covered the weave with held root peeled off the firm run, so the lie read firm. Along the true run they worked the held root loose at its under-veins, the green skin whole on top so the truth read like the lie. A foot-width over. Where the ground held, they made it a throat. Where the throat was, a green floor that would take a long weight one breath and then let it through.

It was slow work and wet work and it cost. A spined leaf in the rot-mat drove its fire up under the skin of his forearm. The arm swelled and went hot and the fire climbed to the shoulder, and he lay still and waited for it to drop him as it would have dropped the old male. It did not drop him. The swelling went down the way a tide went down — fast, leaving the skin slack and wrong, the veins standing blue under the surface like something had drunk the fire and left the husk. The clear-headed came back too quick, the fever-air loud in the ears, and for one breath the green tilted under him, the rot-throat yawning where the poison had been. He put his face to the held root and waited for the ground to find him again. Marrow watched the arm un-swell and did not come closer.

He wanted to know what he was. He pressed at it like you pressed a swelling to find the bone, and there was no bone, only the buzz — the wounds that drew shut, the swarm that rang off his skin, the venom that slid where it should knot and kill. He reached for the why and it had no edges to take hold of. It came apart in the hands before he could lift it. He had no word for it, and no one to ask, and the fever-air was loud, and the Hesh was on its run. He set the wanting down because the work would not wait, not because he was told not to want it. But he wanted it. He wanted it the whole time.

---

A trap was only the half of it. The other half was the bringing. They had to make the Hesh come the moved line, and come it fast, in the rush of its own strength, because a thing read the ground slow and forgot to read it in the rush.

So Rook had to be the thing it rushed. The bait. The moving thing that woke the high eyes. There was a way that was not strength, and the weak thing had it. He told Marrow with the good hand and the gap-toothed sounds — *up the leaning giant, hold the high seat, when he was off the floor, pull and do not stop* — and the high shoulder dropped and he would not look at him, and Rook read off the dropped shoulder the thing he would not make a sound for: he would rather stand the floor himself than hold the rope. The small one did not like the big one being meat. Rook made him go up the giant anyway. One of them light on the floor, one holding the line. The law of two small things against one large one.

---

He did it in the thick light before the sun of the next low, when the Hesh came off its pool, the high eyes up, the long body sliding its weight along the firm run toward the deep throat.

He showed himself. He stood up out of the not-looking on the green floor over the moved line, the small warm moving thing where the high eyes could find it at last — and they found it. The broad flat head came round, the eyes fixed, and the long body that had never once had cause to hurry hurried, because here was meat on the bank the Hesh had named its own. It came off the firm run onto the line Rook had moved, fast, the rows of teeth already wide.

Then the green opened wrong.

Voss-Marr was on the bank. He had trailed the wrong-blood down off the high stone to see the small thing fail — and he was on the *old* firm run, the line that had been true three lows ago and was a lie now. His weight came down where Rook had worked the under-veins loose. He read the ground as the Hesh read it, by the green skin whole on top, and the skin held him one breath — one — and then his foot went through and he was down to the thigh in a thing that read weight and pulled. And the long body was coming.

There was no deciding in it. The body decided before the head could say it, older than fear and lower down. Rook was light, and he was close, and he went to him instead of off the floor. He did not pull him up; the soup had read him, and a pull only gave it the thrashing to read deeper. He went flat on the held root at the floor's edge — the true root, the one held line in all that worked ground — and put it under his hand and made the sound that was not a word, the *stop,* the still-sound the Kala made over him. *Be still. The still is the holding.* And the proud heir, the strongest thing of his season, went still on the wrong-blood's say-so with the apex one body-length off and closing, because the body knew true ground when a smaller thing put it under his hand. The soup lost the thrashing to read. He held the root and did not go down, and the Hesh came on.

It came on, onto the line Rook had moved, because the meat was still on the floor — he was flat on its edge with his hand on Voss-Marr's wrist. Now he went. He rolled off the green floor the breath before the weight came down, foot on the true root and gone, a thing too light to pull. The Hesh set the heavy body down where he had stood, in the rush, and the skin held it one breath — one — and then the rot read the weight and pulled, and the lie opened under the long body and let it through into the soup that did not give back what it swallowed.

---

It went down fighting, the way the strong always fought, the fight that took them deeper. The great body beat the soup to froth, the teeth snapping on rot and on nothing, and the more the strength fought the more the rot read it and pulled. A law strength made worse. Up on the leaning giant Marrow took his turn of the laced vine over the high stub and pulled, the curled hand and the good hand both, the whole small wrong body hung on the rope. He did not pull to lift the Hesh out. He pulled the other way, holding the green skin closed hard over the throat, so the long body could not find firm root to drag itself up. Rook was the eye. Marrow was the arm. The rot was the mouth. They stood the apex where the three met.

Voss-Marr dragged himself out onto true ground a body-length off and did not run. He stood streaming and watched the apex die, watched the two smallest things in the green hold it under, and the air around him changed. It was not contempt this low. It was the thing that came before respect, near enough to it that the body could not tell them apart yet — a strong thing seeing a strength it had no name for, and a hand that had reached for him in the soup when the easy thing was to let him go down and be rid of the wrong-blood. He looked at Rook and did not look away. For one breath they were only two small things and one large thing dying, on the same side of the dying, and the heir bowed the great young head the bare degree the strong bowed to the strong. Rook had a brother for a breath. He would remember that it was only a breath.

It was a long killing, and they held the weave, and the line did not break. The far mouth came back at last out of its struck silence as the froth slowed and the high eyes stilled and the long body went to the deep stillness. He knew that stillness. He had been it, on a deck, in a hold, with the hand closed on the back of the neck. He felt it come off the apex into him, and the thing low under the ribs stood all the way up, fed nothing, leaning at the strength gone still. He made himself stop the leaning short. He did not know what he would become if he fed it.

---

He did not carry the whole of it. It was longer than the old male and him laid end to end, and the soup kept the most of it. But he took the broad flat head. He cut it free with the dead expedition's knife and slung it in the laced vine. Marrow took a corner of the vine on the high shoulder, and they began the dragging-home, which cost more than the hunt. Voss-Marr did not take a corner. He was the heir, not made to carry. But he climbed ahead of them slow and did not climb out of sight, and that was its own kind of carrying from a thing like him.

They came up over the broken lip in the dark, shaking, the rot drying stiff and pale on them, and the tribe was crowded frightened on the high stone, the gladness gone out of it, the small ones pressed close.

And Rook laid the head of the Hesh down on the clean stone in the middle of them.

The whole tribe read it. The broad flat head, the rows of teeth, the high eyes that had named the bank their own — laid down dead by the smallest, softest, wrongest thing among them. The pressed-down fear broke open. The old male with the torn ear came and stood over the dead head and over Rook and made the sound a strong thing made to the thing that had stood between it and the squared dead — low, slow, the great head bowed. The half-grown ones circled the head and the small scarred thing that had carried it. The bold small one rode his shoulder at last, the keeping-place become the thing that kept. Marrow stood at the edge on his crooked gait and let them have Rook, the broken grin gone careful, because the runt read what the tribe was reading before the tribe did.

The Kala folded down beside him, the great gray-silver bulk, and laid the keeping arm over him, and made the still-sound whole this low — *be still, you are held, nothing comes.* And the *nothing comes* did not die half-made in the great chest as it had died when the Hesh had come, because the thing that had come had gone into the soup the small wrong thing she had chosen laid the green down on. He made the sound back, low and torn, scraping over the gap where the three teeth were gone.

For the king was on his broken lip. Gull-Marr stood turned in now, not out into the dark where there was no longer a thing to turn toward — turned to the dead head of the apex, to the small scarred thing the tribe circled. The flat eyes did not warm. They went narrow, the weighing look, no letting-stand, the *could* and not the *is*. The strength-king had met the apex, found his strength too short, turned away, and called it the law. The weak thing had gone up into the dark and brought the apex down. Every eye on the high stone had read which of them did it.

And lower down, at the king's lip but not on it, Voss-Marr stood streaming and silent, and the air around him was going wrong again. The breath of brother in the soup was curdling in the high clean light, here where the whole tribe could see whose head lay on the stone and whose hand it was not. He had bowed in the dark when it was only the two of them and the dying. Up here it was the crown — and the crown had been poured into the world for him, and the wrong-blood pulled him out of the soup and then laid the apex's head down where the king must read it. The grudging-toward-respect was going back to a thing with teeth. He could not forgive the hand that saved him, because it was the hand taking what was his. Rook had a brother for a breath and a rival for the rest of the lows there were.

---

He lay in the warm in the dark, the small one's quick heart at his chest and the Kala's slow vast tide under him, and he laid the new law down behind his teeth, in the gap where the name lay:

*A thing too strong to be fought was not too strong to be read, and the law of the green would bend to the thing that read the ground the strength stood on.*

Not because he was strong. Because he was light, and he read the ground, and he had made the killing ground itself the thing that killed. The high stones were easy in the dark for the first time since the green went quiet, the fear gone off them. Marrow slept near, the rocking gait gone slack, the curled hand open.

But the high stone had only one thing on it now stronger than the thing he had killed, and a second that had bowed to him in the soup and would not bow again. The king stood on the broken lip, the narrow eyes turned in on him. Below him the heir stood with the rot drying on him and the breath of brother already gone sour. He had made the both of them at once — the one by being light, the other by being there with a true root when Voss-Marr went down.

The seer came across the crowded rock before the light, the ash-pale head slow, and folded down at the lip where he lay. The pale finger touched the Eye-and-Thorn bound at his chest and made the long meaning-sound, the *means.* Then it lifted and turned — not out into the empty dark this low, but up, toward the broken lip where the king stood — and made the sound again. *This one. And that one. And the two are one telling, and the telling is not done.*

Rook did not sleep. He lay in the warm with the near one easy and the dead head cooling and the king weighing, and over all of it he laid the next going-up, the one the seer had pointed at. The body kept it. It had learned to keep a thing either way it went.
