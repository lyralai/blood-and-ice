# Book Two, Chapter 31: The Cliffhanger

The quiet was the first thing that went wrong.

Three darks after the walls held, the boy stood on the rim with his palm flat on the warm stone and could not find the enemy anywhere. That was the finding, and the body held it before the count could dress it: the palm wanted a border. The palm had learned, in these last darks, to expect the thin leaning cold under the stone the way the tongue expects the broken tooth, and the stone gave nothing. Gallery six, quiet. Gallery five, quiet. The seventh, the ninth, the fourth turning where the standing white had made its stand — quiet, all of it, all at once, the whole length of the city gone still between one dark and the next like a held breath nine tiers deep.

*Findings,* he entered, in the flat voice, on the rim, alone, the tallow down to its last thumb behind him. *The white is not ended. I did not end it. I ended its standing at the fourth turning, and it has answered by not standing anywhere. Absence, entered. The count has no column for a thing that goes away holding what it came for.*

He stood a long time with his hand on the stone. Below him the city slept its new sleep, the sleep of a city that had watched its walls hold, and the sleeping had a lean in it, the same lean that had come into the tiers after the seventh gallery — heavier now. The story had gone down the stair again in the three darks, and it had gone down changed the way all stories went down changed, and what it had become this time was already in the walls around him, in the way the freed-but-owing had stopped pressing back to let the count pass and had begun turning to face the stair when he came, all of them, whole galleries of faces, the way a field turns under wind.

*The carrier is the wall,* the story said now, down at the fifth. Down at the third, where the tallow was thinnest, he had heard it said plainer: *the wall lives while he lives.*

*Findings,* he entered. *They leaned on the count. Now they lean on the boy. A wall does not care what leans on it. Entered apart, because the boy does.*

His arm he priced at the same gray light, sleeve back, palm down, the wall-reading turned on himself. The border ran past the elbow now, cold into new country, and the new country did not warm under the pressed hand, and it did not move. That was entered too, and re-entered, because the count's law was the count's law: what is, spoken plain. The cold had stopped advancing. It held what it held at exactly the line the burn had left, as if it had been priced, and had agreed to the price, and had kept a copy of the terms.

That was the shape of the quiet, and he made himself hold the whole shape before he moved: the Cold had not been beaten anywhere. It had gone to its books. And in the third gallery, at the fourth turning, where the standing white had gone down last, its one line of white still lay in the stone, thin, deliberate, facing up — his own mark, cut shallow, stopping in the middle of the line — and it had not ended under the blood, and it did not end under the days.

He went down to it once, on the second dark, alone, against Marrow's counted advice. He stood over the stroke with a tallow lamp in his hand and read it the way he read all marks, and the reading had not changed, and the not-changing was the change: a mark keeps. A mark kept is a mark meaning to be read again, by the hand that made it or another. Something down there had left his mark lying in the floor like a promissory note laid in a ledger-line, and had withdrawn with the whole of its deep white, unhurried, unspent, taking nothing but its reading of him — the warm signature it had walked away with, the rate of his holding, the row-law, the ladle, the arm's priced length. It had come up to learn what held the walls. It had learned. And it had gone down to count what it had learned, and counting, it did not need to stand.

*It keeps books now,* he entered, at the rim, at the gray light. *I taught it keeping at the fourth turning, in front of forty lamps. The count is no longer the only thing in this city that writes. Entered plain: what the city taught the Cold, the Cold will teach the deep.*

---

It was Kade who brought the husks, on the fifth dark, up the rim stair at a pace the boy had never seen the man use. Kade, whose watch had been the deep stair, whose wrapped hands were always loose, came up the last landing with his shoulders set wrong and his bright eyes aimed and full, and stood, and could not at first make the words sit.

"Carrier." The wrapped hands worked. "Beyond the flats. Past the rim-stones, out where the tide-farms were. The watch counted them at the gray light and I counted them again and I have come up because the count is wrong in a way I do not have words for, and you taught me that when the count is wrong in a way with no words, it goes to you."

"How many."

"Two hundred and more. Neat." The word came out of him like a stone. "Bloodless. No wound, no rot, no mark. Kneeling in the tide-mud. Rows of them, carrier." And there it was, the thing the man had climbed eleven landings to say, and the saying of it dropped his voice to almost nothing: "Rows. Kneeling in rows, spaced the way — " the wrapped hands made a shape in the air, a spacing, a standing — "the way the freed stood at the fourth turning. The watch wants to know if it is a gathering. I told them gathering is the wrong word. It is not gathering. It is an entry."

The boy went still. Not the count's stillness. The older kind, the den-kind, the body's own.

Rows. Spaced like rows. The Cold had come up through a floor and been met by a city in rows — two deep at the fourth turning, shoulder-set, held by a law spoken down their lengths — and it had gone down, and now, out past the rim, past the tide-farms, two hundred of the bloodless knelt in the mud in the same spacing, and the spacing was not chance. Nothing about the Cold was chance. It did not hate, and it did not copy, because copying was a wanting and the Cold wanted nothing — but it *kept,* and what it kept it laid out in the shapes it had read, and the shape it had read on the third gallery was the shape of the thing that had held it.

The city had taught it to hold. Now it practiced.

*Findings,* he entered, and the hand held level, and the levelness was bought, and he entered the price of it too. *The husks at the rim, multiplied. Rows. Not an army. Armies face. These kneel facing the city. They are not a siege. They are a keeping. The Cold is keeping two hundred at the rim the way it kept the watchman at the arch — held, unspent, without being used — and it has set them in my spacing, because the spacing is the strongest shape it read. Horror, entered plain: it is not building a wall against me. It is building my wall against me.*

That was the finding he took to the floor that dark, and the floor was fuller than any floor the count had ever held. Maren with her slates open. Marrow. The under-priest, who had not sat since the ladle. Brannoc and Lorren. Asha-kel at the stair-mouth, sent up again, and behind her the houses' patience, which had changed its smell since the leash was declined. Kade at the wall. And the Red, behind the door, with its balanced books, saying nothing, warm along the wood like a hand laid flat.

The council priced the husks. There was no price. The blood ended the Cold; the blood did not end the kept; two drops, twenty drops, the kneeling two hundred would kneel, alive, held, gray-faced, breathing their long unasked breath at the rim of a city that could not feed them and could not free them and could not look away from them. The watch had already begun to break along the seam Kade had climbed: men who stood the rim-stones all dark looking down at faces that breathed.

"The houses," Asha-kel said, in the caller's flat way, "have read the flats. The houses enter that a thing which kneels at a wall in the wall's own spacing is a claim, and the houses further enter that the claim runs to the carrier, and the houses' answer is entered and waiting: the rite. Contained, witnessed, lawful. The houses in the row. The leash is not declined at the new price, carrier. It is priced."

The boy looked at her a long moment. There it was, the turn he had been counting on since the sealed clay went back: the houses, who had sent a leash, now asked for a row. Not because they had learned the wall-law. Because they had read the flats the way they read everything — as a claim on the asset — and the asset had to be hedged, and the only hedge left standing in Vgrael was the count. They would kneel in a row of his making to buy a share of the thing the Cold was building against him. Pride, fear, and arithmetic in the same flat face, and he could not even enter the pride as false, because it was not. They were afraid. The houses of Vgrael were afraid in the dark like everyone else, and their fear came to him dressed as terms.

"Entered," the boy said. "The houses stand the row at the next holding, at the price of every row: entered, owing, counted. Tell them that is not a hedge. It is a rung. Tell them exactly that."

And Brannoc's blind face came up off the floor, and the old hands made the asking-gesture, and the boy gave the forearm, and the fingers read the cold border past the elbow a long time in the tallow light, and what the old man said then went into the count's clay and stayed there:

"It has stopped moving," Brannoc said. "Hear the rest, boy, hear it the way I hear it, through what the air does. Nine darks the border climbed at the rate of the holding. Now it holds its line. I have read stone all my life and I will tell you what a stopped border is called in stone." The fingers lay across the new country light as a stylus set down between entries. "A joint. Where two things meet and agree to bear the weight together. It is not a wound anymore, and I do not know that it is a wall. It may be a seam. Enter it plain, boy, and enter the question: the mark in the floor stops where the carrier stops holding. Where does the border in the arm stop?"

The council was quiet. Into the quiet, for the first time in six darks, the Red spoke — warm, level, exact, the ledger-voice, and beneath the level something that had been balanced and was now, very slightly, leaning again:

*Price the flats, boy,* it said. *Not the husks. The flats. Two hundred kneeling, kept, unspent, set in your spacing at the rim of a city whose tithe is failing and whose carrier is one arm. It is not gathering and it is not a claim. It is a dock. It is building a place for what it means to bring. Everything the Cold does is preparation; it has no other dark. It came up and read one holding and went down and began to build the shape of the thing that held it, at scale, outside your walls, in the mud, where your blood has never been. Ask what it prepares to keep in two hundred.* The door creaked, once, the warmth settling along it like a body settling along a wall. *I have run it. The column balances three ways, and all three end the same place: it is coming for the city to get to you. Not soon. Prepared. The difference between the Cold and everything else in your ledger, boy, is that everything else in your ledger wanted your blood. It wants your keeping. You are the only thing it has ever read twice.*

The boy entered all of it. What is, spoken plain. The Red's reading was a finding too, and the Red's readings had been true since the seventh gallery — truer every dark, and the truer they were the flatter they came, and the flatter they came the more the boy counted the flatness. A Red that stood in rows. A Red whose columns balanced. A Red content. He had priced its hunger since the door first stood, and he had no entry yet for its satisfaction, and he entered that as well, at the bottom, in the count's own hand, where no other hand in Vgrael would write it:

*The Red stood in a row and the row held it. Entered: I do not know what a balanced Red is building either. Everything in this city is preparing. I am counting preparations.*

---

The lanterns came in on the ninth dark, and the world came with them.

He saw them first at the gray light, from the rim, where he stood every gray light now with his hand on the stone and his eyes on the water, because the lanterns of the first gray light had not gone out. They had stood far off on the horizon through eight darks, patient the way the quiet was patient, and the count had held them the way it held everything, at distance, entered: *lights where no lights ever stood. Not fishing. Not storm-borne. Holding station. Waiting for what watches them to decide.*

On the ninth dark they decided. He was at the rim when the first hull came through the tide-gate at the flats — a longboat, oars doubled, running lights of a pattern no one on the rim had ever seen, and behind it, out on the water, the standing lanterns began to move.

The longboat grounded on the flats below the husks.

That was the arrangement, and the count took the arrangement whole before the head had dressed any of it: the embassy had grounded itself below the kneeling two hundred, in front of them, in the mud, between the city and its kept — a courtesy, or a reading, or a claim laid at the foot of the Cold's own dock. And the boat's build — the boy read the build the way the body read everything — whole shape first, price after — the build was wrong. Tarred seams. A beam in her that spoke of a hold built for stacking. The oar-spacing of a crew that rowed other men.

Slaver-built. His body knew that build before his name had. His body had crossed the water in the belly of that build, and the belly had a smell, and the smell was in the tide-wind off the flats now, tar and old sickness and the iron of chain-lockers, and the gap behind his teeth where the three were gone went cold, and the old wanting — out, out, the animal wanting under the ledger, the wanting from before the count had a name — came up off the floor of him so fast his hands were on the rim-stones before the head knew they had moved.

He made the hands open. Entered the wanting, plain, aloud, alone on the rim in the gray light: *The boy wants the door. The boy has wanted the door since the water. Entered apart. The boat is not the cage. Price the boat.*

They brought the herald up the stair, and the herald stood on the count's floor — a small dry man of a people from across the water, dressed in the plain wool of a trade-tongue factor, with a scribe's inkless hands and a speaker's careful stillness — and the herald looked at the boy, at the sleeve over the arm, at the freed standing their rows along the gallery walls, and did not kneel, and did not lean, and spoke the trade-tongue slow enough that the count could take every word.

"The lights you have watched," the herald said, "are the Cordon of Andavar. My masters do not come to buy and do not come to bleed. My masters come because a thing has crossed the water." A pause, measured, a speaker's pause. "Word moves on the water, carrier. There is a city on this rock that fed itself to its own fear for nine hundred years, and this year the fear was ended in a night by a blood the likes of which the Cordon's books have no entry for. There is a pit under this city that the books do enter — a pit that answers." The herald's stillness held, and in it, for one breath, the boy read the man's own dread, kept under a factor's wool the way the freed kept their fear under the wall-law: the whole water was afraid of the same story. "And there is a louder word under that one. There is a king across the water — not my masters' king — gathering the old harvest into new hulls. The broken tribes of the sea-rock fly his marks now, all but the ones that will not bow, and the ones that will not bow are hunted, and their hunting has made him strong. The water says his fleet is for the harvest run the water has always known." The herald's eyes went once, deliberately, to the sleeve. "The water also says his fleet is not only for harvest. The water says the pit-city is on his map. My masters price information, carrier. That one they gave free, so that you would know what the free ones cost."

The trade-tongue had a word for the king across the water, and the herald said it, and the boy did not know the word, but Marrow — Marrow, at his shoulder, one-spent-one-owed — went still in the old way, the den-way, and the curled hand found the boy's sleeve, and the runt said, in the tribe's old sound-speech, low, for him alone, the name as the island said it:

*Voss-Marr.*

The name went in. Not into the ledger. Into the older place, the shelf at the back of himself, where the green kept its wrong things — into the room where a high seat stood on a jungle ridge and a hand with a cracked nail had once held out mercy and been refused, and a rival had walked away alive because a boy who had not yet learned the price of anything had let him walk. The war born from a mercy. It had been out there all this time, gathering hulls the way the Cold gathered knees, and it had heard the same story the water told — the blood that woke in the pit-city, the pit that answers with laughter — and it was coming to see.

The boy stood the herald before the rows and gave him the count's answer, flat, carrying: Vgrael was not bought, not bled, not leaned on. The Cordon's lights could stand at the water and be counted. Everything else was terms, and terms were written, and the writing was witnessed, and the witnessing was law. The herald wrote it in his own book with his inkless-looking hands, and was fed, and was rowed back down, and the whole city watched the longboat go out past the kneeling two hundred, and the lanterns on the water stood.

That dark, the boy went down to the sump, alone, and stood on the grating over the black water where the stone carried sound, and asked Grist through the floor, in the vibration the Skell read, what the Skell read in the hulls.

The answer came up through his feet a long time forming, and when it came it came in the Skell's few shapes, and the shapes were old, and the boy stood on the grating in the cold and made himself hold them all: *Those hulls came to the sea-rock before. The grist remembers the build. The grist remembers the stacking.* And then, slower, the floor-voice shaping a thing it had never shaped for him before — a question, turned all the way around, the leavings asking the counter what the counter meant to do: *The one who holds the broken Vorth hunts the ones that will not bow. The Seer's line runs. The sea-rock bleeds. King of the bled: does the count cross water?*

*Ask me at the gray light,* the boy sent down, and climbed, and the climbing took long, because the last answer was already in him, unentered, lying in the stone of him the way the white stroke lay in the third gallery: shallow, face up, unfinished.

---

He stood at the rim at the gray light with the count going.

It went every dark now without him; that was what a law became when it held. The numbers ran down the tiers and the tiers ran them back up, feeding, owed, witnessed, the rows keeping the rows, and the boy stood above all of it with his hand on the warm stone and his sleeve over the cold country of his arm, and read his ledger while the light came.

The mother's face: he checked it the way he checked the galleries. Still there. He stood in it a while, in the gray light, in the face that was going to be the last warm room the Red had not priced, and he did not enter the checking anywhere, because some things the count held off the books, and the holding off the books was his, the one row he stood alone.

Marrow came and stood at his shoulder. Below them, out past the flats, the two hundred knelt in their rows in the tide-mud, breathing, kept, patient, the Cold's dock built and holding, and past them the lanterns of the Cordon stood on the water where no lanterns had ever stood, and beyond the lanterns, past the horizon's cold line, hulls were stacking in the old harbors of a king who did not bow, and beyond that, on a green island that had been the whole world once, a broken tribe ran with a Seer's line running ahead of the hunters.

Three waters, and all of them counting him.

Behind the door, the Red lay along the wood, low, warm, patient, its books balanced, its columns open, waiting the way it had waited since the door first stood — and the boy no longer priced the waiting as hunger. It had learned rows. It had learned law. It had learned that a held thing was worth more than a spent thing, and it had learned the lesson from him, and it lay behind the door now like the mark in the third gallery: cut shallow, face up, stopping in the middle of the line.

*Findings,* the boy entered, at the rim, at the gray light, and it was the last entry of the dark and the first of something else, and he said it aloud, flat, carrying, the way the count said everything that mattered, so the rows could hold it: *The walls held. This dark, this once, and the darks after, while the count runs. The Cold has gone to its books and builds at the rim. The water has found us. The mercy is coming home with a fleet. — Entered plain: I was one boy in a cage, and the cage was the whole world. Then the green was the whole world. Then the island. Then this city.* His hand lay on the stone. The count ran under it, down nine tiers, into the rows, into the keeping. *The world keeps getting bigger. Entered: the world was never getting bigger. The count was.*

Marrow's counted breaths ran their line beside him, one spent, one owed, and the runt watched the water and the kneeling rows and the lanterns, and did not price any of it, and said the last finding the way only Marrow said things, small, and certain, and aimed:

"Still counting."

"Yes," the boy said.

Out past the rim, under the coming light, the whole world counted back.

*End of Book Two*
