# Book Two, Chapter 20: Vessa's Clean Knife

The body of the child lay on the platform stone beside the ultimatum. The stone had taken the small shape the way it took every shape it was given. Twenty-nine faces held the sum at the far wall the way the stone held the body. The winter-thin woman with the three-knot cord had broken what she had and passed half back without looking at the face that took it. The mother stood with her hands empty now. The old ones the Skell had given the wall to lean on kept their lean. No one had asked for the lifting. No one had asked for the name to be spoken again. The tallow light made the small face look older than it had been when the pressed cake went down. The wet edge had stopped. The breath that carried it had stopped too, and neither had asked the count.

Marrow's hand stayed through the bars. The grip had not changed. The reading had. The fingers did not count the pulse at the wrist. They held what they had found and did not like the finding. The runt's oldest answer was still there — *still here* — but the hand had learned a new question from the body on the stone. The question had no words in it. The question was in the pressure that did not count and did not let go.

He stood on the platform and let the still place do what it could. The tablet had the numbers. The bowl had the drops the needle had measured two lows before. The troughs had the pressed cakes that had come short at the twenty-eighth low. The numbers and the cakes did not meet. The difference had a name now. The name was Nai. The body on the stone was the proof that the count had finished its own arithmetic without asking permission. The clay had gone up the stairs at second dark in its oiled cloth, onto the courts' shelf, working above the ceiling without him. The bowl carried the name of the owed in its base. The clay carried his under the number. The city had learned his name from both ends, and the name had not protected the one whose share had been shorted.

The Red attended without pushing. It waited the way a thing waits when the thing it has been waiting for has already happened without its help. The silence was worse than the sentence. The silence meant the arithmetic had not needed the pushing. The thing behind the door had two words now. Later off the city. First off its own counting. The temple had brought a third word down the stairs, and the third word had not needed the Red to speak it.

Lorren came down the last of the stairs from the third gallery. The fasting sign was cut deeper into the wrist than it had been two lows before. She looked at the body on the stone and did not look at him. The line watched her not looking. The not-looking was a law the count had not written. Her shoulders carried the weight of the vigil the way a body carries a thing it has already lost and cannot put down. The child had murmured in her sleep on the stair-foot's rhythm through the lows. The rhythm had not fed the child. The law's time had kept the child waiting and the waiting had taken what it came for.

He put his hand to the gap where the three teeth were gone. The cold behind the ribs did not stop for the palm. The cold was not the old cold from the green or the hold or the pit. It was the cold of a sum that had solved itself while he stood still on the platform. The child whose name the count had taken had not eaten. The share had been shorted. The order built to protect the bled had taken from the bled, and the taking had been clean. The body went cold in the place where the still place lived. A different cold. The cold of a thing that had already been decided.

The Skell moved along the far wall without hurry. The amber eyes went from face to face and did not split the line. The Skell had sorted the old before the new for two lows now. The sorting had held. The bodies who had been born knowing how to stand in a short had learned the new law off the Skell's silence and off the half-cakes moving inside the waiting. The law said later. Later had run out for the one whose name the count had spoken when the cords came down. The Skell stopped at the lean and stood with the old ones. The lean had not changed. The bodies who leaned had learned what the count did with a short it could not close.

He came down from the platform stone. The mother did not step back. She had already done the arithmetic. The eyes that had been not angry two lows before were the same eyes. Anger was a thing a body spent while it still believed the sum could be moved. Her eyes had done the sum and found it already standing. The child at the hip had the weight of a body that had already begun to leave, and now the leaving was finished. The wet edge had become a rattle and then nothing. The twenty-nine faces did the sum the way they had learned to do it from the count. The sum was the same in every pair of eyes. The order built to hold the bled had been the thing that let go.

The ultimatum sat on the stone where the hook-chief had left it. Two lows gone now, and the bowl still no heavier than blood. The frost kept writing its side — the fourth gate's sweat, the fifth's bars, thicker at every change of the watch. The rim-stones drank and asked for more. The wall had waited with the patience the count had taught it. The line at the far wall had not pressed. They had not cursed. They had stood, and the standing was a law the count had not meant to write but had written anyway. The herd was coming or the watch was not a watch or the count would have to choose which of the twenty-nine the glass would measure next. The choosing could be spoken aloud and witnessed and recorded. The same glass that measured the taking could measure the going.

He said none of it.

Marrow's fingers tightened once on the wrist. Not the counting press. A different pressure — the grip of a hand that has held a thing through the cage and the green and the tribe and the pit and the crossing and the hold, and has begun, for the first time, to count what the holding costs. The reading was in the hand alone.

---

The sound came from the stairs at the third gallery. Not the sound of the Skell or the brokers or the ones who carried the clay up to the courts' shelf. It was the sound of cloth that had not been in the sump, of feet that had not learned the wet stone or the rot under the troughs. Clean feet. The sound carried down the tiers the way a thing carried from above always carried — slower than the carrying of the owed, with space between the steps that the sump had never taught.

Vessa came first. She wore the temple's white that had no stain from the bled. The white caught the tallow light and gave none of it back. Her hands were clean in the way hands are clean when they have never held the needle or the trough or the cord or the cracked nail. The healers came behind her with bundles wrapped in the same white. The bundles did not smell of rot or pressed cake or the wall's wet breath or the frost that wrote on the bars. They smelled of nothing the sump had taught the nose to expect. The smell of clean was a foreign thing here. It made the sump smell louder.

She stopped at the edge of the platform stone. The line at the far wall did not press forward. They watched the clean cloth and the clean hands the way bodies watch a thing that has come from a place the count did not reach. The mother stood with the empty hands. The winter-thin woman with the three-knot cord stood with the half-cake broken in her fingers. The old ones at the lean did not straighten. The Skell watched from the far wall with the amber eyes that had sorted the old before the new.

"The temple grieves the bled," Vessa said. It carried the way clean things carry in a place built for blood. The voice had no rasp. It had no blood in it from the gap where teeth were gone. It had the sound of a thing that had been spoken in rooms above the ceiling and had come down whole. "The share was counted for the child. The count took the name when the cords came down. The count said the name would eat. The name has not eaten. The share has been shorted. The temple brings what the count could not hold."

She did not look at the body on the stone first. She looked at the line. The winter-thin woman. The mother. The old ones. The ones whose cords had knots the count had not untied. The eyes that had done the arithmetic and found the sum already standing. The eyes that had watched the half-cakes move inside the waiting. The eyes that had watched the body go still.

One of the healers moved to the mother. The bundle was unwrapped without haste. Clean cloth. A thing that smelled of green that had not been in the green for a long time. The healer touched the mother's shoulder. The touch was not the touch of the Skell or the touch of the hand through the bars or the touch of the cracked nail. It was the touch of a thing that had come to do a thing the count had not done. The mother did not pull away. The body that had held the child against the shoulder for two lows let the healer take the weight of the small shape. The healer laid the child back on the stone with the clean cloth under it. The body did not change. The stone took the shape again.

Lorren watched from the stair-foot. The fasting sign on her wrist caught the tallow light. She did not move toward the clean cloth. The Skell had stopped at the far wall and stood with the line. Somewhere down the line another half-cake broke and half went back. The giving had not stopped for clean feet. The movement was a law the count had taught without meaning to. The clean cloth was a different law.

Vessa spoke again. The voice did not rise. "The temple holds the grief for the bled. The mourning belongs to the temple. Those who stand with the one who shorts the share stand outside the grief. The rites are closed to them until they stand again with the tithe as it was given. The temple grieves the bled. The temple will come again when the next short is counted. The grief will be there for those who stand with the tithe as it was given."

The words sat on the stone beside the body and the ultimatum. The line did the sum. The sum had a new column now. The column had no number. It had the clean hands and the clean cloth and the grief that had not come down the stairs until the short had a name and a body and a mother who had held the child so the line could see the face and the sound that came with each breath. The sum had the apart that had not been there before the clean feet came down.

The mother took what the healer gave. The thing in the bundle was not pressed cake. It was a different weight. The mother put it to her mouth and did not eat it. She held it the way a body holds a thing that had come from above. The line watched the holding. The holding was not the same as the half-cakes moving inside the waiting. The holding was the holding of a thing that had a name the count had not given it.

One of the old ones at the lean took what was offered. The hand that took it did not look at the platform stone. The hand that took it looked at the clean cloth and the healer who had brought it. The old one put the thing in the mouth and swallowed. The swallowing was a sound the line heard. The line at the far wall did the sum. The sum was not the same in every pair of eyes.

Lorren did not take what was offered. The Skell did not take what was offered. The winter-thin woman with the three-knot cord broke what she had and passed half back and did not reach for the clean bundle. Three faces. The rest of the line did the sum the way the count had taught them. The sum had changed. The apart was not a roar. It was the stillness of bodies doing a new arithmetic. Some eyes had taken the clean thing. Some eyes had not. The ones who had taken the clean thing stood a little apart from the ones who had not. The apart was a law the count had not meant to write.

He read Vessa whole. The body was not the body of the pit or the hold or the green. The hands had no cracked nail. The cloth had no sump on it. The eyes had no blood in them from the needle or the glass. The voice had no rasp from the gap where teeth were gone. She was the temple's. The temple had sent its clean knife down the stairs because the count had made the short visible. The knife did not cut the body. The knife cut the line between the count and the ones the count had sworn to hold. The knife cut the place where the bled had believed the count would stop the choosing. The knife cut the shame that the bled now carried for believing. Clean hands. Closed rites. The seam the pit had missed.

The Red attended without stirring. It waited the way a thing waits when the thing it has been waiting for has already been spoken by another mouth. The silence was the same silence that had been there when the body went still on the stone. The arithmetic had finished without him again. This time the finishing had a clean hand on it. The thing behind the door had learned the choosing from the count. The temple had spoken the choosing aloud. The Red had not needed to push. The silence meant the thing was learning the clean way the way it had learned the pit's way and the green's way and the cage's way. The clean way did not require blood on the stones. It required only the choosing, spoken aloud, witnessed, recorded. The same glass that measured the taking could measure the going.

He put his hand to the gap. The palm did not stop the cold. The cold had a name now. The name was the same name the line was learning off the clean cloth and the closed rites and the apart. The name was the one the count could not answer with numbers or with the tablet or with the bowl. The name was the one the clean knife had brought down the stairs.

The healers moved among the shorted. The winter-thin woman let them touch the place where the three-knot cord had worn the skin. The mother let them take the weight of the small shape again and wrap it in the clean cloth that had not been in the sump. The old ones at the lean took what was offered and swallowed. The line at the far wall watched the taking. The taking had come from above, and the above had named the short and closed the grief to the ones who had believed the count would hold what it had counted. The taking had a cost. The cost was the apart. The cost was the not-looking. The cost was Lorren at the stair-foot with the fasting sign cut deeper into the wrist.

Vessa did not look at him. She looked at the line. The voice carried without rising. "The temple grieves the bled. The temple will come again when the next short is counted. The grief will be there for those who stand with the tithe as it was given."

She turned. The clean feet went back up the stairs. The healers went with her. The bundles that had not been in the sump went with them. What they had left behind stayed on the stone and in the hands of the ones who had taken it. The body of the child was gone from the platform stone. The clean cloth had taken the small shape. The ultimatum sat where it had sat. The line at the far wall had fewer faces than it had had when the clean feet came down. The takers stood apart from the rest now, and no voice had been raised to put them there. The apart was a quiet thing — a line redrawn in stillness.

Marrow's hand stayed on the wrist. The grip had not changed. The reading had changed again. The fingers held what they had found and counted the holding and found the holding had a new cost. The cost was the clean cloth and the closed rites and the apart. The cost was the mother who had taken what the healer gave. The cost was the old one who had swallowed. The cost was the line that had learned a new column from the clean hands.

He stood on the platform and let the still place do the sum. The tablet had the numbers. The bowl had the drops. The troughs had the pressed cakes. The line had the clean things and the closed rites and the apart that had not been there before the clean feet came down. The numbers and the cakes and the line did not meet. The difference had a name. The name was Nai. The clean knife had a name too. The name was Vessa. The knife had not cut the body. The knife had cut the place where the count touched the bled who had believed it.

The Red attended without pushing. It did not speak the clean sentence. It waited the way patience in the sump was never rest. It was appetite standing still while another mouth spoke the choosing the count had refused to speak. The silence was the thing the body went cold with. Not the silence. The not-knowing whether the silence was agreement or the thing learning a new way to wait. The thing had learned the pit's way. The thing had learned the green's way. The thing had learned the cage's way. The thing had learned the count's way. The clean way was a new way. The clean way did not require the pit or the blood on the stones. It required only the choosing, spoken aloud, witnessed, recorded. The same glass that measured the taking could measure the going. The Red had not needed to push. The temple had done the pushing for it.

The ultimatum sat on the stone. Two lows had gone. The bowl was not heavier than blood. The frost was thicker on the fifth's bars than it had been at first light. The rim-stones drank what they were given and asked for more. The herd was coming or the watch was not a watch or the count would have to choose which of the twenty-nine the glass would measure next while the clean knife waited for the next short to be counted. The line at the far wall stood. The apart was a law the count had not meant to write. The clean knife had found the seam the pit had missed. The seam was the place where the count touched the bled who had believed it. The seam was the shame the clean hands had brought down the stairs.

He put his hand to the gap where the three teeth were gone. The palm did not stop the cold. The cold had learned the house. It lived there now. It had learned the count's grammar and the count's mercy and which mouths the wall would drink anyway and which mouths the clean knife would name when the next short came. The arithmetic had finished without him. The clean knife had finished what the pit had not finished. The Red attended without stirring. The silence and the cold had learned each other.

Marrow's fingers tightened once. Not the counting press. A different pressure. The grip of a body that has found something in the thing it holds and does not like the finding and has begun to count what the finding will cost the holding. The hand had held through everything the city had done to the one it held. The hand had not let go. The hand was counting the holding now, and the holding had a new column. The column had no number. It had clean hands and closed rites and the apart that the count could not close with a tablet or a bowl or a drop measured by the needle's glass.

The line at the far wall did the sum. The sum was not the same in every pair of eyes. Some eyes had taken the clean thing. Some eyes had not. The ones who had not looked at the platform stone the way they had looked when the cords came down. Like the answer. Like the largest thing that would let them come near. The ones who had taken the clean thing did not look at the platform stone. They looked at the hands that had given the clean thing and at the stairs where the clean feet had gone.

The frost wrote its side of the ultimatum on the bars. The rim-stones drank. The line did the sum, and the sum had a column no tablet would carry. The arithmetic had finished without him again. The clean knife had a name.

The name was Vessa.
