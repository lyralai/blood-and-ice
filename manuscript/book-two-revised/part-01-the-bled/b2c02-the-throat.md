# Book Two, Chapter 2: The Throat

The morning of the ninth low, the guards came with water.

Not the feed-slot water — not the clay jug, not the measured drink. A tub. Wide as the sleeping-platform, shallow as a hand-breadth, the wood dark with use. Two guards carried it between them on a pole, the water sloshing against the rim with the careful step of men who had carried full things through stone corridors and knew the cost of a spill. They set it on the floor outside the door and waited.

A third guard came with a cloth. And a fourth with a blade.

Not the short chain-blade the pit-guards carried. Curved, bronze, the edge bright in the torch-light — a working edge, made for skin. The guard with the blade stood at the door and did not enter.

The tall one — Maren's function — came last. He carried the stool, the three legs, and he set it inside the door and sat the way he sat: the sitting was the first move.

"Wash," the tall one said. "The water is clean. The cloth is clean. The blade is for the hair."

The boy looked at the blade. The boy looked at the water.

"The rite requires the body bare," the tall one said. The voice was the voice of a man reading a thing he had read many times — the words smooth with use, the edges worn. "No cloth. No hair. No mark that is not the body's own. The floor sees what the body brings and nothing else."

The floor. Not the pit — the floor. The boy filed the word. A pit was where you threw a body. A floor was where you stood. The word change was what the city did — what the green had done with the fruit trees, what the slavers had done with the chain. You gave a name that was not the thing's name, and the name taught the body what to feel about it.

The pit was a floor. The cage was a room. The chain was a lead. The boy was property.

He had read this language before.

---

The boy stripped. The cloth went. The guards took the clothes — the rough-woven shirt, the bandage the healer woman had wrapped — and the cloth left the cell the way all things left the cell: through the door that locked from outside.

He stepped into the tub. The water was cold — the dry cold of the stone, the cold of a thing underground that did not care. It hit the scar on the shoulder first. The new skin tightened. The warm pulsed behind the breastbone — faint, the banked thing, the ember under ash — and went quiet.

He washed. The cloth was rough, the weave loose, the kind of cloth that scrubbed rather than smoothed. He worked the water over the skin the way the body washed — the order the body knew: chest first, then the limbs, then the back, then the face last because the face was the thing the water made you close your eyes for, and closing the eyes was the thing the cage had taught him never to do.

He closed his eyes. The water went over his face. The cold found the gap where the three teeth were gone and the cold went through the gap and into the bone and the bone remembered the cage.

He opened his eyes. The water was on the floor. The guards had not moved.

The blade came next. The guard with the curved edge entered — slow, the professional distance, the blade held away from the body the way a man holds a weapon that can end a conversation. The tall one nodded. The guard knelt.

The boy stood still. The blade took the hair — the head-hair first, the dark coarse growth that the green had left matted and the cell had left matted worse. The blade worked close, the bronze scraping the scalp, the sound of removal. The hair fell in clumps on the wet stone. The guard's hand was steady — not the barber's carefulness of a man making something beautiful, but the field-stripping steadiness of a man preparing a surface.

The body-hair next. The chest, the arms, the legs. The blade found the scar on the shoulder and went around it — not careful, just practical. The scar was raised; the blade could not take what was not there.

The guard stood. The boy stood in the tub, stripped and smooth, the skin showing every ridge and hollow the cell and the green and the cage had put into it. The ribs. The brands — the old marks, the seer's marks, the tribe's marks, healed into the skin the way the city's eye-and-thorn was healed into the stone. The scar on the shoulder, white, the shape of a jaw.

The tall one looked at the brands. The eyes went to them the way the eyes had gone to the scar — the pricing, the reckoning. But the brands were not the scar. The brands were given, not taken. The tall one's eyes stayed on them one beat longer than the pricing needed, and the staying was what the boy read: *the brands do not fit the shelf either.*

"Clean him," the tall one said.

The guards poured clean water over him — a second tub, carried and upended. The cold went through him. The warm did not stir. The root did not stir. The still place held.

They gave him a cloth — not the washing cloth, a wrapping cloth, thin, the color of unbleached fiber. It went around the waist. It was the only covering the rite allowed. The wrapping was the last gift the city would give him before the floor.

The walking-chain went on. The door opened. The corridor was stone and torch-light and the smell of tallow and old blood, the baseline smell of the place, and under it the smell of something else: smoke. Not wood-smoke. Fat-smoke, tallow burned in quantity, the smoke of many torches in a space that needed them.

The smoke was the throat.

---

The corridor went down.

Not the corridor the boy had walked before — not the tier-climb from cell to room, the upward path past the Mure and the counting-rooms. This corridor descended, the stone worn smooth in the center of the floor by the passage of many feet over many lows. The walls narrowed as they went. The torches came closer together. The tallow-smell thickened until the boy's throat closed around it and the breathing went shallow.

The guards walked at his sides. The tall one walked ahead. The short one with the ledger was not there — the nib-scratch was absent, and the absence told the boy a thing: the counting had already been done. Whatever the short one wrote, it had been written. The tall one was not counting now. The tall one was *delivering.*

The corridor branched. The right branch went up — the boy read the angle, the cleaner air, the direction of the room he had been moved to. The left branch went down. The smoke came from the left.

They went left.

The corridor opened into a space the boy read the way he read all spaces: whole shape first, then the parts.

The shape was a throat.

A shaft — circular, wider than the cell-gallery, wider than the holding-tier, the walls cut stone gone dark with moisture and age. The shaft went up and the shaft went down. The down was a floor — packed earth, dark, the color of old blood the way the pit-floor had been, but larger, wider, the earth of a thing that had been a killing-ground for longer than the pit. The up was a gallery — no, galleries. Tiers of galleries, ring on ring, cutting into the shaft-wall the way the counting-rooms cut into the holding-tier, but these were not rooms. These were overlooks. Bench-rows, stone-cut, rising in rings, each ring wider than the one below, each ring full.

Full of faces.

The boy read the faces the way he read the green — layers. The lowest gallery was the pit-guards, the hard-handed men, the chain-workers. Above them: the working-tier faces, the ink-stained, the bent-step people, the ones who served the thing that ate. Above them: the broker-faces — Maren's kind, the counters, the ledgers held in laps, the eyes doing the pricing from a distance. And above the brokers, higher, the gallery rimmed with carved stone: the temple faces. Robes. The eye-and-thorn not stitched but burned into the fabric — burned, the mark of a thing that was older than the stitching. And higher still, the topmost gallery, the one the boy could barely see: the seats that were not benches but chairs, the chairs that had backs, the backs that had carving. The blood-courts. The houses that held the bloodlines and the ledgers and the old genealogies that priced a body by what it remembered.

Four powers. Four galleries. Each one stationed at a different distance from the floor.

The throat was not a pit. The throat was a map.

---

The boy stood at the edge of the floor. The walking-chain came off. The guards stepped back — not up, not out. Back. Through a side-passage, a low door the boy had not seen, the door closing behind them.

He was alone on the floor.

The floor was a circle. The packed earth was dark — not the dark of wet earth but the dark of soaked earth, the dark of ground that had been blooded so many times the blood had become the soil. The boy's bare feet read it: cold, dense, the give of earth that had been packed and repacked until the blood was a layer in the stone under it. The green's earth had been alive — roots, rot, the breathing of growing things. This earth was dead. This earth had been dead a long time.

The torches were in iron baskets on the walls — high, above the lowest gallery, the light falling at an angle that lit the floor and left the galleries in half-dark. The faces were lit from below. The light caught the chins, the mouths, the hollows of the eyes. The effect was what the boy recognized from the fire-light on the island — how fire-light made the tribe's faces into masks, the features sharpened, the eyes gone to shadow.

But the tribe had been one fire, one ring, one kind of face. Here there were four kinds, and each kind watched from its own height.

The boy set his feet. The still place set the count: the galleries, the tiers, the exits — the low door the guards had gone through, and across the floor, two other low doors, closed, the stone of their frames dark with old use. Three doors. Three ways in or out. The running-chain ring was in the floor at the center — an iron ring, heavy, set into a stone block that was older than the surrounding earth. The ring had grooves in it. The grooves were worn smooth by chain.

The boy was not chained. The walking-chain had come off. He was on the floor with nothing on him but the wrapping cloth and the brands and the scar and the name behind the teeth and the count and the still place.

And what lived under the ribs.

---

A sound went through the galleries. Not the roar the boy had heard in the pit — this was ordered, rhythmic, a thing made by many throats working together. A chanting. The temple-gallery began it — the low, sustained drone of voices that had been trained to hold a note, the sound rolling down the shaft-walls the way water rolls down stone. The broker-gallery took it up a half-beat behind, the voices thinner, less practiced. The working-tier joined. The pit-guards did not.

The chanting was something the boy felt in the floor. The vibration came through the packed earth and through the soles and through the bones of his feet and into the legs, and the body read it the way it read the green's vibration — the deep hum of something alive and larger than him.

But the green's hum had been the sound of growth. This hum was the sound of watching.

The chanting said: *the floor is open. The rite has begun. Watch.*

The boy stood on the floor and the chanting went through him and he counted — each head in each gallery, each tier, each exit — the way the body tallied a crossing or a holding or a low. And the tally said: *they are not here to watch you. They are here to watch what your blood does.*

The boy was not what the floor had been built for. The boy was the lens. The blood was what the floor had been built for.

---

One of the low doors opened.

What came through was not the pit-thing from the first fight — not the heavy, low-slung, island-hauled predator. This creature was built different. Taller. Narrower. The legs were long, the body lean, the head set high on a neck that curved. The skin was pale — not the green-rot pallor of the starved island thing, but a paleness the boy read as original. It had been pale before it was caged. It had been born in the dark.

The eyes were what the body read first. Not the dead eyes of a starved predator. Not the flat reading-eyes of the Hesh. These eyes were bright — bright the way the patron's eyes went bright, the look of a mind that had seen a number and was still calculating. The eyes of something that read back.

The wrongness stirred. The root, deep in its blood-stone country, opened one fraction — not the full uncurling, not the assessment-display, but the crack of something waking to read what it knew.

The boy pressed the root down. The reading came through the pressure: *not above. Not below. Not on. Unknown.*

The root did not know this creature. The root had known the Mure — *same rung* — and the root had known the island predator — *below* — and the root had known the apex — *above.* This one the root did not place. The root tasted the shape of it through the floor-vibration and the taste was a frequency the root had no shelf for.

The boy filed the reading. *The root does not know everything.* The filing went into the still place beside the count.

---

It stood across the floor. Twenty strides. The distance of a charge — the distance the boy knew from the island, the distance the Hesh had crossed, the distance the pit-thing had crossed. The killing-distance.

But it did not charge.

The usher stood. The pale eyes read the boy. The curved neck held the head high — a posture the boy recognized from the king on the high ground, the king who had touched the bad leg and opened instead of closing. The posture of a creature that assessed before it acted. One that did not need to charge because the charging was not what it was best at.

The chanting had stopped. The silence was worse than the sound — the silence was the sound of many throats holding their breath, and the held breath was what the boy felt in the floor-vibration: *the watching.*

The boy read it. The legs said: *fast.* The neck said: *reach.* The head said: *teeth.* The eyes said: *I am reading you too.* The overall shape said: *I have been here before. I have been on this floor before. I know what the floor is for.*

Not starved. Not stupid. Kept for the floor the way the boy had been kept for the cell — measured, fed, brought out on a schedule. A champion.

The boy set his feet in the old blood. The wrapping cloth hung from the waist. The brands showed in the torch-light — the marks the tall one's eyes had lingered on, the marks that did not fit the shelf. The gap where the three teeth were gone held the name behind it. The scar on the shoulder was a white line in the torch-light, and the warm pulsed under it — not waking, not rising, just the banked ember turning over in its ash.

The boy had no chain. No spear. No fire. No walls. The only things he had were the body and what lived under the ribs and the count and the floor.

The floor was all old blood.

The root read the floor. The boy felt it — the root reading the ground through his soles the way it had read the Mure through the iron bars. The packed earth was layers of blood — low after low, year after year, the blood of every wrong-blood who had been brought to the floor and opened and read. The root tasted the layers, and the tasting was not the simple *below* of the pit-thing or the *same rung* of the Mure. The tasting was complexity — many bloods, many kinds, some above and some below and some on and some the root had no name for. A history of wrong bloods, soaked into the earth, layered, compressed.

The root wanted to drink.

The boy pressed the root down. The still place bore. The root went quiet — not asleep, not cowed. Patient. The patience of something that had been patient a very long time and saw no reason to change.

---

It moved first.

Not a charge — a walk. The long legs covered the ground in measured strides, the curved neck holding the head at the height of a man's chest. The pale eyes did not blink. The mouth was closed. No teeth showing. The posture said: *I am coming to read you at the distance where reading becomes touching.*

The boy held. He had learned this on the island — the body that reads last is the body that reads best. The Hesh had taught him to go sideways. The pit-thing had taught him to go forward. This creature was teaching him nothing yet. It was coming to teach.

It stopped. Five strides. The distance of a reaching jaw, a lunging neck, a fast body's first strike. The level gaze held the boy's chest. The nostrils flared — it was reading the boy's smell while the boy read its shape. The reading went through the air between them.

It smelled the warm.

The boy saw it happen. The nostrils flared wider — not the flare of a predator smelling meat. The flare of a creature that had found a scent it knew and had not expected. The pale eyes went bright. The head drew back — not fear, not the pit-thing's flinch. The drawing-back of something that has been burned before and has just smelled the fire.

It knew the warm.

The root filed it — *it knows.* The root's patience shifted. Not aggressive. Interested. The way the pale-rip one on the high ground had been interested in the king's seat: measuring distance with the eyes while the hands did something else.

The boy watched it draw back. The level gaze recalculated. It had come to read the boy, and the reading had given it a number it did not expect. The usher was re-shelving.

The galleries held their breath.

---

It struck.

Fast — faster than the boy had read. The neck coiled and the head came down and the mouth opened and the teeth were there, long, pale, the teeth of a predator evolved for the dark and the cold. The strike was not the pit-thing's charge — no weight behind it, no commitment. The strike was a test. The teeth came for the shoulder — the scarred shoulder, the one the warm had healed — and the boy went sideways, the old instinct, the Hesh-instinct, and the teeth caught air.

It recovered. Fast — faster than the pit-thing's recovery. The long neck swung and the head came around and the pale eyes found the boy and the reading was already done. The usher had tested the sideways. It would not test the same way twice.

The boy set his feet. The body was doing what the body did — the shaking, the hollow, the adrenaline-sharpening that the cage had taught him was the body getting ready, not the body falling apart. His hands were empty. His teeth were a weapon for a creature this size the way a stone was a weapon for a river.

It struck again. Not the shoulder — the thigh. Low, fast, the angle different. The boy went forward — the pit-instinct, under the strike — and the teeth caught his ribs instead of the thigh. Not deep. A rake. Three lines, the skin opening, the blood coming hot and red down the side.

The galleries roared.

The blood hit the floor. The packed earth drank it — fast, the way dry earth drinks, the blood disappearing into the dark ground. And the floor *changed.* The boy felt it through his soles. The old blood in the earth — the layers, the history — stirred. Not the earth stirring. The blood stirring. The old blood in the ground tasted the new blood, and the tasting was a vibration that went up through the boy's feet and into the root.

The root *opened.*

Not the boy's choice. Not the warm. The root — the survival-core, the fist, what the island had burned him down to — opened because the floor opened it. The old blood in the earth was what the root recognized. The old blood was *the same kind.* Not the same as the boy's blood. Not the same as the warm. The same as the *root.* The root tasted the floor and the floor tasted the root, and the tasting was a conversation the boy was not part of.

The root pressed up. The still place bore down. The line held — thin, fraying. The root was stronger here. The root was stronger on this floor than it had been in the cell or the pit or the green. The floor was feeding the root the way the green's earth had fed the roots of the trees — from below, silently, the sustenance the surface did not see.

Across the floor, the usher read the change. The pale eyes widened. It saw the boy's body shift — the stance changing, the weight dropping lower, the shoulders squaring in a way they had not been squared before. It saw the root.

The boy pulled the root down. Hard. The still place locked — the name, the count, the Kala's sound, everything the island had built on top of the root — and the root went down, and the boy stood on the floor with his bleeding ribs and his bare feet and his empty hands, and the count said: *the floor is the weapon. Not the thing across from me. The floor.*

---

It circled. The boy turned with it. The ribs bled. The blood went into the earth and the earth drank it and the floor-vibration changed again — a deeper frequency, a lower note, the hum of a hunger that had been given a taste and wanted the rest.

The galleries were making sound — not the chanting, not the roar. A murmuring. The many-throated sound of a crowd that had seen the blood go down and was waiting for the blood to do what blood was supposed to do. The crowd had paid for the blood's behavior. The floor was the stage. The boy was the actor.

The boy was not performing.

It struck a third time. The neck coiled and the head came fast and low — under the guard, under the sideways, under the forward — and the teeth found the calf and went in.

The pain was white. The pain was total. The calf muscle seized around the teeth and the jaw locked and the weight came through and the boy went down.

He hit the floor. The packed earth — the old blood earth — hit his back and the floor-vibration went through him like a current. The root surged. The root tasted the floor at full contact — the full weight of the boy's body on the blood-soaked earth — and the root *sang.*

Not a sound. A vibration. A frequency the boy felt in the bone, in the marrow, in the deep places where the blood was made. The root sang the floor's frequency, and the floor sang back, and the two frequencies were the same.

The warm stirred.

Under the root, deep behind the breastbone, the warm turned over. The ember, banked in ash, felt the root's song and felt the floor's answer, and the warm woke — not the slow surfacing of the pit-fight, not the desperate weapon-flare that had closed the shoulder wound. A slower waking. A deeper waking. The waking of a thing that had been called by something it recognized, the way a body wakes at a sound it knows even in sleep.

Its teeth were in the calf. The long neck pulled, the legs found purchase, dragging the boy toward the center of the floor. Toward the iron ring. Toward the stone block with the chain-grooves.

The boy understood. It was not killing him. It was *positioning* him. It had been trained — not to fight, but to drag. To bring the wrong-blood to the center, where the ring was, where the chain could go on, where the bleeding could begin. The usher was the floor's hand.

Not the champion. The *usher.*

---

The boy twisted. The calf screamed. His hands found the curved neck — the high-held neck — and he pulled. Not the warm's pull. Not the wrongness's pull. The body's pull — the cage-pull, the old strength that came from the back and the arms and the hatred of being dragged.

The jaw loosened. Not much — a fraction, the teeth pulling from the muscle — and the boy ripped his leg free. The blood went down. The floor drank it.

He rolled. The ribs, the leg, the torn calf — the body catalogued the damage the way the body catalogued all damage: fast, practical, the counting of costs. The leg would bear weight. The ribs were bleeding but not deep. The calf was bad — the muscle was torn, the blood was bright, the kind of bleeding that would weaken a body in minutes if the body were a finite thing.

The boy was not finite. He knew this the way the tall one knew his ledgers — the number was on the page.

He came up. The usher was between him and the center ring. The pale eyes tracked him. It did not charge. It waited — patient now, the way the root was patient, a body that had done its job and was waiting for the floor to do the rest.

The boy's blood was on the floor. The boy's blood was in the earth. The root was singing the floor's frequency, and the floor was singing back, and the warm was awake behind the breastbone, and the galleries were murmuring the murmur of a crowd that had seen the blood go down and was waiting.

Waiting for what the blood did.

---

The boy read the floor.

Not the way the tall one read the bleeding-books, not the way Maren's function priced the yield. The boy read the floor the way the green had taught him to read the green — the layers, the ages, what the surface covered. The packed earth was not earth. The packed earth was a body. A vast, old, layered body made of blood, and the blood was not dead. The blood was in the earth the way the root was in the boy — banked, waiting, fed by every new body that bled on it.

The floor was a root.

The boy understood it the way he understood the Mure in the cage — the flash of recognition, the root saying *same kind.* The floor was not a killing-ground. The floor was an organ. The city was not built on blood-trade. The city was built on *this* — this floor, this earth, this root-mass of old blood that drank and held and vibrated at the frequency the root knew. The tiers, the galleries, the counting-rooms, the bleeding-houses, the eye-and-thorn on every wall — all of it was built around the floor. All of it was built to feed the floor.

The eye-and-thorn. The eye watches. The thorn draws.

The floor was the eye. The blood was the thorn.

The warm came up.

---

Not the root. The warm — what lived under the root, the deeper layer, the heritage the seer's finger had found. It came up all at once, a door opening, not a faucet turning. The warm came up from behind the breastbone and went into the ribs and the torn calf and the bleeding side, and the boy's body went from a body that was torn and bleeding and finite to a body that was *warm*, and the warm was not healing and it was not strength.

It was the floor.

The warm was the same frequency as the floor. The warm and the floor were one — the same root, the same old blood, the same deep layer that the city had been built to harvest and the boy carried behind his breastbone. The warm recognized the floor. The floor recognized the warm. And the recognition was what the boy had never had on the island — the *place.* Not a den, not a territory, not a held space. A place where the warm was *meant to be.*

The warm went down through his feet.

The floor received it.

---

The packed earth changed color. The boy saw it happen from above — from inside the warm, from the place where the body was warm and the warm was the floor and the floor was the boy. The dark earth lightened around his feet — not drying, not cracking. *Warming.* The blood in the earth — the layers, the history — the blood stirred. The old blood in the ground felt the warm the way the Mure had felt the warm in the pit — not poison, not weapon. A frequency. A key.

The vibration changed. The floor's hum went from the deep, patient, waiting frequency to a higher one — sharper, keener, the frequency of a hunger that had been given what it needed. The boy felt it in his bones, in his teeth, in the gap where the three were gone. The gap vibrated. The name behind the teeth vibrated.

The warm was not just the boy's.

The warm was the floor's. The warm was the old blood's. The warm was what the floor had been drinking toward for centuries — wrong-blood after wrong-blood, each one brought to the floor and opened, each one's blood soaking into the earth, each one giving the floor a taste of the frequency it was built to find. And none of them had been enough. None of them had been the *right* blood. The floor had been tasting and waiting and tasting and waiting, and the tasting had kept the city running — the blood-trade, the tithe, the yield, the jars — but the waiting had never ended.

Until the warm came up through the boy's feet and the floor said: *here.*

---

The usher went still.

Not the locked-rigid stillness of the pit-thing that had tasted the warm and flinched. A different stillness. The stillness of a creature that had felt the floor change under its feet and did not know what the change meant but knew what the change *was.* The pale eyes went wide. The curved neck drew back. The body gathered — legs under, weight centered — preparing to flee.

The thing had been trained to drag wrong-bloods to the center. The thing knew the floor. The thing had walked this floor many times, and the floor had always been the floor — dark earth, old blood, the baseline thing under the feet. The thing had never felt the floor *respond.*

The thing fled.

Not toward the boy. Away. Toward the low door it had come through — the door the usher lived behind. The long legs drove for the door. The door was closed.

The thing hit the door. The sound was the sound of a body hitting stone, and the body rebounded, and the thing stood in front of the closed door and made a sound the boy had not heard a living thing make.

Not a scream. Not a roar. A sound older than the throat making it — the sound deep things make when the deep things change, the sound of a mind that has read the world one way for its whole life and the world has just told it the reading was wrong.

The galleries had gone silent. Not the held-breath silence of the watching. The silence of a crowd that had seen the blood do a thing the blood was not supposed to do. The silence of four powers that had stationed themselves at four distances from the floor, and the floor had just answered the boy the way it had never answered any of them.

---

The warm was in the floor now. The boy could feel it — the warm under his feet, spreading through the packed earth the way water spreads through sand, going down into the layers, going down into the old blood. And the old blood was waking.

The memories hit him.

Not his memories. Not the sister's hair or the Old One's page-sound or the fruit taste — his memories, the after-things, the things the warm had burned as fuel in the pit. These were different. These were the floor's memories — the old blood's memories, layered, compressed, ancient. The boy saw them the way he saw the green — in fragments, in shapes, in the body's reading before the head's understanding.

A room. Stone. Fire-light. The eye-and-thorn on the wall — not carved into stone but *painted* on plaster, the colors bright, the eye red, the thorn black. Hands — not his hands, smaller, the fingers delicate — mixing pigment. The hands wore the eye-and-thorn on a ring. The ring was gold.

A corridor. Stone. Cold. The cold of a place that had been cold for a long time — a cold the boy knew, the cold the green's rain carried, but deeper. Drier. The corridor had doors. Behind the doors: blood. Not killing — bleeding. Controlled. The tubes, the jars, the measured draw. The corridor was old. The corridor was the city before the city was the city.

A face. Not the sister's face. Not the mother's face the boy could not find. A face that was *his* face — the bones of it, the jaw, the brow — on a body that was not his, in clothes that were not his, in a light that was not his. The face looked at him. The face was not afraid. The face was *old*, and the old was not years. The old was the thing the root recognized when the root tasted the floor — the same kind, the same deep layer, the same old blood.

The face opened its mouth. The mouth shaped a word.

The word was a name. Not *Rook.* Not the name behind the teeth. A different name — older, heavier, a name that had weight the way the iron ring in the floor had weight. A name the boy's bones recognized before his ears did.

The name went into the gap where the three teeth were gone, and the gap *held* it — the way the gap held the name behind the teeth, the way the gap held everything the boy kept. The gap took the old name and set it beside the new name and the two names sat together in the warm dark of the gap, and neither one was wrong.

---

The visions broke.

The warm snapped back behind the breastbone — fast, the way a hand pulls from a flame. The boy stood on the floor with his bleeding ribs and his torn calf and his bare feet on the dark earth, and the earth was dark again — not warm, not responding. The warm had touched the floor and the floor had touched back and the warm had retreated, and the retreat was the thing the boy recognized: the warm retreating the way it had retreated in the pit, the way it always retreated. The warm did not stay. The warm came and took and left.

But the visions had stayed.

The boy had them — fragments, pieces, the room with the painted eye-and-thorn, the corridor with the bleeding-doors, the face that was his face on a body that was not his. The old name in the gap. The floor's memories, broken and partial, filed in the still place beside the count.

The thing by the door was making the old sound. The galleries were silent. The torches guttered in a draft the boy could not feel.

He stood on the floor and the floor was the floor and the boy was the boy, and between them the warm had opened a door and the door had closed and the thing behind the door was still there — not gone, not burned as fuel, not eaten by the root. Filed. Waiting.

---

The low door opened. The guards came — four, the professional grip, the chain. They did not rush. They walked the way men walk who have been told to bring a thing back and are not sure the thing is the same thing they brought.

The boy went. The chain went on the wrists — the walking-chain, not the running-chain. He walked. The calf left a trail on the packed earth — bright blood, new blood, the blood the floor drank as he left it.

At the low door, the boy looked up.

The galleries. The four tiers. The faces in the torch-light — the chins, the mouths, the hollows of the eyes. The pit-guards at the bottom, the working-tier above, the brokers with their ledgers, the temple with their burned-on eye-and-thorn. And at the top, barely visible in the dark, the chairs with the backs, the blood-courts, the houses.

The patron was not in the galleries. The patron was above the galleries. The patron was the thing the galleries were built under. The boy could not see the patron. The boy did not need to see the patron. The patron had seen the floor respond, and the patron was re-shelving, and the re-shelving was the thing the boy could not read yet — the number too large for the shelf, the thing that did not fit.

But the boy had seen the shape of it — the brief flash in the tall one's eyes when the tall one had looked at the brands, the pause before the tall one said *I will price you.* The tall one had known the floor might respond. The tall one had not known how much.

Nobody had known how much.

The boy went through the low door. The door closed. The corridor took him — the stone corridor, the torch-light, the tallow-smell. The guards walked at his sides. The calf left its trail.

He was not going back to the room. He was not going back to the cell. The corridor went down, not up, and the down was the direction the boy read as: *the floor gave us a number and the number needs a new shelf and the shelf is not on the tier where the boy was kept.*

The patron was moving him.

The boy counted the steps. Each one down. The body measured depth the way it measured a fever — by the weight of it, the pressing, the answer the stone gave back. The count said: *deeper.* Not higher. The room on the middle tier had been higher — the upgrade, the pricing-room, the room with the window-slit. This was not that direction. This was down.

The down was the thing the boy had read in the pit-guards' careful step and the closed low doors and the smoke-thick air. The down was the bled. The bottom tiers. The sumps, the cages, the bleeding-houses. The place where the finite things were kept.

The boy was going down.

---

The corridor opened into a gallery the boy had not seen. Lower than the cells. Lower than the Mure's tier. The air was different — wetter, the wet of stone that had been cut below the water-line, the cold of a thing the dry upper tiers had been built to keep out. The smell was the smell the boy knew from the hold — the bilge-smell, the under-smell, the smell of a place where things lived that should not live and things died that should not die.

Cages lined the gallery. Not the iron-bar cells of the upper tiers — older cages, the iron rougher, the bars set closer. Some were empty. Some held shapes the boy read without wanting to read: gray skin, wide mouths, the slick-dry look of things that had been hauled across water and brought here. Mure. Two of them, in two cages, the tubes running from their arms to the clay jars on the floor. The jars were half-full. The Mure were still.

The tubes were new. The cages were old. The Mure were the horror — the Beast rung of the corruption ladder, caged and tithed, the thing the boy could become. The boy read them the way the root read the floor — recognition, the same kind, the deep-layer connection that said *this is what the ladder's bottom looks like.*

And past the Mure-cages, in the wall: a door. Not iron-barred. Stone. The stone of the older building — the stone the tiers had been cut into, the stone that was here before the city was the city. The door had a bar. The bar was wood, heavy, iron-banded.

The guards stopped. The walking-chain came off. The stone door opened.

The room inside was not a cell. It was a chamber — cut stone, the walls carved, the ceiling vaulted the way the upper room had been vaulted but older, much older. The stone was different. Not the cut-and-lined stone of the tiers. Living stone — the stone of a cave that had been shaped, not built. The walls had marks. Not the eye-and-thorn. Older marks. The kind of marks the boy had seen in the floor's memory — the painted eye-and-thorn on plaster, the corridor with the bleeding-doors.

The chamber had a sleeping-platform, cut into the wall. A water-jug, clay, larger than the room-jug. A basin, stone, set into the floor, the water in it clean and cold. A pile of cloth — not the wrapping cloth. Real cloth. A shirt. A blanket.

And on the sleeping-platform: a strip of dried meat, a pot of the healer's paste, and a roll of clean bandage.

The healer had been here. The healer had prepared the room.

The boy stood in the doorway. The guards behind him did not enter. The tall one was not here. Nobody was here. The chamber was the chamber, and the chamber was waiting.

The boy read the chamber the way he read all things. The sleeping-platform said: *you will sleep here.* The water said: *you will drink here.* The bandage said: *you will heal here.* The carved walls said: *this room was here before the city.* The old marks said: *this room was built for the thing the city was built around.*

The boy was being moved to the floor's room.

Not the patron's room. Not the broker's room. Not the cell or the pricing-room or the middle-tier room with the window-slit. The room that belonged to the floor — the room the floor's memory had shown him, the corridor with the bleeding-doors, the place where the old blood had been kept before the city learned to sell it.

The patron had not moved the boy. The floor had moved the boy. The patron had seen the floor respond and the patron had done the thing the boy had read in the green — the thing that looked like mistake and was not. The green let the boy find the fruit tree so the boy would stay near it. The patron was putting the boy near the floor.

---

The boy went in. The stone door closed behind him. The bar went across — the sound of wood on stone, the sound of a thing locked from outside.

But the chamber was not a cell. The chamber was a room. The room had water and cloth and meat and bandage and a sleeping-platform and marks on the walls that were older than the city and older than the eye-and-thorn and older than the name.

The boy sat on the platform. The ribs bled. The calf bled. The torn muscle in the calf was the bad one — the boy could feel the damage the way he felt the weather, the barometric reading of a body that was taking inventory. The warm was behind the breastbone, banked, the ember in its ash. The warm had retreated. But the warm had left things behind.

The visions. The fragments. The room with the painted eye-and-thorn. The corridor. The face that was his face on a body that was not his. The old name in the gap.

And the floor. The warm had touched the floor and the floor had answered, and the answer was the thing the boy could not un-know: the floor was the same kind as the warm. The city had been built on the warm, or the warm's older cousin, or the warm's source — the thing the warm came from, the root under the root under the root. The boy was not standing on the city. The city was standing on *him.* On the thing in him. On the blood the floor had been waiting for.

He pressed the bandage to the calf. The paste went on — the healer's paste, the fat-and-bitter, the sting of it on the torn muscle. He bandaged the ribs. He bandaged the calf. He ate the meat.

The meat was the second real food. The body took it. The healing was already starting — the warm, behind the breastbone, doing the thing the warm did: the edges pulling, the heat going into the wound, the cost going somewhere the boy could not track. But the cost was different this time. The warm had not burned the after-things. The warm had not taken the sister's hair or the Old One's page-sound. The warm had taken from the *floor* — the old blood's memories, the layered history, the fragments the floor had given up when the warm touched it.

The warm's fuel was not always the boy's memories. The warm's fuel was whatever blood it touched. And the floor was all blood.

The boy lay on the platform. The stone was cold. The cold was the cold the boy knew — not the green's wet cold, not the cell's dry cold. The cold of a thing underground that had been underground for a long time. The old cold. The cold the room had been cut to hold.

The marks on the walls watched. Not the eye-and-thorn — the eye watched, the thorn drew. These marks were older. These marks were the marks the boy had seen in the floor's memory, the marks that came before the eye-and-thorn, the marks that the eye-and-thorn had been simplified *from.* These marks were the complicated version — the full symbol, the thing the city had reduced to an eye and a thorn because the full thing was too large to carve on every wall.

The full thing was a body. A body with a mark on its chest. A body with its mouth open. A body with blood going from its chest into the ground.

The boy closed his eyes. The gap behind the teeth held two names now — the name the Kala had given him and the name the floor had given him. The ribs were pulling. The calf was pulling. The warm was banked.

Below — somewhere below, through stone and old blood and the dark the city was built on — Marrow breathed. The boy could not hear it. The distance was too great. But the count said Marrow was there, the way the count said the Kala was three levels up and the big one was one level up. The count did not need the sound. The count was the still place's own frequency, and the still place held what it held.

The boy set the new things in the still place. The floor's response. The visions. The old name. The chamber. The marks on the walls. The usher-thing's old sound. The patron's re-shelving.

And the thing under all of it — the thing the boy had read in the warm's moment of touching the floor. The floor was waiting. The floor had been waiting a long time. The floor was not a machine the city had built. The floor was a living thing the city had been built *around*, the way the green had been built around the root-mass under the island. The city was not harvesting blood. The city was *feeding* something.

The eye-and-thorn on every wall. The eye watches. The thorn draws.

But the eye was not the city's eye. And the thorn was not the city's thorn.

The boy breathed. The warm breathed under the root. The root breathed in the blood-stone dark.

And under all of it — under the chamber, under the tiers, under the stone and the iron and the old blood — the floor breathed too.

The count ran.

The city counted back.

And the boy did not know yet — could not know yet, not with the fragments he had, not with the visions the floor had given him in pieces — that the thing the city was feeding was the same thing the boy carried behind his breastbone.

But the floor knew.

The floor had been waiting.

And the floor had just said *here.*
