The crown cut for the sixteenth low before the blind one came.

The sinew had learned the shape of his temples. Each time the band settled it opened the same two lines, the same thin burn that kept bleeding after the weight lifted. The blood was thin and dark and tasted of the stone. He had stopped wiping it away. The Skell watched it run and did not look away. Kade watched it run until the brightness in his eyes went flat, as if the seeing cost something he had not known he could spend.

Marrow's shoulder sat one degree lower than when Lorren left. The runt's hand still curled against the chest, but the fingers sometimes opened far enough to press the bars when the boy leaned his back to the iron between the cages. The count held that much. The shoulder coming down was the runt's whole argument made flesh.

The Red had not pushed since the priestling spoke. It studied.

He could feel the studying the way a body feels a hand that has not yet touched it but has already chosen where it will land. The studying was not hunger. It was not recognition. It was the attention a thing gives a shape it cannot file and has decided not to eat. The unritualized vulnerability. The body that gave without return. Lorren's standing that did not offer and did not kneel.

The Red turned the shape in the place behind the door. It did not eat it. It placed the shape beside *keep* and *mine* and the law's cage and the spaces between the bars. The placing was slow. It had patience in it, the patience the boy had taught it when he held the door against the chanting.

He put the studying on the shelf with the rest and counted what the placing cost. The hands shook longer after each low. The iron taste sat behind the missing teeth without swallowing. The membrane between himself and the Red was the same thickness as the day before, but the thickness was the thing he could no longer trust. Thickness could be learned. Thickness could be worn thin.

The Skell made the low sound in its cage when the old one came.

Not the recognition-sound it had made for the boy on the first day in the sump. A different sound. The sound a scavenger makes when something older than the kill arrives at the edge of the leavings and the leavings are no longer safe. The amber eyes tracked the passage. The body pressed lower against the stone, not in fear, in the old reading of a thing that had already decided what larger things do when they come down.

The old one came through the digger-channel slower than Lorren had come, and with no fear left in the breathing. The fear had been spent long ago. What walked in its place was the weight of a body that had carried the same knowledge longer than any crown had held it.

He was blind. The eyes were clouded white under lids that did not quite close. The hands were the seeing. They moved along the passage wall the way a man reads a thing he has read before and still does not trust to be the same between one low and the next. The fingers were gnarled and cut in the places antler-carvers cut themselves when the stone turns against the hand. He wore no temple cloth. Only old leather.

He stopped at the edge of the circle. The bled watched. The five at the sump's edge counted him the way they counted everything that came down: how much weight the body carried, how much yield it would give before it broke.

The old one did not speak the boy's name. He spoke the thing the name was standing on.

"Brannoc," the old one said. The voice was low and carried the rasp of a man who had spent his words on stone and bone. "They called me that when I still carved what the temple asked. The name stayed when the eyes did not."

He read the body. The old one stood without the shaking Lorren had carried. The standing was not strength. It was the stillness of a thing that had already paid the price of moving and had decided paying again was not worth the cost unless the paying changed the ground. The hands rested on a length of antler lashed with sinew. The bone was yellowed and marked with cuts that were not decoration.

"Speak," the boy said.

Brannoc's head turned toward the sound the way a body turns toward a thing it already knows the shape of. The clouded eyes did not move.

"You have the priestling's lever," the old one said. "The wall. The old force traveling in the blood. The Cold and the force sharing root. Now you want the thing the simplification hides. The thing the carvers were told to forget."

He did not answer.

Brannoc's hands found the antler and turned it. The cuts caught the tallow light. They were not the eye-and-thorn. They were older marks.

"There were three before you that the city counted as carriers," Brannoc said. "Not the ones the temple sings about. The ones the temple buried because the endings did not serve the price."

The Red listened. It did not move behind the door. It turned the words the way it turned everything it meant to keep.

"The first held the rim for seven turns of the low. He made the count the law the temple could not reinterpret. When the Cold came to the wall he had built, he walked out to meet it. Not to fight. To ask what the wall was for if the wall could be walked through." Brannoc's fingers moved over the cuts. "The Cold took him. Not as enemy. As the thing the wall had been built to hold. The body that came back wore his face and spoke his count, but the count was the Cold's now."

The studying took the shape and placed it beside the others. The boy felt the placing.

"The second tried to own the force the way you own the name. He spoke the three names the inner law requires and thought the speaking made the force his. The force took the names and gave them back as hunger. He stopped counting the cages because the cages were full of the thing he had become."

Marrow's hand pressed the bars harder. The shoulder climbed one degree back toward the ear.

"The third held longest. He learned the studying the way you are learning it. He placed what he could not use beside what he could. When the door thinned enough for the placing to cross, the third walked the rim and did not come back. The city says he held the Cold at bay until the end. The city lies. The Cold took him because he had stopped being the one who held and become the holding itself."

The Red listened.

He felt the listening the way a body feels the change in air when a larger thing turns its head in the dark. The studying did not stop. It took the three endings and turned them the way it had turned Lorren's standing. It placed them where it placed the vulnerability. It did not push. It filed the shape of how carriers end the way a body files the shape of a wound it means to carry without showing.

Brannoc's hands moved over the cuts. The fingers knew the marks without the eyes. The cuts were not a story told to the sighted. They were a story the hands had kept when the eyes were taken.

"The factions did not make the force," the old one said. "They found where it leaked and built customs around the leak. The temple simplified the leak into a price. The brokers priced the price. The masters ate what the price left on the floor. None of them made the thing that leaks. The thing was here before the first stone was set in the rim. The carriers are the places it finds to wear a face and a name and a count. The carriers end when the face and the name and the count stop being the thing that holds and become the thing that is held."

Marrow's hand pressed the bars harder. The shoulder climbed one degree back toward the ear. The runt had heard the ending before the words finished. The body always heard the ending first.

Kade's brightness had gone out. The devotee stared at the old one with the look of a man who has been told the god he worships will eat him and has nowhere left to put the worship that does not become the eating.

The five at the edge were quiet. The Skell made the low sound again.

Brannoc turned his head toward the third cage. The clouded eyes did not see the runt, but the hands knew the shape of a body that had been carrying a debt since the throat and had decided the debt was the only thing still worth carrying.

"The runt counts you," Brannoc said. "The runt's count is the only thing the force has not learned how to eat. That is why the runt still has a shoulder that can come down. That is why the runt's hand still finds the iron between you. When the count stops being the thing you carry and becomes the thing that carries you, the runt will be the last thing that remembers you were the one who counted."

He read the old one's body. The stillness was not peace. It was the exhaustion of a man who had carried the knowledge of three endings and had decided the carrying was the only thing left that was still his.

"Why come now," the boy said.

Brannoc's mouth moved in something that was not a smile and not a grimace.

"Because the priestling stood and did not kneel. Because the simplification is cracked and the crack lets the old knowledge through. Because you are still counting. The last one who counted like this walked into the Cold and the Cold did not have to take him. He gave himself to it the way a body gives itself to a count that has already decided what it is for. He became the thing the count was meant to hold. The Cold took the holding and left the count behind as a warning the city chose to forget."

The Red placed the last carrier's ending beside the others. The placing had a new quality. Not patience. Recognition. The studying had found the shape of its own future in the shape of what had already happened.

Brannoc reached out. The gnarled hand found the space between the bars of the third cage and touched Marrow's curled fingers without asking. The runt did not pull away. The body that had locked since the throat let the old one's touch land. The shoulder did not come down further. It held the touch the way a body holds a weight it has decided to share.

"The blind one sees the end," Brannoc said. "The one who counts still has time to decide whether the end is the thing he carries or the thing that carries him. The crown cuts. The Red studies. The count holds. These three things are true at the same time. The carriers who ended believed they could make two of them serve the third. The ending was the moment they learned the three could not be made to serve one another."

He did not leave. The old one stood with the staff in both hands and the clouded eyes turned toward the circle as if the seeing that mattered had never lived in the eyes.

"Before the simplification, the carvers kept the true marks. I carved them. I kept the old marks on antler I hid in the passages the bled use. The hands remember what the eyes were not allowed to keep."

He held the staff out. Not offering. Placing it where the boy could take it or leave it.

He took the staff. The antler was heavier than it looked. The cuts under his fingers were lines that crossed and circled and ended in points that had no name in the temple's counting. His fingers read what the eyes of the man who had carved them could no longer see.

"The first carrier's mark is the one that ends in the open hollow," Brannoc said. "He asked what the wall was for. The mark ends because the asking was the thing the Cold answered. The second carrier's mark is the one that circles back on itself. He tried to own the hunger. The mark circles because the hunger owned the one who tried to own it. The third carrier's mark is the one that stops in the middle of the line. He learned the studying. The mark stops because the studying did not need the line to finish."

The Red turned the marks the way it turned the endings. The studying took the open hollow and the circling line and the stopped mark and placed them where it placed Lorren's standing. The placing was the same slow patience. The Red was learning the shape of the thing that had ended the carriers who had carried before the boy had a name for the counting.

Brannoc's hand found the gap where the three teeth were gone. The gnarled fingers touched the warm dark behind the missing teeth without asking, the way the old one had touched Marrow's curled hand.

"The gap is where the name lives," the old one said. His fingers stayed in the warm. "The carriers who ended had names in the gap. The names were the last thing the force took. When the name was gone the count had nothing left to hold the body to the one who had carried it. The gap stayed. The name did not."

He read the touch. The old one's fingers knew the shape of a gap that had once held a name and now held the deciding the Red had not yet crossed. The touch was not threat. It was the record of what the gap could become if the studying learned how to take what the gap held. The Red turned the shape of the touch the way it turned the marks on the antler.

Brannoc withdrew the hand. The touch left the warmth behind it.

"The priestling gave you the lever. I have given you the endings the wall was built to hide. The lever and the endings are the same ground seen from two sides. The carriers end when they stop being the one who sees the ground and become the ground the others walk on."

He turned then. The old one walked back into the digger-channel without waiting for an answer. The staff was left in the boy's hands. The tapped sound of the old one's leaving was the only thing that answered the question the boy had not asked aloud.

The bled sat with the endings.

The Red sat with the endings behind the door.

He sat with the crown cutting and the count holding and the shelf taking the new weight. The weight was not the knowledge. The weight was the shape the knowledge made in the body that had to carry it: the three carriers who had stopped being the count and become the counted. The first had asked what the wall was for. The second had tried to own the hunger. The third had learned the studying and walked into what the studying had prepared for him.

The studying behind the door did not push. It turned the three shapes the way a hand turns a thing it means to use when the time comes. The time had not come. The studying was patient. The studying had learned patience from the boy who held the door. Now it had the marks. The marks were the shape of how the patience had ended before.

He put his hand over Marrow's where the old one's hand had been.

The count held.

Eleven cages. Ten bodies. One empty.

The empty was still his. The endings the blind one had given were the shape the empty could take if the studying learned how to fill it with the thing the carriers had become when they stopped counting.

He felt the Red learning the shape.

The studying was the thing he feared more than the pushing.

Because the Red that knew how carriers ended was the Red that was learning how to make the ending its own, and the ending was the thing that did not need a door once the studying had crossed.

The crown cut. The blood ran. The count held.

The blind one had given the shape. The shape was the only thing the city had not yet learned how to simplify away.
