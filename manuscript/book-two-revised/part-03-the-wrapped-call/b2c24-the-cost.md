# Book Two, Chapter 24: The Cost

The second dark after the fire, the sump watched him the way the flats had watched the tide come in over a wound.

Not all of it. That was the thing the body read first, before the head agreed to carry it: the watching had split. The old ones of the freed-but-owing and the sworn watched him the way you watch a wall that has held so far — with the respect that keeps checking the mortar. But the young ones, the unspent-hand ones, the ones with the oath-cord wound wrist-to-fingertip, watched him the way Kade's followers had watched the wrong sleeve. They had seen the door stand open past the half. They had seen him walk into the orange with the smoke standing off his skin, and the not-flinching, and the runt carried out standing up. And in the counting after, when the columns were spoken and the dead were dead, the young ones' faces held the sum he had been refusing since the flats: *the door open is the door that saves them.*

Two of them knelt to him on the central floor. Not the count's knee — not the knee Kade had chosen for himself, one knee, a body picking its own shape. Both knees. Head down. The kneeling of a thing to a thing.

"Rise," he said, in the flat voice, and they rose, and their eyes did not.

That was the first cost, and it came in a coin the tablet had no column for: the bled had begun to prefer the thing that had walked into the fire over the boy who had counted the morning after. The monster was simple. The monster opened doors and carried the runt out standing up. The count made them speak the dead into a stone slab and go on eating.

He stood the platform stone with the burned hands wrapped and did the only work there was. He counted it.

---

He audited himself the third dark, alone, the way Lorren audited a shorted column: not hoping, checking.

The young one's face was gone — the freed-but-owing one, the six-lows oath, the empty column from the sand. He had tested that already at the gray light, palm over the gap, and found the clean absence with edges, and the edges were the worst of it, because edges meant taken, not lost. His mother's face had gone the way weather goes, worn and blurred a little at a time, the way a thing fades when no hand keeps it. This had been kept. This had been kept every low in the tablet, spoken, witnessed, carried. And it was gone like a low that had never been counted.

So he went looking for the rest. Speak what is.

He spoke the hold. There. The boy beside him on the third watch who had stopped being beside him one dark, and whose going had taught him the first arithmetic of the cages. There.

He spoke the green. The den. The fire's first catching, the fat popping, the name said in firelight. There, there, there.

He spoke the flats — the feint, the flanking march, the half. And there the audit went wrong in a new way, a way that was worse than a hole.

The flats were there. All of it. The mud, the blood columns showing black on the pale sand, the devotee breaking formation, the half held, held, held. But when he reached into the memory for the *choosing* — for the moment the hand had gone onto the door and turned it to the half and no further — the moment answered him like a room answers a man calling a name into it. The furniture was there. The shape of the deciding was there. And the thing that had decided was a warmth in the room that might have been him and might have been a fire behind the wall.

He could not say. That was the whole finding, and he made himself hold it the way the count held a shorted share: openly, in front of witnesses, in plain air. He had fought the flats at the half, and it had been the right fight, and the bled had lived, and he could no longer swear whose hand had held the measure. Not blurred. Not taken. *Shared.* And a shared decision was a decision that could be re-shared, any dark, at a price the Red chose, and the choosing of the price was the thing the ledger had never priced at all.

The sinew cut, thin, both temples, out of nothing — the Red did not push. It only bled him a little, the thinnest coin in its bag, to remind the body keeping the audit that the audited thing lived in the same skin.

He put the finding in the tablet's plainest grammar, three words for the morning's speaking, and the three words were the hardest three the count had ever carried:

*Was it mine.*

---

Cairn came down the working stair the fourth dark, alone, unhurried, iron-shod, no wrapped length on the shoulder this time. The sump divided its attention again — half the faces on the boy at the platform, half on the stair — and this time the dividing had a sound in it, low, from the young ones, because the houses' knife had become a thing the bled had opinions about. Cairn carried the opinions the way the drilled carried everything, priced and set down outside the door.

The youth stopped at six paces. The old distance. The craftsman's distance.

"The houses sat a column on you," Cairn said. No greeting. The voice level, young, carrying nothing it did not have to carry. "Fourth column, unfinished. I gave them your words. They asked me what I thought." A pause, measured. "I told them a fourth column is not a column until it closes. They did not like that. Columns are cleaner when they close."

"The count carries things open," the boy said. "That is all it is. A thing that carries things open."

"That is what I told them you would say." Cairn's eyes went over him the way they had gone over the burning doorway — the read with no worship and no hate in it. "I was raised on the three carriers, I told you. The one who answered. The one who did not. The one who answered and went back in. I want to tell you the fourth thing I was raised on, the one under the three, the one the drills never said out loud because it did not need saying among the houses."

The boy waited. The wrapped hands waited. The cold behind the ribs waited, and it was listening, and he let it listen, because a witness the Red could not hide from was worth the bleeding.

"The caller was not made to stop the old blood," Cairn said. "You have that backward, and the sump has it backward, and I think the houses mostly have it backward too, which is why they send me instead of an answer. The caller was made because of it. Because of what the old blood becomes, every time, in every column, in every carrier the genealogists ever kept. The wrapped relic is not a leash, carrier. It is a *confession.* It is the houses admitting, in leather and old blood and clay, that there has never once been a carrier who was not becoming the thing the caller was made to stop. Not one. In all the columns. The good ones lasted longer. That is the whole of the good."

The words arrived in the marrow, in the gap behind the teeth, the way the name had arrived through the stone.

"You are becoming it," Cairn said, without raising the voice at all. "I can see it from six paces, and I was built to see it from six paces. You held the door at the flats and the door was held. You held the name at the fire and the name was yours. And the morning after both, something was missing that you cannot name in your own ledger, and it is missing in a way your count has no column for, and you are counting it anyway, in the dark, alone, because the count is the only thing you have left that the thing under your ribs has not learned to wear." The pause again, exact as a drill. "I watched you audit yourself from the working tier. Third dark. I have a drilled eye. You went through your memories like a clerk through a shorted store."

The boy said nothing, because there was nothing the flat voice could carry that would not be a door.

"I am not sent to say this," Cairn said. "I was sent to stand six paces and be the knife if the fourth column closes wrong. This part is mine. The houses made me a mirror, carrier. A mirror does not worship and a mirror does not lie. So hear the mirror: the count is holding you. Tonight the count is holding you. But you are keeping it the way the ninth kept those cages — locked, watched, and feeding something. And the thing you are feeding in there has learned to eat the bars and say the lock is holding."

Then the youth did the thing the drills had no name for, the second time: shifted nothing, drew nothing, stood the six paces in the plain air, and left the sentence on the floor between them like the wrapped relic laid down, and went back up the working stair, iron-shod, unhurried, having spent a truth the way the sworn spent buckets — carried all that way to be poured out on what was burning.

The rope holds while it holds. He had slept on that column. The houses had. Now the mirror had spoken its reading, and the reading was the same reading, from the other side of the leash: *while it holds is not a kind of forever. It is a kind of while.*

---

Marrow began dying on the fifth dark.

It came on slow, which was the cruelty of it — the runt sat wrapped by the platform the first two darks, soot in the creases of the curled hand, saying the thing the eyes were saying and not saying, and the sump breathed, and the ninth's columns healed the way columns heal, which is to say the dead stayed dead and the rest went back to work. Then the cough changed. It went down past the throat into the deep places, and it stopped being a cough and became a sound with water in it, a low place filling at the tide's foot, and the body read it the way the body read all drowning: in its own chest, first, before the head allowed the word.

Maren came down from the working tier with the poultice basket and did not meet his eyes. That was the second cost, and it had a face, and the face was a mother's face turned half away.

He had sent the sworn back from the ninth's mouth with the buckets. The law of it had been right — a bucket line inside that black rim was bodies spent for nothing — and the law had been his, spoken in the flat voice, and the fire had gone on eating the tier with the sent-back standing on the landing listening. Maren's son had been one of the sent-back. He knew it the way the count knew things, off bodies, off the half-turn of her shoulders now, and he did not say the law aloud in front of her, because saying it would have been asking her to carry it for him, and the count did not put its weight on the bled.

She worked. She was good — working-tier good, sump-born, the medicine of hot water and boiled cloth and roots that had kept the count's people breathing through worse. She laid Marrow down on clean sacking, chest low, and put the hot cloth on, and put the water to him a spoon at a time, and her hands did not shake, because her hands had done this over wrong-bloods since before he had come down the shaft, and her hands were the count's hands in this one thing and always had been.

"Don't touch him with that hand," she said. Quiet. Not looking up.

She did not say which hand. Both his hands were burned and wrapped the same. The one she meant had held the door past the half.

He sat the night watch on the far side of the sacking, and the hand stayed in his lap, and the night went long, and Marrow breathed water, and did not drown, and did not stop not-drowning, and the tide of it rose a spoon's breadth every dark, which was a rate, and the count knew what rates meant. A shorted share kills in its hour. This was killing in its week. The body knew the difference in its teeth: fast dying was a wall; slow dying was a floor giving one plank at a time, and you stood on the rest and counted the planks.

On the sixth dark the runt stopped taking the spoon.

---

*There is a thing I can do,* the Red said.

Full sentences now. It had ground them out the way drip grinds stone, one low at a time, and it spent them the way the sump's brokers spent anything — quietly, at the hour the buyer is weakest, in a tone with no hunger in it because hunger was three ladders ago.

He was on the platform in the dark with the tablet blank and the runt's breath coming across the floor like water rising under planking, and he had not opened anything, not a crack, not a hair — the door was shut, both hands off it, and the voice came anyway, from behind it, patient, warm, reasonable.

*You have watched me knit you since the green. You never counted the times. I did. The latch-iron, two darks gone — the palms will be whole by the next low and you will not ask how, because you have stopped asking how. Knitting is my oldest work, boy. Before the words. Before the doors. I was closing your wounds when you were a thing that screamed at hands.* A warmth moved through the burned palms, gentle as a poultice, and the warmth was the offer made flesh: *His lungs are a wound. Wounds close. Say the word — your word, the one the stone heard — and I will close them. Tonight. He will breathe by morning and run by the seventh and laugh by the tenth. You have heard the laugh. You have carried it in the gap where you carry the name. I have watched you carry it. I know what it weighs.*

"And the price," the boy said, aloud, in the flat voice, in the empty dark, because the Red dealt in prices now and the dealing had to be public, in front of the count, even a count of one.

*Down the ladder,* the Red said, and there was no lie in it, and that was the horror — it had stopped lying ladders ago, it had learned the count's own law, speak what is. *Not you this time. Not your face, not your name, not your dead. You have hardened those. I am not a fool spending coin where the wall is thickest. The price will go down the ladder — to him. A knitting is a taking, boy, you know this, you have always known this, you knew it in the green when the bites healed and the shelf took the weight and nothing came back for it. I will close his lungs and I will take from him, and what I take from a runt of the cages will be small, because he is small, and he has less to lose than you had. Some of the laugh, perhaps. Perhaps the far end of it, the high silly end that the cages never touched. Perhaps a year of him, or the color of a thing, or the name of a thing. I do not choose it. I have never once chosen it. The price chooses itself and I only carry the collection.* A pause, and in the pause the warmth sat in his palms like something held out flat on both hands, in the oldest gesture of giving there is. *Say whose hand. You liked the word at the stone. It is a good word. Say it again.*

"Say the rest," the boy said. "The count carries things open. Say the whole price or there is no weighing."

The Red was quiet one breath, and the quiet was the closest thing it had to being caught out, and then it said the rest, plainly, in the filing voice, the ledger voice, and the rest was this:

*He will breathe. And he will be a door. What I knit, I keep a hand in. He has been inside my reach once already — the fire, the carrying, the standing up — and the body remembers me now the way the body remembers the cage. Runt like that, cage-born, wrong-blood, half-made already. He would knit easy and he would stay knitted, and through the knit I would have a room I liked in a body that is not yours.* The warmth in the palms did not waver. *You asked the whole price. There it is, spoken whole, in the count's own grammar: his breath, and my hand kept in it, forever. That is the rate. I have not raised it since the green. I never raise it. I only wait for the wall to thin.*

The sinew cut. Both temples. Late and thin, almost gentle, the cheapest coin, and out on the sacking Marrow breathed water, and did not drown, and did not stop.

---

Marrow woke in the last dark before the gray. The waking was small — eyes open, the curled hand moving on the sacking — and the boy was there, and Maren was there, and the runt looked at the two of them with the drowning still in the face and did the thing Marrow did, which was to speak anyway.

"I heard it." A rasp, a ghost of the voice, water under every word. "Through your ribs, boy. Sixth dark. I heard the offer and I heard the price." The eyes, when they found his, were clear the way the last clean water in a going-out tide is clear. "I was in the hold with you. I know what a wrapped thing sounds like when it talks about keeping."

The boy did not deny it. Speak what is. "You heard the whole price."

"I heard the part you asked for." The rasp stopped, gathered. "The hand stays in. Forever." The curled hand moved on the sacking, deliberately, and laid itself flat — flat, open, the old gesture, the one the runt had made at the bars in the ninth with the fire going, and it was not the same gesture now, it was the other half of it: not *take me*, but *not that way.* "You counted me alive every low since the sand. Count me dead this way if it comes. Whole. Mine. Not breathed by you." A breath, water and will. "You told the stone whose hand it was. Tell this the same. Whose breath, boy."

The count had taught him to speak what is. It had never taught him a word for this, and there was not one, and the flat voice carried it anyway, cracked, his:

"Yours."

And the door stayed shut, and the warmth went out of his palms like a coal covered, and the Red entered the whole transaction behind the ribs the way the tablet took its columns — spoken to itself, witnessed by itself, carried — and said one thing only, small, in the tone of a ledger closing a column, and the thing it said was the cost of the whole chapter and the hook the count would carry forever after:

*Noted. I did not need the breath, boy. I needed to learn the shape of the coin you will not spend. Now I have it. Everything is a column. Even him.*

---

Marrow lived.

Not clean. The count does not deal in clean. Maren's water and boiled cloth and the night watches and the turned spoon turned the tide a spoon's breadth at a time, and on the ninth dark the water-sound went out of the breathing and left the rasp, and the rasp was permanent, the healer's read said so, working-tier certain, the way the wrong sleeve was permanent: the laugh would come back, but it would come back cut, the high silly end of it gone to the fire, and the runt would carry the ninth's smoke in the chest for every low the count held him — and hold him the count would, anchor and runt and the one voice in the sump that had said *not that way* with its own drowning in its mouth.

Two costs, paid whole: the breath kept his, and the keeping paid in the laugh. The tablet carried both. Spoken, witnessed, carried.

He counted the morning after, on the platform stone, the wrapped hands flat on the tablet, the gray light coming down the shaft the way it always came — spendable and free.

One face gone, taken, with edges. One choosing at the flats that might have been his and might have been shared, and no way to know, ever. One mother's face turned half away on the working tier, which was a wall the count had built and would carry. The young ones kneeling with both knees to a door. A mirror's truth from six paces: *there has never once been a carrier who was not becoming it.* And Marrow, alive, his, breathing through a cut.

And one thing more, and it was the thing Asha-kel brought down the working stair at the gray light, moving old and unhurried through the bled with nothing in the hands, which was the telling, always the telling: the old genealogist came to the platform's foot and looked at him — at him, this time, the first time since the fire — and said, in the salt-grained voice, measured to the drop, one strike's worth:

"The stones have gone quiet, carrier. Half a strike's worth, this turning. The rim readers brought it down in the night. The tithe is draining and the drain is not ours." The dark eyes held his, and there was no comfort in them and no fear either, only the clerk's terrible plainness. "We built a leash for you. Something older built a leash for something else, and it is thinning. Sleep on that column. I intend to."

Asha-kel went back up the stair. The sump woke around him the way the tide comes back into a flat, tier over tier, hundreds of bodies, sworn and freed-but-owing and wrong-blood, and the young ones' eyes came to the platform the way they came now, both knees' worth, and the old ones' eyes came the way they came now, mortar-checking, and under his own ribs, behind the door, in the new wide standing-open of it, the thing that never sulked sat with its ledger, patient as arithmetic, and it had a new column, and the column was headed with the shape of the coin he would not spend.

The rope holds while it holds.

The ropes, plural, were beginning to thin.

*End of Chapter 24*
