# Book Three, Chapter 10: The Invoice

Two darks to the winter turn, and the fires came up the warm water.

The body found it before the rim did. The boy woke in the gray before the tiers woke, out of a sleep that had not been sleep, and the waking was a checking now, had been a checking since the sand — the old thumb crossing the old knuckle-bars, going back to the warm room behind his eye where the mother's face lived — and the wall was there. The wall was there again. The smile that had come with the old name every dark of his life since the black shore was spent, one line, exact, and the checking found the smooth place where it had been, and the body did what a body does with a wall it has priced: it logged the wall, and it stood up, and it went to the water, because the water was where the count was kept, and grief was an entry like any other entry, and the flat voice made it kneeling-height flat, alone, in the cold of the ninth stair:

*Entered: the wall stands. The trade took its line and the trade does not give change. The face I have left turns in the dark. The turning is mine. And the dark is shorter than it was, because the turn is coming, and the book does not keep itself.*

Then he was at the rim, and Marrow was at the shoulder, where Marrow was, the counted breaths running their line, one spent, one owed, and the runt did not spend words. He turned the lamp down the water, and the boy read the shape whole before the head dressed it — the frost road's lying was part of the shape: the road had held all dark, white on the black water, rim to hull, edge to edge, and Siv's hull sat at its anchor at the road's end with its one lamp doused, queueing, patient as its freight. And beyond the far light, out where the water stopped being the city's and started being the world's, the horizon held a wrongness the boy had seen once before, in the story of another water: fires.

Not one fire. Not a boat's light. A line of them, low on the water, strung along the dark like entries down a ruled page, and as the boy stood the rim-stone and counted, the counting running out of him flat and unwilled, the line of fires moved. North. Toward the light.

Marrow's lamp did not shake. Marrow did not let it. "Nine fires," the runt said, aimed, counted, the three beats in it. "Kade's two make nine hulls. None of them fishing. None of them hailing. They're coming up on the far light like it's a door they mean to be first at."

The boy stood the rim with the going-book under his arm — he carried it in the body now, and it weighed more than it weighed — and out past the lanterns the winter water lay black and ruled and patient, and the fires came on down the warm dark, and the flat voice ran the finding under everything before the head had a cloth to dress it in:

*Entered: a fleet is a queue that has not been priced yet.*

---

The council took it at the rim in the gray light, and the taking was two readings, and the two readings did not agree, and the not-agreeing was the politics of the whole coast in one morning.

Maren's chalk counted the hulls as the watch called them: nine, deep-keeled, warm-water lines, and two scutt-burned and mended with green wood — hulls that had been in a fire and had not died of it, mended not like a navy but like refugees. "Those hulls are loaded past their lines," Maren said, and the chalk did not move while he said it, which from Maren was a shout. "Full holds, full decks. Whatever they've come for, they've come to stay till they get it."

"Then the houses enter the prices," Asha-kel said, flat, standing where the houses stood, at the shoulder of the discussion, and the flat was doing the work the flat always did, laying the ledge for heavier stone. "Price one: they're spenders. The warm water's sayings have reached this rim for a moon — the burning lines, the young blood waking, the strong pricing the weak in the old currency. Nine hulls of that, landing on a coast three darks — two darks — from the turn, on a city whose tiers already believe the Cold's ferry sits in their harbor. The tiers will not read those hulls as strangers. They'll read them as the tithe." "Price two: they were told. A fleet does not find the black isles. There is no chart of this water that a warm hand would trust. Somebody gave them the address, as somebody gave the hull its address, and the houses have noticed that everything given an address this season arrives at the same door."

The boy said nothing. The flat voice said it inside, kneeling-height, in the room under the room: *Entered: the address is the door. The door is me. The sayings were a bill of lading. Freight is delivered by a seller, and the seller does not care which buyer unloads the crate.*

Serl came up the stair while they stood there, and priced the fires with the row-craft's oldest instrument, which was a long look, and gave the finding in her own diction, plain:

"That's not a war, carrier." She said it to the boy and to the water together. "A war burns as it goes. A market comes to the door with its purse. Nine fires in line, no burning, holding formation — that's a market that means to be let in before it thinks about taking. And a market that arrives two darks before a term, at a coast where an account is standing open—" her eyes went, briefly, to the hull at the end of the frost road, "—hasn't come for fish."

"The count answers at the turn," the boy said. "In its own hand. That ruling stands. It was made before these fires were lit and it will stand after them." He looked down the stair, where the queues were already forming wrong, tier-people carrying their children and their slate-tallies up toward the rim on a finding that had already run the stair on its own feet and put on its worst face to do it. "But rulings don't feed queues. Maren: the stores stand the turn twice, or they don't. Price it true."

"They don't," Maren said, and the chalk moved at last, and the moving was the worst entry of the morning. "Not with nine hulls of buyers sitting on the water. Salt enters it plain: if those hulls land hungry, there isn't a ledger on this coast that holds."

"Then the count prices the landing before the landing prices itself," the boy said. "Serl. Row-craft. If it's a market — what does a coast do?"

Serl looked at him, and the look had the thumb-in-the-seam quality she brought to a saying she was testing whole before she spent it. "A coast does what you did on the sand, carrier. It stands the water with its hands open. Coasts don't grab. But a coast also doesn't let the market set the ground." She turned her face back to the fires. "And a market two darks from a term has a seller in it somewhere, and sellers carry invoices, and the invoice is the whole game. First one to name the invoice owns the trade. Find the invoice before their boat touches your sand, or the trade will be dressed before you know it's a trade."

*Entered,* the flat voice said. *The invoice is named already. It is a page, ruled for my hand, one stroke short of whole, and it has been in my keeping since the sand. The question the row-woman is asking is not what the invoice is. It is who wrote the address on it. Find the hand. The hand knows the ground.*

The boy took the stair down two turnings, alone, and the stair said what it said to the one man who could hear it, and the boy went where the stair said to go.

---

The cell off the third gallery was as cold as the tale-floor, and the door stood open, as the door always stood open, and Brannoc sat at his stone table with both hands flat on the slab of wall-stone, and the blind face was turned to the stair before the boy was off the last step, because the stair told him everything, and this dark the stair had told him the fires too.

"Nine hulls," the old man said. "The watch-talk ran the gallery while you were at the rim. The tiers have priced them as the tithe already; the under-priest's house hasn't said it, and hasn't said it loud, three times since first queue. You've come down here to ask me a question you already hold, boy. So I'll save us the dark. Ask it anyway. Some entries have to be made in the body's own voice or they don't take."

"The hull," the boy said. "Siv's hull. Somebody gave it the address. A fleet doesn't find the black isles and a carrier four seasons from home doesn't queue inside a lantern line she's never seen unless the ground was drawn for her. The hand that gave that address knows this coast. Knows where the buyer stands." The cold of the cell lay on the going-book under his arm like a hand. "And the fires down the water were given the same address, and I've been standing the rim pricing hulls and stores, and the whole time the finding the body made was this: the only map of this coast that a warm hand would trust is not a chart. It's a story. It's the old stories, true-shape, and there's one man in this city who has held them thirty years, and one house that has sung beside him for thirty years."

The fingers on the stone made the small motion. Twice. The reading-twice motion. And then the hands went still, both of them, flat, and the stillness was not the wall's stillness, which held; it was a mason's stillness, which is a man standing in the wreck of his own work and finding it sound.

"I told it singing," Brannoc said. Level. "Thirty years I buried the true stories inside the cant, line under line, where only a reader could find them. And I priced my singing safe, boy, all thirty years, on the one finding a blind man prices last: that I was the only reader left." The blind face turned toward the third-tier stair, exact, the small blind turning that missed nothing. "The under-priest can read. Has always been able to. He stood the hymns beside me dark by dark for thirty years with his mouth full of the cant and his ear in the lines under it, and I never priced it, because a man who tells himself the lamp-smoke is eating his eyes also tells himself the man beside him is only singing." The thumb came down once on the slab, hard, a graver's stroke, and the stroke was aimed at himself. "He didn't need to send a chart down the water. He only needed to stand the evening cant facing the wrong wall — toward the water, boy, instead of the tier — and sing the buried lines up out of the cant into the wind, where the tithe-boats' exchange would carry them down the warm water hymn by hymn, to every house that once knew the old trade. The stories were the map. The map was the address. I made the map, and he mailed it, and I have been sitting in this cell for a moon telling myself the sayings reached the warm water on their own."

The boy stood in the cold with the book under his arm, and the finding went through him, whole, as a crack goes through wall-stone — through, not around — and the finding was worse than treason, because treason has a want in it, and the boy read the under-priest whole shape first, as the body read anything: an old man who had kept a starving city calm through the worst winters by managing what it feared — the same burial Brannoc had made, for the same reason: the tiers burned the last man who told them plain — and who had heard, in the buried lines, a doctrine older and colder than his own: that the settlement collects the line, not the tiers. That the Cold's account, falling due at the turn, could be aimed. That if the line's heir stood on the buyer's ground when the term came, the collection would spend the boy and pass the city over, the way a tide passes over the piling it has a post for.

The under-priest had not sold the city. He had priced the boy for the city, from inside the city's own hymns, using a blind man's thirty-year craft as his couriers — and the boy's body flinched whole with the recognition before the head could dress it, because the shape of the thing was a shape the body had known since the cage: a hand that fed you and aimed you at once, and the two never once in the same stroke distinguishable.

*Entered,* the flat voice made itself say. *The hand in the telling is named. And enter the shape under it, whole: the same dark the under-priest's cant reached the warm water, the maker cut one stroke in nine hundred years of closed books. The white does not distinguish the line and the author. Two hands wrote the same address, one in hymn-cant and one in stone, and both of them aimed at the same door, and neither of them asked the other. That is not conspiracy. That is what a term is: the date every debt in the account comes due at once, and the debts walk in together.*

"Why," the boy said aloud, and it was not a question for Brannoc, but Brannoc answered it anyway, because the stair had told him thirty years of the under-priest's evenings too.

"Because he loves them," the old man said, simply, and the saying lay in the cold like a laid stone. "You'll stand in front of him today, boy, and you'll see it, and I'm telling you first so it doesn't take you unaware the way it took me. The man has buried more truth than the doctrine ever asked of him to keep a riot off the third tier, and he watched the famine queues and priced them child by child, and somewhere in the dark he priced this: one boy against a city, and the boy not even a tier-boy. He'll have a cant for it. He'll have made it righteous in the singing, the way I made the stories safe. That's what craft is, boy, mine and his: making a thing stand in a shape it couldn't hold on its own." The blind face came back to him. "Enter him true. Not as a villain; the stories have no entry for him either. Enter him as a keeper who thought he'd found the one thing a keeper can keep and let go of on purpose."

---

They brought the under-priest to the tally-floor at the middle dark, in front of the slates, in front of the houses, in front of the tier-people the man had spent his life calming, and the boy stood the count's ground and did not sit, because this was not a ruling that could be made sitting.

The man came up the floor in his gray house-cloth, small, older than the boy had ever read him, and the stair's saying about him was plain now that the body knew: he was worn to the same shape Brannoc was worn to, the shape of a man who has carried a buried thing one dark too long. He did not deny. Denying was a wall-trade, and the under-priest had spent his life in the line's own country, and he stood at the spacing a man stands at when the entry is already made, and the boy asked him the only question the count had.

"Did you stand the cant toward the water."

"Every dark since the statement," the under-priest said. His voice was a cant-voice, worn smooth, and it did not shake, and the not-shaking was a craft too, and the boy priced it. "The doctrine's whole trade is knowing what the tiers can carry and what they can't. What they can't carry is a collection with no name on it. So I gave the collection a name. I sang it the only true thing I hold: the line, the floor, the heir. And the old trade did the rest. The buyers heard the account was open. The buyers came. It queues, count — everything your coast has become queues — and I did not bring a war to this water. I brought a market, to a coast that has a counter, in the two darks the counter has left to price it. That was my ruling. It stands in my body."

And then the man knelt.

He knelt on the tally-floor stone, and the kneeling was not the tiers' kneeling, and it was not Siv's kneeling either, though the boy's skin flinched to the shape of both before the head could rule them out. It was the oldest kneeling on the floor of it: the spacing-grief, one pace off, the mourning that does not spend. He was offering himself to the keeping. Keep me instead, the kneeling said, in the only language a keeper has ever spoken. Keep me, and the collection spends me, and the tiers stand.

The room held its breath. The houses did not move. Maren's chalk did not move. And the boy stood the count's ground with two laws tearing at him in one body, the spender's law — *let it go, at a price* — and the keeper's law — *hold, forever* — and priced, whole shape first, what the man was actually selling, and the pricing came back flat and terrible: the under-priest was not offering a martyrdom. He was offering a trade. His body for the city's calm, entered before the turn, so that when the fleet and the floor and the term came due together, the tiers would already have their story: the doctrine spent itself for them. He was trying to write the last entry of the old city in his own hand.

"No," the boy said.

The flat voice said it, through him, kneeling-height flat, the floor's own register, and the room took it the way the flats take a load-bearing stone.

"The count does not spend people. That law was made on this floor before either of us stood it, and it is the only law on this coast that doesn't keep you, and it doesn't keep them, and it will not be unmade in the two darks we have left because the alternative is older and warmer. Entered, and entered plain: no keeping. You knelt in the wrong spacing, priest. There's nothing here that wants a kept priest." He let one breath fall, white, small, and then he gave the ruling, and the ruling was aimed, the way Marrow aimed the three beats, counted, so it would land whole: "You'll stand the water at the turn. At the sand, in the open, entered, owing, counted, where the tiers can see you — the hand that gave the address, standing in the buyer's ground when the freight it addressed comes down the water. Whatever the collection prices, priest, the coast will see it price the man who aimed it. That's the count's whole ruling. You wanted to be an entry. Be a witness."

The man rose out of the kneeling by degrees, the keeping still in his knees, and the boy watched him take the ruling the way a wall takes weather — facing it, bearing it, giving it nothing — and there was no reading, in the worn face, whether the standing was a mercy or a sentence, and the boy entered that too: some prices aren't dressed until they're paid.

*Entered: a villain who loves, entered true. The count does not get to keep this one either.*

---

The fleet's boat came at the middle of the second dark, and it came under a seller's hail.

That was the finding the rim read before the boat read anything else: one lamp, lit, doused, aimed at the rim, the old trade's oldest courtesy, from a war-keel nine hulls deep in buyers. The market had hailed. And the boy stood the black sand below the rim-stones as he had stood it for Siv — hands open at his sides, Asha-kel behind him with the houses' flat, Kade's two facing the city, backs to all of it, the law of the standing — and the frost road lay white on the water to his left, and Siv's hull sat its anchor at the road's end, and the fleet's boat came in along the road's far side, deliberately, the way a buyer walks a market's marked lane: because the line's hull was in the road.

The boy watched the fleet's boat shorten the distance to a hull that had queueing rights a moon older than its own, and the boat slowed, and held, and took station behind the line's dead — nine hulls of warm-water spenders, holding their place in a queue behind a gray boat with one doused lamp and no living flag — and something went through the boy that was not fear and not anger, an old body-knowing from before the count, before the crown, from the cage: even wolves queue at a ground that has a counter. Especially wolves.

The man who came over the gunwale and down the wet sand was thin, burned brown, wearing the remains of a book-house keeper's coat with the house's mark cut out of the breast and a fire's edge burned up the left side of it, and he came up the sand at the pace the boy had priced in Siv and priced again now: a man who had arrived all his life at doors that did not want him, and kept his pace anyway. He stopped at the spacing — three strides, the seller's whole arithmetic in one number. And he set his hands open at his sides, the mirror come again without the knowing, the old courtesy still living in the muscle of a man whose whole warm side had burned.

"I am Osk," he said. "I kept the third book-house at Shennan Reach. The fires priced my house out of me a moon ago, and the sayings priced my sayings out of me too — you're in them, count, three ways, a beast, a pit, a line come home. What's left of the book-houses is on that water. What's left of a lot of things is on that water." His voice was a ledger-voice, and the boy heard his own flat in it, the terrible cousinship of two people who had both survived by counting. "We burned, count. That's the invoice nobody wrote and everybody paid. The old blood woke in the young ones and there was no law among them, and the law we had was coin, and coin is a wall-invention, and the walls burned. So the keepers of what's left priced the one account still standing open on any water, and it's yours, and the term is the turn, and we have not come to fight you." One degree off the flat, and the degree was the first true thing, the same break Siv's flat had broken on the sand. "We've come to stand the collection. Every spender on those hulls has priced it the same: the trade wakes at the term or the trade dies at the term, and either way we stand in the buyer's ground when it does, because the only thing worse than what the collection might spend is being spent from the outside of it again."

"You were given an address," the boy said. "Say who held the pen."

"The hymns," Osk said, plain, spent. "You know it or you'd not have asked. The cant came down the water with the tithe-boat exchange, the old stories sung out true, line under line, and every keeper who heard it read the ground out of it — the black isles, the rim, the kept rows, the heir. Somebody in your city wanted the account aimed, count. It's aimed." He looked, once, at the frost road, at the hull at its end, and the burned face did a thing the boy priced whole before the head dressed it: grief, the keeping-grief, nine hundred years deep, worn by a stranger. "The line's last carrier queueing ahead of the fires that burned the line's book-houses. There's the old world in one road."

The Red stirred at the border of him, forward, warm, and it did not want the book this dark. It wanted the fleet. The boy felt it price the nine hulls with a slow terrible delight, entry-shaped: *Entered, boy. Buyers are made of currency. Warm water, warm blood, an army of spenders standing in our ground at term — the largest entry ever aimed at this body, and they've hailed us. Say the word and we'll go down the water and make the collection early.* And through the Red's warmth, at the same stroke, the frost stood, and the frost did not want anything — it held. It held the road. It held the stillness of the two hundred rows facing the water. It held the hull's one doused lamp in its keeping, exact, and the boy stood between the two of them on the black sand, spender and keeper, wanting and holding, and for one breath the whole trade of his body was the whole trade of the water: nine fires of hunger queueing at one road of stillness.

"The count's ruling stands," the boy said, and the flat voice carried it down the sand and out over the flats. "The count answers at the turn. In its own hand. No fleet stands this collection, and no fleet spends it early. You queue, or you burn your fuel and go home to your fires — the ground's price is the same either way, and the ground is mine to price." He closed his hands, at last, and the closing was the ruling's second half, aimed: "But enter this, Osk, and carry it to your hulls, because it's the only entry on this coast that's free. There's a page in the book under my arm ruled for a hand, and there's a stroke already cut in it by a maker who doesn't distinguish the line and the author, and I am both. When the term comes, whatever's spent, gets spent from inside this body. Not from yours. If your market's come to watch the trade wake, queue and watch. If it's come to hold the ledger — " and here the boy let the Red into the flat, one stroke, and the sand heard it, and Osk's face took it and did not dress it, " — the count has a row for that."

---

The last dark to the turn came down on the city like a lid.

The boy stood the rim in the small snow with the book under his arm and the wall behind his eye, and out past the lanterns the water lay black and ruled and still, and the market rode its nine fires at the far light, holding station behind the line's gray hull, and the frost road ran between them all, white, exact, and it did not go out. Marrow's lamp was steady. The counted breaths ran their line, one spent, one owed. And down on the flats, in the mud, the two hundred rows knelt facing the water as they had knelt since the book changed hands — and in the last dark's first stroke, the boy heard the finding that no slate would carry, that the body made before the head could hold it, low, in the room under the room:

The rows were breathing with the fires.

The long breath, two hundred kept bodies, and the nine fires down the water rose and fell on it — one breath, one body, the fleet's flames breathing in the stroke of the kept dead, as the warm water's fires had stood still for one breath the dark the maker served notice. The whole water was one account. The fires were entries in it. And the boy stood the rim with the flat voice running the last entry of the dark, alone, where no tier could hear it:

*Entered: one dark. The buyers are come, and the invoice is the page, and the hand that wrote the address is standing witness at my sand, and the carrier is queueing in my road, and the account is one body wide and two hands full, and both hands are mine, and at the turn the trade spends what the trade spends, and the body keeps.*

He carried the book down the stair. Behind him the fires breathed on the water, one breath with the dead, patient, exact, and the term came on.
