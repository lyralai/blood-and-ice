# Book Three, Chapter 15: The Soundings

The soundings came ashore with the gray, and the boy read them off Osk's hands before the man had spent a word, because the hands had told the truth about Osk before the mouth ever had, and the hands were wrong this gray.

They were steady. That was the wrong of it. A man who has spent his life handling other people's sealed law learns to carry anything level, and Osk came up the flats at his own pace with the sounding-leaf squared under his arm like a book of the houses', and the pace lied better than any tongue on the coast. The boy had watched those hands fold a burned coat, hold a wax seal they would not touch, take a ruling that gave eleven generations a floor. He had never watched them be steady about a thing that was spoiling.

"Run them at the spacing," the boy said. "With the leaf. Witnessed."

"I'd have run them at the spacing if they were good," Osk said. Ledger-voice, and under it the crack, held level like everything else the man carried. "They're not good, count. That's the news. First line of the road is already lies. The warm water crossed it in the dark, two points off the far mark, and no course written Tuesday holds on water that moved Wednesday. Second line is thinning. Third and fourth run true." He unrolled the leaf a hand's width, the fleet's marks down the left edge in keeper's chalk, and rolled it again, and the rolling was slower than the man. "Two darks. The bearings are good for two darks, and I'd not price the second one at full. The sea is eating the road, and it's eating it faster than the courses can be written, and I've stopped telling you the water's waking, because you entered that a season ago. I'm telling you the water's *here*."

The boy took the terms. The body took them first — a cold off the flats had come up under the shirt and set itself between the shoulder-blades, and the breath ran shorter by a line, and the boy let the body have its finding before the head dressed it, because the body had been right about the water before any sounding was run. The gray stood on the sand. The market was striking its stalls behind them, and the stalls were full, and the ground had learned to read a gray now as it had once learned to read the slate, which is to say as a thing with tomorrow in it, and two hundred faces were pricing the count's face off the rim while the count priced a spoiling road.

*Entered, kneeling-height: the coast has held rates before. It has held queues, terms, a posted price on names. It has never held a deadline. A rate keeps. A deadline spoils. We are about to teach two hundred spenders to watch a thing run out, and the thing that is running out is a road, and roads are the one asset this sand has never owned.*

The thumb went to the gap in the teeth. The gray came up off the water, ordinary, and ordinary was getting expensive.

---

They raised the spacing at mid-morning, because the ground had queued for it — actually queued, spenders and hull-keepers and the young ones with the waking blood, standing the three strides off with their arms folded, waiting to watch a leaf get read, and the boy stood his side of the spacing and understood that the reading had stopped being his the dark the audit ruled it witnessed. The coast believed him now. That was the cost of being believed: your readings belonged to the readers.

Maren stood at the slate with the chalk. Serl stood the rim-stone with her hands in her seams. Osk stood the far side with the sounding-leaf, and the leaf of the road lay on a board between them, the ruled page with its four written lines and its empty rule running down past them, and the crowd had gone quiet by rows, flats-quiet, hold-still.

"Line one," the boy said, flat, carrying, and nodded to Osk.

"Run at the first dark, twice," Osk said. "Current two points off the far mark against the leaf's bearing. Mark moved. The line is lies. A hull that sailed it would spend herself on the warming and never know why." He said it to the ground, not to the count, and the saying to the ground was the man's whole house talking, eleven generations of a law that covered marks now reading one aloud. "Line two. Run at the second bell. The current stands yet, but the warm is under it. I'd sail it this dark. I'd not promise it the next."

Maren's chalk moved. The chalk did not write *true* or *false*; Maren cut a third column with the side of the chalk, in front of the ground, the way the man had cut the column for the kept things one dark ago, and headed it with the fleet's own word: *spoiling.* Line one: spoiled. Line two: spoiling. Lines three and four: sound, this dark, entered without promise. The board and the slate stood side by side, and the ground read them together, the leaf's ruled emptiness beside the chalk's spoil-column, and the boy watched the ground do its arithmetic face to face and row to row, and the arithmetic came back the same everywhere: a road that had four true lines yesterday had three today, and the third was on the water's ledger already.

"Entered," the boy said. "The road out is an asset in spoil. The count does not rule on a spoiling asset in the dark. The ruling gets made at the next gray, here, witnessed, with the fleet's soundings fresh off the water and the slate's columns up to the hour. If the page is cut, it is cut in front of the ground or not cut at all. If it is not cut, the coast enters what not cutting costs, the same as it enters what cutting costs. Nobody sails blind and nobody rules blind. That's the whole ruling for today. Queue or stand."

The ground stood. And then, out of the rows, the young one came forward.

She came the way she had come to the slate the dark before, fists at her seams, at arm's length from her own knowing, and the boy saw the rows take her in and price her — the oil-fee girl, the lost word, entered at a posted rate — and the pricing followed her up to the board the way fog follows a lantern, and she stood at the leaf and did not look at the lines at all. She looked at the fold.

"Grandmother's fold had three creases," she said, loud enough, because entered meant said in front of everyone, and the ground had taught her that and she had spent the fee and learned the lesson whole. "A leaf that carries a road is not folded like a letter. Letters fold once, any way, because letters are read and thrown." Her hands came off her seams, and the hands did a small thing in the air above the ruled page, a thing the body had kept when the mouth had lost the word: three turns, small, exact, a folding that never touched the leaf. "A road folds thrice. Road to road, edge to edge, so the lines lie shut inside and the water can't read them. So it can be carried." She stood there with the shape of it in her hands, held out over the board like a thing she was still pricing. "The old tongue had a word for a leaf folded for carrying. I don't have the word. I have the fold. And that leaf —" the fists came back to the seams, and the aim of her went level, young and old-blooded both, "— that leaf is folded thrice. I saw it when the wax came off. It's worn along the creases. It has been folded and carried, and folded and carried, many times, by hands that knew the fold. It isn't a letter, count. It was never a letter. Letters are sent. This was brought."

The spacing held it. The boy felt the ground's quiet change weight under him, the way a hull changes weight when the cargo shifts, and the change was the finding going into two hundred keepers at once: *a letter waits for an answer. A chart waits for a weather.* The leaf on the board was not a page asking to be read. It was a tool asking to be used, carried to this sand in a pocket, over water, by hands — and the hands were the question the coast did not ask out loud, and the boy did not ask it either, because the asking was entered everywhere already, in ink no keeper could name, and some entries do not need a second writing.

"Entered," the boy said, and his voice ran flat while the entry under it ran otherwise. "The fold is a finding. Posted at the oil rate. The coast thanks the hand that kept it." He looked at the young one, face to face, and gave her the terms plain, as terms had been given on that sand all season. "The shape in your mouth is not lost. It's kept. There's a difference, and the coast just learned it stands between spoiling and sound. Next."

She went back up the rows with her spine straight and her fists at her seams, and the boy watched the ground part for her, and entered the parting too: *a coast that had priced her knowing at oil-fee the dark before made a lane for it this gray. Entered: that is what a posted rate does. It doesn't pay for the finding. It pays for the ground to learn the finding has a standing.*

Serl's eyes came off the crowd once, landed on the folded leaf, and went back. Counting. The boy had stopped pricing which was the mercy, some darks back. He priced only that she was counting the same thing he was: two darks. One sound.

---

Asha-kel came down to the rim at the last of the light, and the place was the message, as the place was always the message: not the ninth landing. The rim. Count's ground. The houses had sent nothing, which meant the houses had sent her, and she had walked the stair down past her own landing to stand where the ground stood, and the walking-past was hers.

"The houses' finding," she said, flat, worn round by every weather in the world. "They'll post it at the next gray if you don't rule first: the road should not be cut. Not this season. The count ashore is this coast's whole floor — the trade, the terms, the rates, the ground that queued its own grief one dark ago. Nine hulls can find no other floor anywhere in the world, and the houses have entered what that is worth, because the houses have watched you spend a season making yourself the ground's ledger, and a ledger that sails is a ledger that can be sunk." The flat never wavered. "That's the houses' finding, and it's honest, and you should hear the honesty before you hear the rest, which is that the houses have also entered what the count ashore is worth to the houses, and their books and your coast do not carry each other at the same rate. They're not wrong that the floor is safer with you on it. They're not right that safety is the ruling. They're a house. Houses price floors."

"And yours," the boy said. "The finding the houses don't post."

The fog breathed on the rim-stone. Below them the lantern line took the first watch, flame by flame, and out past the far light the sea ran black and money-moving, nine fires riding it, and Asha-kel stood the count's ground with her hands folded and spent the stopping before the words, the way she spent every stopping, as coin.

"Four darks gone I crossed three strides and put your hand where it would do work," she said. "I have not priced that since, and I won't price it now, because the pricing was done that dark and the account doesn't reopen. But you're ruling on a road, count, and a road is a thing that takes a body out of reach of the ground it's rigged to, and you'll do the ruling better with the terms whole than with the terms kept." One degree, no more, and the degree took the whole stair of her in it. "An anchor holds the boat to the ground while the weather decides what the boat is. That's the work. It was never the anchor's work to hold the boat *back*, and if you sail, the anchor does not fail — it pays out. That's all. It pays out to the end of its line and then it holds, and the holding is the same holding, at any length of line, or it was never holding. The houses will tell you a ledger that sails can be sunk. I'm telling you the other finding, and then I'm done, because a counsel that argues is a counsel that hasn't found its rate: something rode out to you on that water all season, in packets, in ink, and every hull you'd sail with is a hull that packet found first. If the count goes aboard, the count is not leaving the letters behind. The count is sailing to the address. Enter that before the next gray, or the gray will enter it for you."

She took the stair up, unhurried, and the boy stood the rim while the fog settled on him and the two findings walking in him, and the findings walked in opposite directions, and both of them had come off the same tongue, and the boy priced that, too, kneeling-height, where the body keeps its own book: *the houses' woman carried the houses' finding in the flat and her own under it, and I cannot split the cargo, and that may be her design, and it may be the design of a man who wants both findings aboard whatever he sails, and both readings stand.*

The Red said nothing. It lay at the border, warm, exact, patient as ruled paper, fourth dark running, and the boy had stopped pricing the silence as either spender or ledger. He priced it now as weather. Weather does not speak. Weather waits to be read, and the reading was the teaching, and he had taught it two hundred entries deep, one dark ago, in front of the whole ground.

---

He told Marrow at the lamp, at the mid-dark, because the runt's whole craft was standing at a shoulder while weather ran through a body, and there was weather.

He said it plain. The soundings. The spoiling column. The fold that was never a letter. The two darks and the one sound line. The houses' floor and the houses' aim, the anchor that pays out, the address the count would be sailing to. And the ruling he had not made, which was the one thing on the coast that dark that still had a tomorrow in it.

Marrow listened as Marrow counted, whole, and spent nothing early. The lamp kept its hook and the flame did not lean, and when the boy was done the runt was quiet for the length of three counted beats, and spent the name, which Marrow spent rarely.

"Rook. You told me once the thing inside you had stopped being a guest that wants and become a keeper that keeps. I've been counting that trade since. A guest that wants can be priced, because wanting names a lack, and a lack is a door. A keeper that keeps can't be priced, because keeping names a *have*, and a have is a floor." The lamp-light stood in the runt's eyes. "Now hear the count. Four darks it's kept quiet while you checked its work, and one dark ago you stopped checking alone — you read it out, witnessed, two hundred deep, and the coast entered the column. And the thing has said nothing since. Not a word, not a door, not a bid. Rook, in four darks you've had the use of it exactly never, and the silence of a thing that *wants* is famine — it gnaws, it shows. The silence of a thing that *has* is a hold. It doesn't gnaw. It doesn't show. It sits on what it's holding and it waits for the count to come to it, and you've been entering that patience as peace, as breath held, as ruled paper —" one breath, aimed, "— because those are the words you have. Here's one you've been dodging: *ready.* The keeper isn't holding still, Rook. The keeper is holding ready. Go check what it's ruled ready *for*."

One spent. One owed. The boy breathed them both, and went down.

---

The checking was done in the dark, in the room under the room, and the walls stood as they had stood — spent, spent, draft and weather — and the face behind them turned, dark eyes, gray, dark, kept, and the entries ran exact in the not-his hand, dark by dark, all of it carried, all of it true, and the boy went past all of it, past the heartbeat at the foot of the page, to the page that had been ruled blank.

It was not blank.

No. It was blank, and the blank was charted — which is to say ruled — and the ruling had grown. The single line the boy had found two darks gone, straight and clean and waiting — it had company now. Down the page, from the ruling hand that owed nothing to the boy's consent, strokes. Column strokes. Short, vertical, exact, cut at even spacings the full height of the page: a column cut for entries, and beside it another, and between them a margin ruled narrow, and the boy lay in his own dark with his thumb off the gap for the first time in a season — the thumb flat on the page, measuring, because the body had a finding and the finding was arithmetic.

He counted the strokes. Margin to margin, as he had counted the fleet's marks at the spacing, and the body's count came up, and the boy ran it twice, and it came up the same.

The ruled strokes numbered the soundings.

Not the soundings as the fleet had run them, three lines sound at the last bell. The strokes ran one fewer than that. The keeper had cut its column to the road as the keeper read the road — and the keeper read the water faster than the fleet, or the keeper read the fleet honestly and the water faster, and either finding was the same finding: the column already ruled inside him was one line shorter than the column chalked on the slate, and somewhere between the slate and the boy's own dark, the second line had gone to lies, and nobody had run it yet, and the boy knew it anyway, kneeling-height, past all dressing, the knowledge riding under him like a current under a keel.

And at the foot of the columns, ruled straight and clean across both, waiting for a spending not yet spent: the closing line. The total. A rule no counter cuts until the account is meant to close.

The boy lay in his own dark with the lamp out and the ground's heartbeat in reach down the flats and one sound line left on a road folded for carrying, and he made the entry himself, in the one stroke that was his, and the book took it, as it took everything, ruling or no:

*Entered: the keeper has ruled the account ready. Entered: the keeper reads the water one sounding ahead of the fleet, and I do not know which of us that makes the better pilot, and the next gray will not either. Entered: the ruling gets made at the spacing, witnessed, with the ground — and the book has already cut its total line, and the book does not rule, and the book does not ask, and the book has never once, all season, been wrong.*

Out past the far light the black water was eating the road one line ahead of the chalk, and somewhere on it, in ink that was family to his own, a hand that folded charts for carrying had folded one for him. The gray would come up on the deciding hour. The columns waited under his thumb, ruled to the last sound line, and the closing line waited under them, and the boy slept toward the gray the way a chart sleeps in a pocket: folded, carried, shut along the creases, road to road, edge to edge, against the water getting in.
