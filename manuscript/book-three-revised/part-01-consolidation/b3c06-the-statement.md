# Book Three, Chapter 6: The Statement

Three darks, and the stone said nothing.

That was the first cost, and the boy had not priced it ahead of time. He had cut the line, closed the column, footed it in his own hand's length, and some part of the body — the old part, the part that had waited out storms in the throat of a crack — had believed the answering would come at once, the way a struck wall answers at once. Stone did not work that way. Nine hundred years did not work that way. The white had read his three unfinished lines across a hundred darks of patience, and now it could afford to let a closed line lie, because a closed line was a kept thing, and the white was the oldest keeper there was.

So the boy waited, and the waiting had a shape, and the shape was winter.

The cold came down off the water in the fourth dark and stayed. Not the storm-cold the city knew — the long cold, the gray one, the cold that did not spend itself in a night but banked, dark after dark, like a fire built by a careful hand. The tiers felt it first in the mortar joints, where the wind found the old seams, and then in the shelf-line, where the clay-pots went further down the row before they ran dry. The count had cut the rates in clay and the rates held: small ones first, old ones second, the tiers after, the Cordon's sixteen hulls entered last and fed last, on the count's law, witnessed. The law held. The boy counted what the holding cost, because the holding cost more each dark — the line at the shelf stood longer, and the faces in it read further down into themselves, and the third tier had begun to say *oarsmen* in a new voice, flat, closed, a word worn like a stone carried in a cheek.

On the fifth dark the boy took a pot and stood at the line's end.

Serl was at the shelf with her slates. She saw him come and did not see him — that was the craft of it, the row-voice craft, the same not-seeing she had spent on worse things — and the boy stood the line's end behind the last of the Cordon's oarsmen, behind the tiers, behind the old ones, behind the small ones, and ate when the line's eating came to him, which was last, and the pot was lighter than the pots at the line's head, because the shelf ran down as the line ran down. He ate what was in it. The tiers watched him eat it. Nothing was said, and the nothing went up the stone tier by tier, and the boy priced the entry honestly, with no dressing on it: a third of a clay-pot of thin gruel, spent once each dark, bought a thing that had no column yet but needed one. He opened the column that dark in the tally-room clay. *Spendings, standing.* First entry: one pot, taken last, in the open, entered.

*Watch which reading I act from,* he entered under it, old entry, entered again. *The beast reads the line's end as the low place. The count reads it as the footing. Both true. Feed the footing.*

---

The statement came at the sixth dark's turning, and it came through Harn.

The boy was at the tally-house by then most turnings, because the tally-house was where the city's two times ran — the clay's fast time above, the stone's slow time below — and because Serl's stool sat at the foot of a pallet he could not stop pricing. Harn lay as Harn lay. Chest rising, falling, the breath riding the stair's line a breath behind the boy's own. Serl had the watch. The lamp was at its height.

The kept man's eyes opened.

Not the slow surfacing of a sleeper. One breath the lids were down and the next they were not, and the eyes under them were not empty and were not Harn's — they were *placed*, the boy's body knew it before the count could name it, placed as stones are placed, and Serl came off the stool with her back to the wall and her hand finding the doorframe behind her without looking, and the room went cold as rooms go cold when a draft finds a seam, except the seam was under the world.

Harn's chest rose. And with it the boy's own chest rose — not after. With.

"The account stands," Harn said.

The voice was Harn's voice, waterman's voice, the vowels the crossing had salted — and under it, through it, the voice ran level, ruled from far under. It did not hurry. It had nowhere to be. Nine hundred years had nowhere to be.

"Deposit received. One drop, entered at the footing. Currency: the old currency. Accounts in the old currency render their statements in kind." A breath, and the breath was the room's, and the boy's, and the custody's far under, all one length for the length of the speaking. "Statement follows. Balance carried: one line, closed, footed, ruled. Held. Collection is not yet rendered. Collection renders at the winter turn, in the currency of the account, at the address of the footing. Notice is given. Notice is the courtesy of the law."

The eyes moved. That was the worst of it, the finding the boy carried afterward in the shelf of things that did not file: the eyes moved, and they found the boy kneeling at the bed's edge, and they *read* him — one pass, patient, at reading distance, the wear's own patience in a waterman's salted face — and then they closed, and Harn's chest fell, and the boy's chest fell a half-breath behind its own time, wrong-footed, and the kept man breathed on at the old rate, a breath behind, and did not wake, and never woke, and would say, when the gray light brought the freed to sit him up and spoon the thin gruel, that he had slept the dark through and dreamed of nothing.

Serl stood at the wall with her hand on the doorframe. Her face had done the thin thing and undone it and done it again, and when she could speak she spoke in the row-voice, flat, for the record, though no record in the world had a column for it.

"That wasn't the man."

"No."

"That was the floor." Her knuckles were white on the frame. "The floor spoke through a rower I set a watch on, carrier. I sat his watch. I sat it dark by dark, and the floor sat it with me, and it waited for you to be in the room. It *addressed* you." A stroke's silence. "You wrote to it in blood. It's writing back in Harn."

He knelt at the bed and made the entries plain, because plain was the only mercy and the mercy was not for him.

*Findings. The statement has been rendered. Terms: collection at the winter turn, in blood, at the address of the footing. The address of the footing is the fourth turning. Entered plain: the address is also me. The white does not distinguish between a line's ground and its author. To the law under the floor, the maker is the entry's place. It is not going to come to the turning to collect from the stone.*

*And entered, cost: it spoke through a kept man and read me while it spoke, and the reading was the same reading as the wear — kneeling height, reading distance — and I knelt there and let it, because the man it spoke through is a man the count has entered as kept, as owed, and the owing is mine. I could not leave the bed. It knew that. It chose its page for that.*

---

The stonemason's name was Vetch, and his trouble came to a head the same dark, and the boy took it as the dark took everything — in the order it arrived, at the price it asked.

The shelf-women caught it. Two clay-pots of the Cordon's own grain, buried under the third tier's stair, wrapped in sacking, cached — the boy stood over the little buried hoard and felt the beast come forward under his ribs before the count had entered the shape, because the beast knew a cache when it smelled one, and the count knew it too, and the two knowings sat badly together, one in each hand. Vetch stood in the tally-room between two of Serl's gallery-women with his winter-knuckled hands open at his sides, and his face said *do it* before anyone had said anything, and that was the wrong reading, and the boy made himself read again.

Vetch had two small ones, third tier, rib-showing, fed before him by the count's law and fed not enough by the winter's arithmetic. The cached grain was not for him. That came out flat, unadorned, in the voice of a man pricing his own entry and paying in pride because pride was the only coin he had left: the oarsmen ate last and ate enough, the tiers had counted, and a mason who had laid the city's winter seams for thirty years could not out-wait a hull that had rowed in a fortnight past. He had not taken it from the small ones' mouths. He had taken it from the *order* of the mouths, and he knew the difference, and he stood there and made the difference stand with him.

The tiers would have taken his hand for it two moons past. Vetch knew that too; the knowledge was in his open hands, held out flat, already a stump in the wanting. The boy sat the tally-stone and let the room wait while the count did its work, and the work was slow because it mattered: law that bent for a likeable man was not law, and law that took a mason's hand for two pots of grain in the first winter of the count was law the tiers would obey once and hate forever, and hate is a debt that compounds.

"Entered," the boy said, and the flat voice carried to the back of the room, where the third tier had crowded in to see a hand come off. "Vetch, mason, third tier. Took from the count's order: two pots. The order is the count's. The taking is entered." He let it stand until the room heard the *entered* and not the hanging. "The price: the two pots, repaid at the winter's slack, entered as debt, not as blood. And the seams — the tiers' winter seams, all of them, yours to walk and yours to mend, dark by dark, the count's rate, until the debt stands paid. The city's cold comes through the joints, and the city holds a mason who owes it, and the count collects what it is owed in the coin that does the most good. That is the law's shape. It is not mercy. It is arithmetic."

Vetch's face did a thing the boy had only seen the shelf do — went from closed to open in one motion, all at once, like a pot unwrapped. He did not say thank you. He said, "The east joints on the second tier go first. I've been telling them for two moons." And that was the other face of the man, the finding beneath the finding: the man had been mending the city's seams for nothing, in the dark of the winter, on his own watch, for two moons, and the city had not entered it anywhere, and the not-entering was half of what had made the cache.

*Entered,* the boy wrote, when the room had emptied. *The count's law held a hand today and spent a debt instead. Watch it: mercy priced as arithmetic is still mercy, and the tiers will read it as mercy, and some of them will read it as weakness, and the reading is the true price. Entered plain: the count did not do it for the mason's small ones alone. The count did it because the white collects at the winter turn, and the city the white collects into must be a city that holds together when the collection comes. Every entry between now and the turn is against that dark. Every one.*

---

He went down to the fourth turning after, because the dark had one more finding in it, and the body knew which turning held it before the lamp was lit.

The counter-entry lay beside his three lines.

He knelt at the wear — his knees went to the white's stone without asking him, the body taking the reading-place as its own now, another small annexation the boy entered at cost — and the lamp's circle found the fourth line, cut shallow, promissory-shallow, matched to the first line's depth, lying beside the closed column like a boat lying beside a moored fleet.

And it was cut short. One stroke's width short.

The boy knelt there a long time with the lamp, and the cold came up through his knees, and he read the line for what it was and could not make it be anything else: the white had answered his finished line with a new line, an open line, a line begun and not closed — and it had cut it in *his* length. Not the white's ruled whole. The maker's missing length, the honest difference, the one stroke short that the boy had left standing in his own closure as the mark of his own hand. The white had studied him at the wear, dark after dark, kneeling height, reading distance — and what it had read was not the book. The book it could read from anywhere. It had come up to the wear to read the *hand*. And now it had written back in the hand it read.

The beast came forward at the finding, and the warmth that rose was not the keeping-warmth and not the hoard-satisfaction. It was older than both, flat and heavy, the heat of a marked border: another body had cut in this body's ground. Under his ribs the beast read the counter-entry as a rival's mark laid over a cache, and it wanted one thing, simple, total, older than the count by ten thousand years — it wanted to go down to that line and cut *through* it, mark over mark, and hold.

The boy held. That was the whole of the dark's work, in the end: the boy on his knees in the white's cold, the beast leaning its whole old weight at the door, and the count between them doing what the count did, entering the finding plain while the body shook.

*Entered: the white has answered a closed line with an open one. It has opened a column in my book, in my hand, on my ground. It is not collecting. It is corresponding. Entered plain, and entered whole: this is how the deep courts. It does not take a maker's book. It learns the maker's hand, and writes in it, and waits to see if the maker writes back in the hand he shares with it now. Two books that keep in blood, writing in one hand. That is not a bailiff's notice anymore.*

*The line is open. It can still be anybody's. It can still be mine.*

---

"It is not asking you to answer," the Red said, when the boy came to the door with the cold still in his knees and the shake still in the hands. The ledger-voice ran slow, the reading-as-it-spoke register, the truest one. *It is asking you to agree there is no difference between answering and not answering. That is the deep's whole trade, boy. It wrote in your hand so that whatever you cut next is cut in a hand it also holds. Silence is entry. Refusal is entry. The only move that is not entry is—"*

"Closing the book."

*There is no closing the book,* the Red said, and the warmth lay level, and under the level ran a faint second warmth, and the boy counted both and did not like the total. *You footed the column. The column is carried to you. You paid a drop into the floor and the floor opened an account and the account has a term: the winter turn, in blood, at the address of the footing. Entered plain: the address breathes, boy. I have been trying to price it since the kept man spoke, and I will tell you what I can price and what I cannot. I can price the term. Blood in the old currency is the one tongue both books speak, and the white has been rendering it two hundred and one at a time since before your walls. What I cannot price is the amount. The tithe was a rate. A collection on a footed column is not a rate. It is a balance, and the balance is whatever the law under the floor reads the account to be worth.* A page turned along the grain. *And you know what an account is worth to a law that old, boy. It is worth what it holds. What does the account hold?*

He sat with it. The cold came up and the ache came down, and between the two holdings he made himself say it in the flat voice, because saying it plain was the only footing he had.

"Me."

*Entered,* the Red said, and there was no warmth in it at all now, and the no-warmth was its own finding. *Then the question is not whether the white collects. The question is what it collects with. It has no feet. It has no hands but borrowed ones — a kept man's breath, a graver's arm that was not your graver's arm when it ruled your third line whole. It cuts, boy. It has always cut. If it comes to the turn to collect an account whose balance is a maker, then the collection is a cutting, and the address is the maker's margin, and the margin—* the voice went slower still, *—the margin is already marked. One drop fell in the fourth turning. Ask yourself which of the two of us it fell from, and then ask yourself why the statement called it the old currency, and then stop asking, because I have priced this from the inside for six darks, and the answer is the one thing in this city I will not say aloud in the dark.*

---

The rim held its gray light, and Marrow was at the shoulder, and the runt did not let the boy speak first. Marrow had been counting since before the boy came up the stair.

"Sixty and nine," Marrow said. Flat. Steady. Aimed.

He counted it himself, hull by hull, along the dark rows past the far light. Sixty and nine. The middle-rows hull — the one that had burned its lamp the dark after the gate was cut, the one a man stands in who wants to be seen to be part of a thing — stood inside the far light now. Alone. One hull, advanced across a line of open water in the dark of a statement and a counter-entry, without a light, without a sound the wall or the water or the gate-stone had entered anywhere. The stacking answered entries. The finished line had been an entry, and the white's counter-entry had been an entry, and the stacking's answer was a hull.

"It's not a threat," Marrow said. "If it were a threat it would have come with more than one." The counted breaths ran their line, one spent, one owed. "One hull is a *footing.* First foot on new ground. Rook." The shoulder was up, the old debt, one degree off true. "Sellers hail before they land. That thing came across the far light in the dark without hailing, and the not-hailing is the entry. It didn't come to talk. It came to stand. It's collecting ground."

Out on the flat water the hull lay dark against the dark, close enough now that a man who looked long enough could count the seams of her winter-cased sail, and the boy stood the watch-length and let the cold do its patient arithmetic on him, and under the stone, in the deep, the custody held its long breath around one small new account, and the boy held his around one small old one.

A closed line gets collected. The white had said when. The stacking had said where. The only word left unentered on the page was the oldest one, the one under the whole count, the one the Red would not say in the dark:

What.

*Entered,* the boy said, inside, where the flat voice kept its truest ledger. *The count answers at the winter turn. Not before. And the count answers in its own hand.*

He took the lamp down the stair, and behind him the one hull stood its ground inside the far light, patient as the law under the floor, and the sea lay flat and colorless as a ruled page with one mark on it.
