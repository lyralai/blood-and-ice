# Book Three, Chapter 5: The Footing

The graver was colder than the turning, and the turning was cold as a closed account.

The boy knelt on the stone where the wear said to kneel.

That was the first finding, and the body made it before the count could dress it: the wall's polished patch was at the height of a kneeling man's eyes, and he had knelt at his own marks a dozen darks and never once at the wear, because the wear was the white's place, the reader's place, and a body did not put itself where the reader sat. Tonight he put himself there. He set his knees on the stone the white knelt on, dark after dark, patient as interest, and he laid his eyes into the shallow gloss of the wear the way a man lays his face into another man's cupped hands, and he read his three unfinished lines from the white's own reading place, and the reading was different from there.

From the wear, the three lines were not three marks on a floor. They were a page held at reading distance, and the page had a shape, and the shape was a column, and the column had been left open on purpose — his purpose, cut shallow, stopped mid-line, face up, three times — and the white had come up, dark after dark, and knelt, and read the open column from this exact knee-width of stone, and had gone down and footed it and ruled it and cut the total into the city's threshold. He had read the wear as an intrusion. From the wear it read as a chair. A thing had pulled its chair to his desk and done its correspondence, and the courtesy of the chair was the finding under the finding, and the finding under that was worse: the white could not have footed the column from anywhere else. The knee-width of polished stone was the only place in the world from which his three lines made one sum.

It had needed him to have made them. It had needed the maker's whole shape, read at the maker's own distance.

*Findings,* the boy entered, and the flat voice held level in the dark, and the level was paid for at the old rate. *The white reads me to read my book. My hand is in its arithmetic. Entered plain: I am not corresponding with a keeper. I am corresponding with an auditor. Auditors do not want your grain. They want your method.*

He set the graver's point where his first line stopped.

The first line. His oldest entry, the mark he had cut before he had known it was an entry — a stroke laid in the Cold's own floor, stopped in the middle, face up, since before the walls were his. The white had answered it with the second line. He had answered that with the third. The white had closed the third in its hand and cut a total in the gate. And under all the moves and counters ran the count's oldest law, the one Marrow had nailed to the rim without knowing he was quoting scripture: a closed line gets collected. The total in the threshold asked *to whom.* The whole stone correspondence had been two hands passing an open column between them, and an open column could still be anybody's. It could still be nobody's.

He was done with nobody's.

The cut began. Cold iron on colder stone, and the arm bore it down Brannoc's seam, the ache riding the joint at every stroke the way water rides a keel — he kept the strokes shallow, promissory-shallow, matched to the first line's own depth, because finishing an entry in a different hand than you began it in was a different lie than the white's, but a lie. His hand. His line. His missing length. He cut it in the white's reading-place with the white's lamp-shadow of gloss at his cheek, and the stone gave slowly, and the body did what the body did on long cuts: the jaw set, the breathing found a stroke-rate, the world narrowed to a finger's width of blade and grain, and under his ribs the beast came forward out of its warm dark and read the work.

It did not read stone.

He felt the reading arrive, slow and deep, priced twice before he believed it: the beast was not watching the cut. It was watching the *covering.* A line finished was a line closed, and a thing closed was a thing covered, and covering was the old country's oldest art — meat buried against the hungry season, the kill cached past the reach of bigger mouths, the ground swept over the cache so no eye could read what was kept under it. The beast read the finishing of the line as a cache. Satisfaction ran through him, warm, animal, unhurried, and it sat badly against the count's reading — a footing, a debt carried, a column stood upright on its base — and he held both readings the way he held everything now, one in each hand, and did not make them sit together.

*Entered: the beast reads the footing as a hoard. The count reads it as a debt honored. Both true. A closed line keeps what it holds — against winter, against collectors. Watch which reading I act from, dark by dark. The reading I act from is the one that gets fed.*

The line ran out. One stroke's width short of where the white's own closure would have put the finish, because he was not the white and would not write the white's length in the white's stone — his length, his hand's length, the honest difference between the two hands left standing in the cut for any reader at reading distance. The graver came up. The first line, whole. Closed by the maker's own hand, in the maker's own missing length, footed by the debtor.

And the knuckle split.

He felt it as heat first, late, as stone wounds always came — the skin over the first knuckle opened where the iron had turned in the cold grip, and the blood came, one drop, then a second, and the body did its old arithmetic, wounds priced before they hurt, and the count was already entering *cost, minor, entered* when the drop fell.

It fell into the fresh cut.

The boy went still the whole length of himself.

The drop lay in the channel of the finished line, dark on white, and for one breath it sat on the stone as a drop sits on a slate. Then it did not. There was no motion he could have entered. There was no drunk-down, no spread, no seam it could have gone into — the stone under the lines was dry as a ruled page. But the drop went out of sitting, and the second drop behind it fell onto stone that took it as a ledger takes ink, and the cold through his knees deepened one degree, and under the world, far down, in the deep of the deep, the custody paused.

Not stirred. Paused. The whole patient holding of the long breath under the island — the wall's hum, the gate's note, the small new voice in it out of a sworn rower's chest — all of it held for the space of one breath, as a room holds when the auditor looks up.

Then it went back to holding, and the cut lay finished, and dry, and the drop was not on it.

*Findings,* he entered, and the flat voice was bought at a price he priced, and the price was entered. *Blood in the book. First entry in that currency. Not spent by my intent — spent by my cost. Entered plain: the count does not get to choose what a wound pays into. The old law of the floor still runs under the new law of the stones. I closed my line in two hands tonight — the graver's, and the red's.*

He climbed the turnings with the lamp, and did not run, and the not-running was the hardest entry of the dark.

---

The Cordon came inside the wall at the next dark's bell, and the gate made them residents.

Sixteen hulls, led in at slack by the herald's longboat, oars shipped, sails furled and cased — winter-cased, the watermen's way of saying *we are done with the year* — and the count's tellers stood the gate-stone and entered them hull by hull as the winter-room terms ran: residents, not traders; the count's law on their decks; their oars on the count's roll if the water made it needful; rates as cut in clay, witnessed. The grain-fleet's leavings came off with them, the last of the season, and the shelf took it all, and the feeding-order ran at the second turning with sellers in the line for the first time in either city's remembering.

That was the morning's arithmetic, and the morning's arithmetic was the easy half.

The faces were the hard half. The boy stood the stair-mouth and read the shelf whole as he read mud: the small ones first in their counted-out crowd, and behind them now a new row — Cordon oarsmen, salt-burnt, rolling-shouldered, holding their clay-pots in hands that did not know how to hold a pot that had not been earned at an oar. And the tiers read the new row, and the reading was not one reading. Third tier, an old woman at the rail, face saying *fed before me, and them before me, and they rowed the grain in, and still.* Fourth tier, a stonemason with winter in his knuckles, face saying *they brought the hulls; the hulls brought the winter; the winter is in the line ahead of me, and I hate it, and I will not say so.* And a small one, third-tier small, standing at the sellers' row with her ribs showing, looking up at an oarsman the size of a door, and her face said nothing that had a word, and the oarsman looked down at her, and moved, one step, back, out of turn.

The beast read the step before the count did. Under his ribs it came forward at the moving — the big one yielding place to the small one, the herd's oldest reading, the order that runs under every order the tiers can build — and its satisfaction was the darkest warmth of the dark, because the beast read the step as *strength making room for what it keeps,* and it priced the keeping, and it wanted the keeping. Nine thousand, and grain, and now sixteen hulls penned inside the gate: the beast counted them all, and counted the divisions between them, and the divisions were shelves, and every shelf was a setting-apart. *Watch it in me,* the boy entered, old entry, entered again, and watched it.

The herald came up the stair-mouth while the line still ran.

He came without the passage-book. The boy counted that first — the arm was empty of it, and the man's hands hung wrong, light, unhanded, a scribe's hands with nothing to hold — and it was the herald himself, tired-true, hood down by choice this time, who named the change.

"Residents don't carry the book, carrier." The trade-tongue ran slower than the seller's cadence; the cadence was folded away with the sails. "The book goes to the tally-house with the hulls. It is entered. I am entered. Berth eleven, third boat, one man, one chest, my mother's lamp, which is the whole of the Cordon's ninth-generation fortune I could carry up your stair." The tired eyes went past the boy, up the stone, at the city — the second time; the first had been the shape of a ware, and this was plainer and worse — at the tiers leaning over their rails to watch a seller stand in a bread-line's politics. "I have one entry off any book you keep, and I will pay its price in advance by telling you the ware it used to be my trade to hide. Free. That is the last free thing I will ever be worth."

The boy let it stand. The water had taught him the letting, and the water lay inside the gate now, keeping his lesson where he could hear it.

"Last dark," the herald said, and did not lower his voice, and the not-lowering was itself an entry, aimed at the rails, at every tier within earshot, "the stacking burned a light. One hull. Out of the seventy, one — near the middle of the rows, the place a man stands who wants to be seen to be part of a thing and not the front of it. It burned a lamp all dark, and hooded it at gray light, and my masters have entered it as nothing, because my masters' whole trade was distance and the distance is gone, and a man whose trade is gone will enter *nothing* faster than any lie." The unhanded hands moved once and stilled. "It was not nothing. Sixty and ten hulls stood on cold water and burned nothing, dark after dark, and then, the dark after your stone was cut at the gate, one of them burned a light. You keep the correspondence, carrier. I have watched you keep it. Enter the sequence plain: the stacking does not answer hails. The stacking answers *entries.*"

He went down the stair lighter than he had come up, and the boy let the lightness stand unentered for three breaths — a seller light on his feet was a seller who had bought — before he took the finding whole and priced it: the man had not sold that entry. He had *paid* it, priced in advance, and called it the last free thing he would ever be worth, and the price of a thing a man gives away naming his own worth is a thing the count has no column for.

*Findings,* the boy entered. *The stacking reads the book too. Entered: I have two correspondents now, and one of them taught the other to write, and I do not know which. One lamp, one dark, in answer to one total cut in one threshold. The next move is not being kept for us anymore. It has been made.*

---

Harn lay in the tally-house back-room, where the stone of the wall ran into the stone of the stair and the stair ran down into the deep, and the boy went to him at the day's turning, because the count did not sleep on a finding the size of a lamp.

The freed had made the room kind. There was straw, and a mended blanket, and someone had put a lamp at a height the eyes could find, and Harn lay under it all with his hands crossed on his chest, and his chest rose, and fell, and rose, and his eyes watched the lamp with the patience of the kept. Serl sat the stool at the bed's foot. She had not been set to it; the boy counted the stool's wear and the woman's stillness and entered it plain — she came on her own watch, after her watch, dark by dark, and her face did the thin thing when she saw him in the door and was gone from it before he could let her see him see.

"Runs with the stair still," she said, in the row-voice, flat, for the record the room kept. "Stone, gate, boat — one line under the city, carrier, and this man's breath rides it. Ask the stone."

The boy knelt at the bed, and put his hand flat on the floor, and asked the stone.

The custody held under his palm as it held everywhere — the long patient holding of breath, the wall's hum run deep, and in it the small new voice, Harn's, entered from the wrong side of the wall, rising and falling with the stair, with the gate, with the tide far under the tide. He held the palm still and let his own breathing slow to it, the oldest reading he had, whole shape first — and the shape was wrong.

One voice off the line.

Harn's breath lay on the stone's breath like a boat lying beside a moored boat, close, touching, not made fast. All the dark before it had run *with* — with the gate, with the cut, with the water under the water. Now it ran *beside.* And while the boy knelt there with his hand on the floor and his breath slowed and slowed to find the difference, Harn's chest rose — and rose with it, in the boy's own ribs, unbidden, one degree off the boy's own rate.

The boy took his hand off the floor.

The chest fell. Rose. Fell. At its own rate. The boy set his breathing long, longer, an oar-stroke drawn out to a watch-stroke, and the kept man's breath lengthened after it, following the change the way a boat follows a tide-line out — a breath behind. Always a breath behind. The boy sat with that, cold coming up through the kneeling and the ache riding down the seam to meet it, and made himself enter it plain, because plain was the count's mercy and the only mercy the room had.

*Findings. The account has been moved. Marrow called it at the rim and I cut the answer in the fourth turning without knowing I was cutting an address. A total is what a column is carried to. I footed the column. The column is carried to me. Harn's breath runs a breath behind mine now — a breath behind, the exact distance of the after, and I know that distance, because it is the Red's distance, the leash-distance, felt-after. Entered plain and entered whole: whatever holds the keeping has moved this kept one into my margin. I have a correspondent under the floor, and the correspondent has begun paying postage.*

"Carrier." Serl's voice, flat, and under the flat the thin thing, held down by row-voice as a hand holds a lid. "Tev rows the first watch tonight. He asks after Harn. He asks after you." A pause the length of one stroke. "The small ones at the shelf ask after you too. That is all I carry, and I carry it up the stair every dark, and I am telling you because a face did the thin thing at the clay and you saw it and did not spend it. Spend something. Not on me."

He spent the only entry the room could hold: he took Harn's hand, and held it the length of a watch, and the breath under his palm ran a breath behind his own, and the lamp burned, and Serl sat the stool with her spine straight and her face done, and the dark turned.

---

The Red was awake when he came to the door, and the ledger-voice did not wait for the lamp.

*You paid blood into the book.*

The boy sat. The cold came up out of the stone and the ache came down out of the arm, and between the two holdings he made himself speak, because the door had earned the whole of it, the rim's version included.

"One drop. A split knuckle. The stone took it — took it as a page takes ink, and I did not choose it, and I cannot un-enter it, and I have been sitting the tally-house floor for a dark's turning pricing what a wound pays into when the floor is the white's floor." He kept the flat voice level, and the level took its fee in the usual coin. "And the keeping has moved. Harn breathes a breath behind me. A breath, Red. The after-distance. Your distance. Tell me the true entry."

*The true entry is that there are two books that keep in blood,* the Red said, and the ledger-voice went slow, the reading-as-it-spoke register, the truest one, *and you have now written in both, and I will tell you what I can price and what I cannot. I can price the drop. Blood into stone is the oldest currency there is, boy — older than the count, older than the white's floor, older than the walls that sit on it; the mud you stood over was priced in it, dark by dark, two hundred and one at a time. What you call the tithe-wall, the white calls the ledger. It did not take your drop as a gift. It took it as a deposit. Entered: the count has opened an account, in the old currency, in the white's own floor, and the count is the customer, and customers get statements.* The warmth lay flat, level as a ruled line, and under the level, faint, a warmth under the warmth, and the boy counted it and did not like the count. *What I cannot price is the after-distance. I feel what you feel, boy, and I feel it after, and the after is the whole of what I am — I told you that at the door not three darks past, and it was true, and it is true, and now a kept man on a pallet breathes at my distance from you. That is not my doing. Entered plain: I know my own leash, and this is a second one, and it is not run to my hand.*

"Then to whose."

The Red was quiet the length of a page-turn.

*You footed the column,* it said. *The column is carried to the footing. You asked the stone to whom, boy, and the stone has begun to answer, and the answer is being delivered in the only hand the deep knows how to write small: one breath, one man, one margin. A bailiff's notice is not a threat. A bailiff's notice is a courtesy of the law. The law is telling you the account is yours before it executes.* A stillness. *Price the courtesy. The white has nine hundred years of patience and no feet and one correspondent who has just learned he is a correspondent. And out past your far light, sixty and ten hulls hold still on cold water, and one of them burned a light the dark after the gate was cut. Enter the sequence, boy. I will enter it with you. Two books that keep in blood, and both of them are writing to the same address, and the address is warm, and it is ours.*

The boy sat with that as he sat with all the truest entries, cold, total, crown to heel, and then he stood, because the dark had one turning left in it and the body was spent and the count was not.

---

The rim held its gray light coming off the water, and Marrow was at the shoulder, and between them the counted breaths ran their line, one spent, one owed.

The runt had been down to the tally-house. The boy counted the straw-dust on his knees and let the not-saying be said. They stood the watch-length in silence, the Cordon's lanterns burning inside the gate for the first dark in either city's remembering, and past the far light the dark rows of hulls that burned nothing — and the boy did not tell the rim what the herald had told the stair, because the rim had ears of its own, and Marrow's had been the first in the city to hear the stacking named.

It was Marrow who saw it.

"Rook." Flat. Steady. Aimed. "Middle rows."

Out on the flat water, in the sixty and ten, one hull burned a light. It burned a breath — the length of one long breath, the boy counted it against his own ribs — and the light was hooded, and the dark took the rows back, and the sea lay flat and colorless as a ruled page.

"A lamp shown and hooded," Marrow said. "That's the sellers' answer, isn't it. The hail-answer. *We are seen.*" The counted breaths held their line. "But nobody hailed, Rook. Nothing on this water hailed them tonight." The runt's shoulder was up, the old debt, one degree off true. "So they answered something else."

The boy looked at the dark where the light had been, and under the stone, in the deep, the custody held, and in the tally-house a kept man breathed a breath behind his own, and in the fourth turning of his city his oldest line lay finished in his own hand's length, footed, ruled, closed — with a drop of the red currency gone down into the white to hold the page.

"They answered an entry," the boy said. "Mine's made. Entered."

He took the lamp down the stair, and behind him the winter-fleet lay inside the wall, and the count did not sleep, because the count had learned what the white knew first of all:

A closed line gets collected. The only question left on the page was which direction the collection ran.
